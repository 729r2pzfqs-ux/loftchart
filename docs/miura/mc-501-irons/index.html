About the Miura MC-501

The MC-501 launched Miura's Muscle Cavity line, announced on 6 February 2018 with retail orders opening 20 February 2018 at 260 US dollars per head. Billed by Miura as "the modern blade", it paired a blade-like profile with a T-shaped muscle pad low in the back, about 20 grams of mass repositioned toward a sole wider than any previous Miura blade, and Yoshitaka Miura's Y-grind sole — on the longest heel-to-toe head the company had forged to that point. Note that Miura's published lie angles for this model are markedly flatter than its later irons: 61 degrees on the 7-iron against the 62.5 degrees of the MC-502 that replaced it (announced 15 November 2022). Heads came in copper-nickel satin and Black QPQ finishes, 3-iron through pitching wedge. Miura sells heads through authorized dealers and fitters and builds sets to order, so there is no stock shaft; specs below are Miura's published standard.

Frequently asked questions

What is the loft of a Miura MC-501 7-iron?

The Miura MC-501 7-iron has a loft of 34 degrees, a lie of 61 degrees and a length of 37.0 inches, per Miura's published spec chart.

What does MC stand for in Miura MC-501?

Muscle Cavity. The MC-501 pairs a blade-like profile with a T-shaped muscle pad and about 20 grams of mass moved low into a wider sole, giving slightly more forgiveness than Miura's pure muscle-backs.

Is the Miura MC-501 discontinued?

Yes. Miura announced its successor, the MC-502, on 15 November 2022, and the MC-501 has left Miura's current line.

Why are the Miura MC-501's lie angles so flat?

Miura's published spec for the MC-501 runs from 59 degrees on the 3-iron to 62.5 on the pitching wedge — about 1.5 degrees flatter through the set than the MC-502 that replaced it. Since Miura heads are sold through fitters who bend lie to order, the published figure is only the starting point.

Finding a used set

The Miura MC-501 ran from 2018 to 2022 and is no longer in production, so the used market is the only source. Search eBay, 2nd Swing, Golf Avenue and the PGA Tour Superstore trade-in listings, and check the loft and lie against the chart above before buying — sets this age have often been bent from standard.
