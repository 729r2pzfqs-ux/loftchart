About the Miura TC-201

The TC-201 ("Tour Cavity") was announced in early 2020 as Miura's shallow cavity-back for better players, filling the slot left by the discontinued CB-1008 and sitting between the MB-101 muscle-back and the larger CB-301. A notched cavity moves weight to heel and toe, and a progressive weight pad (thicker in the short irons, thinner in the long irons) shifts centre of gravity lower in the long irons and higher in the short irons. Heads are hand-forged from S20C soft carbon steel at the Miura factory in Himeji, Japan, and sold component-style through authorized dealers and fitters, so there is no stock shaft; the lengths below are the standard assembly lengths from Miura's own published spec chart, though built lengths vary with the order. It was succeeded by the TC-202, announced 11 March 2025.

Frequently asked questions

What is the loft of a Miura TC-201 7-iron?

The Miura TC-201 7-iron has a loft of 33 degrees, with a 62.5-degree lie and a 37-inch standard steel length.

What does TC stand for in Miura TC-201?

TC stands for Tour Cavity. The TC-201 is a shallow cavity-back that sits between Miura's MB-101 muscle-back and its larger cavity-backs, pairing a blade-like profile with modest heel-toe forgiveness.

Is the Miura TC-201 discontinued?

Yes. Miura announced its successor, the TC-202, on 11 March 2025, and the TC-201 has been removed from Miura's current line.

Does the Miura TC-201 come with a stock shaft?

No. Miura sells the TC-201 as forged heads through authorized dealers and fitters, who build them to order, so there is no factory stock shaft.

Finding a used set

The Miura TC-201 ran from 2020 to 2025 and is no longer in production, so the used market is the only source. Search eBay, 2nd Swing, Golf Avenue and the PGA Tour Superstore trade-in listings, and check the loft and lie against the chart above before buying — sets this age have often been bent from standard.
