About the Miura CB-2008

The CB-2008 was Miura's most forgiving iron to date when the company announced it on 6 June 2017, part of the transitional "MG Collection" launch alongside the CB-1008 and MB-5005 that reached Western retail that autumn. Miura's own release framed it as proof "forged irons are not reserved for the game's most skilled players": the 5-through-8 heads weld a 4 mm forged face to a SUS stainless composite pocket cavity - the first composite-body iron made exclusively at the Himeji factory - for the widest sole Miura had built, 20 mm at the 7-iron, while the 9-iron and pitching wedge revert to a conventional one-piece forging. It continued a house lineage of progressively easier-to-hit Miura irons that ran P9005, P9005G, then the CB-2008, before the dedicated IC-601 arrived within the same product cycle and took over the forgiving-iron slot; the CB-2008's own run appears to have been brief. Miura sells the head through authorised fitters built to order, so no stock shaft is recorded.

Frequently asked questions

What is the loft of a Miura CB-2008 7-iron?

The Miura CB-2008 7-iron has a loft of 32 degrees and a lie of 61 degrees, per matching dealer spec charts.

Is the Miura CB-2008 forged?

Partly. The 5-8 irons weld a 4 mm forged carbon-steel face to a stainless composite pocket-cavity body - Miura's first composite-body iron - while the 9-iron and pitching wedge are conventional one-piece forgings.

What replaced the Miura CB-2008?

The IC-601, which arrived within the same product cycle as Miura's next, more dedicated step in forgiving-iron design; the CB-2008 does not appear to have stayed in the catalog long.

How forgiving is the Miura CB-2008 compared to other Miura irons?

Very, by Miura standards: a 20 mm sole at the 7-iron, the widest Miura had built at the time, and Miura marketed it explicitly as proof that forged irons could work for golfers of all skill levels, not just better players.

Finding a used set

The Miura CB-2008 ran from 2017 to 2018 and is no longer in production, so the used market is the only source. Search eBay, 2nd Swing, Golf Avenue and the PGA Tour Superstore trade-in listings, and check the loft and lie against the chart above before buying — sets this age have often been bent from standard.
