About the Miura IC-601

The IC-601 was Miura's first genuine game-improvement iron, introduced in late 2017 to give mid and higher handicaps a way into the Himeji brand. It breaks with the house one-piece forging: an inner-cavity hollow construction pairs a 455 Carpenter steel face with a SUS304 stainless body and an adjustable back weight screw, producing a low centre of gravity, a bigger sweet spot and a wider sole under a noticeably larger head with a thicker topline than any Miura forging. Lofts are only modestly strong — a 31-degree 7-iron and 45-degree pitching wedge across a 4-GW factory chart — so Miura's launch line was distance through CG rather than jacked lofts. It stayed in the range for six years and appeared in Golf.com's ClubTest in both 2018 and 2020; the hollow-body IC-602 replaced it in March 2024. Miura sells through authorized fitters and builds sets to order from a shaft menu, so no stock shaft is recorded; the lengths below are from Miura's own factory chart.

Frequently asked questions

What is the loft of a Miura IC-601 7-iron?

The Miura IC-601 7-iron has a loft of 31 degrees, a lie of 61.5 degrees and a factory length of 37 inches.

Is the Miura IC-601 forged?

Not in the traditional Miura sense. It pairs a 455 Carpenter steel face with a SUS304 stainless body in an inner-cavity construction with a back weight screw — the point was game-improvement help rather than one-piece forging.

Who is the Miura IC-601 for?

Miura aimed it at mid and higher handicaps wanting extra distance and forgiveness with Miura looks — the largest head and thickest topline the company offered at the time.

What replaced the Miura IC-601?

The hollow-body IC-602, announced 19 March 2024 and available from 16 April 2024.

Finding a used set

The Miura IC-601 ran from 2017 to 2024 and is no longer in production, so the used market is the only source. Search eBay, 2nd Swing, Golf Avenue and the PGA Tour Superstore trade-in listings, and check the loft and lie against the chart above before buying — sets this age have often been bent from standard.
