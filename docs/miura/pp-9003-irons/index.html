About the Miura PP-9003

The Passing Point 9003 was Miura's invitation to golfers who wanted the Himeji forged feel without a tour player's precision — the friendliest iron the family workshop made. Designed by the Miura sons Shinei and Yoshitaka and introduced worldwide on 1 April 2011, it is a forged cavity back with perimeter weighting, a wider sole shaped to glide through turf, generous offset that flows into the head, and a low centre of gravity for a high, soft-landing flight. Lofts are traditional — a 32-degree 7-iron and 45-degree pitching wedge across a 4-GW factory chart. A Straight Neck (PP-9003 SN) variant followed with the same lofts and lies but roughly a third less offset, for players who did not want the trajectory help. The PP-9003 remained in Miura's range through the brand's 2017 change of ownership and left the line around the 2019 relaunch of the lineup, with no direct successor named. Miura sold heads through dealers and fitters, so the lengths below are Miura's standard assembly lengths of the era rather than a factory sheet; specs are for the regular (offset) model.

Frequently asked questions

What is the loft of a Miura PP-9003 7-iron?

The Miura PP-9003 7-iron has a loft of 32 degrees and a lie of 61.5 degrees, with about 4.3 mm of offset and a standard-build length of 37 inches.

What does Passing Point mean on the Miura PP-9003?

It is the Miura family's name for the moments a golfer passes from one stage to the next — the PP-9003 was designed as the 'friendly' Miura, a forged cavity back with a wider sole and real offset for golfers still improving.

What is the Miura PP-9003 Straight Neck?

A variant with the same lofts, lies and weights but roughly a third less offset (3.8 mm at the 4-iron against the regular model's 5.6), for players who liked the head but not the trajectory help.

When was the Miura PP-9003 made?

It was introduced worldwide on 1 April 2011 and stayed in the range until around 2019, when Miura's relaunched lineup dropped it without a named successor.

Finding a used set

The Miura PP-9003 ran from 2011 to 2019 and is no longer in production, so the used market is the only source. Search eBay, 2nd Swing, Golf Avenue and the PGA Tour Superstore trade-in listings, and check the loft and lie against the chart above before buying — sets this age have often been bent from standard.
