About the Miura CB-301

The CB-301, announced on 15 January 2019, was the largest and most distance-oriented cavity back Miura had produced to that point — the company's launch release called it the longest forged cavity back in its history and its first fully forged cavity back since the PP-9003 of 2011 — aimed at players who wanted the Himeji forging in a more forgiving head. Each iron carries a variable sole and cavity thickness so the center of gravity is tuned by loft, and the lofts are the strongest of the Miura irons of its era — a 32-degree 7-iron and 44-degree pitching wedge, with the stock range running 4-iron through a 48-degree gap wedge. Miura removed the model from its lineup around the March 2023 launch of the mid-size CB-302, which took over the same loft structure. Miura sells heads through authorized fitters and builds sets to order, so no stock shaft is recorded.

Frequently asked questions

What is the loft of a Miura CB-301 7-iron?

The Miura CB-301 7-iron has a loft of 32 degrees, a lie of 62.5 degrees and a length of 37.0 inches.

Is the Miura CB-301 discontinued?

Yes. Miura announced the mid-size CB-302 in March 2023 and the CB-301 subsequently left the miuragolf.com lineup, though dealers continued to build remaining heads to order.

What clubs were available in the Miura CB-301?

4-iron through a 48-degree gap wedge, with a 44-degree pitching wedge — the strongest lofts in the Miura iron range of its era.

What did the Miura CB-301 replace?

Miura's launch release called it the company's first fully forged cavity back since the PP-9003 of 2011. It was succeeded by the mid-size CB-302, announced in March 2023.

Finding a used set

The Miura CB-301 ran from 2019 to 2023 and is no longer in production, so the used market is the only source. Search eBay, 2nd Swing, Golf Avenue and the PGA Tour Superstore trade-in listings, and check the loft and lie against the chart above before buying — sets this age have often been bent from standard.
