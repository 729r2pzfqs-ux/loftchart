About the Miura MB-5005

The MB-5005 is the blade of Miura's transitional MG Collection, announced for North America in July 2017 alongside the CB-1008 and CB-2008 and sold through fitters at $279 per head. It had first appeared in Japan as a limited run reported at 150 sets, and its trick is subtle: about 15 grams are shaved from where a conventional muscle pad would sit and redistributed around the head, so a club that reads as a blade at address carries an expanded sweet spot and a lower centre of gravity — "looks like a blade but plays like a cavity back" in the launch coverage. The head is slightly longer heel-to-toe than Miura's earlier blades while keeping the thin topline and sole, forged from S25C soft carbon steel in Himeji with the W.D.D. accurate-forging stamp of the MG series, in satin chrome. Lofts are Miura-traditional (34-degree 7-iron, 47-degree pitching wedge) over a 3-PW range. Miura sold it as heads only, so the lengths below are Miura's standard assembly lengths of the era rather than a factory sheet. It left the range around 2019, when the MB-101 took over the flagship blade slot.

Frequently asked questions

What is the loft of a Miura MB-5005 7-iron?

The Miura MB-5005 7-iron has a loft of 34 degrees and a lie of 61 degrees, with a standard-build length of 37 inches.

How is the Miura MB-5005 different from a normal blade?

Roughly 15 grams that would normally form the muscle pad are redistributed around the head, expanding the sweet spot and lowering the centre of gravity — Miura pitched it as a blade at address that plays like a cavity back.

When did the Miura MB-5005 come out?

It reached North America in July 2017 as part of the MG Collection with the CB-1008 and CB-2008, after an earlier limited Japan run, and left the range around 2019 when the MB-101 arrived.

What clubs were offered in the Miura MB-5005?

Heads ran 3-iron through pitching wedge (47 degrees), sold through authorized fitters who build any composition to order.

Finding a used set

The Miura MB-5005 ran from 2017 to 2019 and is no longer in production, so the used market is the only source. Search eBay, 2nd Swing, Golf Avenue and the PGA Tour Superstore trade-in listings, and check the loft and lie against the chart above before buying — sets this age have often been bent from standard.
