About the Miura MB-001

The MB-001 was Miura's first new blade in six years when it debuted in October 2013, replacing the Tournament Blade with a slightly stronger loft structure, a rounded leading-edge grind and increased sole camber for cleaner turf interaction. Nicknamed the "Baby Blade" by players for its compact profile, it carried traditional specs — a 34-degree 7-iron and a 47-degree pitching wedge across a 3-PW right-hand-only range — and sold through authorized Miura dealers at roughly $235 per club, built to order. It remained the company's flagship muscle-back until Miura announced the MB-101 in October 2019, billed as the maker's first new blade since 2013. Miura sold the MB-001 as component heads through fitters and published no standard assembly lengths, so the length column is omitted here.

Frequently asked questions

What is the loft of a Miura MB-001 7-iron?

The Miura MB-001 7-iron has a loft of 34 degrees and a lie of 61 degrees. Miura published no standard length because sets were built to order through dealers.

Why is the Miura MB-001 called the Baby Blade?

Players nicknamed the MB-001 the Baby Blade for its compact head. It was Miura's first new blade in six years when it debuted in October 2013, replacing the Tournament Blade.

What replaced the Miura MB-001?

The Miura MB-101, announced in October 2019 and billed by Miura as its first new blade since 2013, with sole width and face thickness reworked from the MB-001.

What clubs were available in the Miura MB-001?

3-iron through pitching wedge, right handed only, with a traditional 47-degree pitching wedge. Heads were sold through authorized Miura dealers and built to order.

Finding a used set

The Miura MB-001 ran from 2013 to 2019 and is no longer in production, so the used market is the only source. Search eBay, 2nd Swing, Golf Avenue and the PGA Tour Superstore trade-in listings, and check the loft and lie against the chart above before buying — sets this age have often been bent from standard.
