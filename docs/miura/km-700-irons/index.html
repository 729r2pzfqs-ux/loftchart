About the Miura KM-700

The KM-700 carries founder Katsuhiro Miura's initials and is presented as his signature design, five years in development before its unveiling in May 2022. It is hand-forged from S20C soft carbon steel in Himeji, Japan, using the factory's third-strike forging process, and blends a blade-like profile with a more forgiving head: a toe-cut grind on the sole tunes camber and bounce, and reshaped hosel and heel geometry shifts the centre of gravity about three millimetres toward the toe, behind the centre of the face. Each head is approved with Katsuhiro Miura's red hanko stamp in the back cavity. Lofts are traditional for the line — a 33-degree 7-iron and 46-degree pitching wedge across the 4-PW range, at $2,800 for a 4-PW set of heads at launch. Miura sells heads through authorized dealers and fitters and builds sets to order, so no stock shaft is recorded.

Frequently asked questions

What is the loft of a Miura KM-700 7-iron?

The Miura KM-700 7-iron has a loft of 33 degrees, a lie of 62.5 degrees and a length of 37.0 inches.

What does KM stand for in the Miura KM-700?

The initials of founder Katsuhiro Miura. The KM-700 is presented as his signature design, five years in development, and each head carries his red hanko approval stamp in the back cavity.

When was the Miura KM-700 released and what did it cost?

Miura unveiled the KM-700 in May 2022, at $2,800 for a 4-PW set of heads, built to order through authorized dealers and fitters.

Where it sits in the lineup

Introduced in 2022, the Miura KM-700 is a current players set in the Miura range as of September 2026, with no replacement announced. New sets are built to the standard lofts, lies and lengths in the chart above, though a fitting can move any of them — so confirm the numbers on the order sheet. Used sets appear on eBay, 2nd Swing and Golf Avenue, and should be measured against this chart before buying.
