About the Nike Slingshot OSS

The Slingshot OSS pushed the original Slingshot's design further toward maximum forgiveness: the Slingback bridge sits farther back from the face, the already-wide sole gets wider still, and 3-D flow weighting moves mass low and deep in the long irons with a progressively higher centre of gravity as the set shortens. Offset is generous throughout. Lofts carry over from the original Slingshot, with a 34-degree 7-iron and a 46-degree pitching wedge. The stock set is 4-iron through 50-degree approach wedge, with the 2-iron, 3-iron and sand wedge as custom orders. The per-club lie and length figures below come from a spec database that appears to round to whole units, so treat them as approximate; the lofts are solid.

Frequently asked questions

What is the loft of a Nike Slingshot OSS 7-iron?

The Nike Slingshot OSS 7-iron has a loft of 34 degrees - the same as the original Slingshot - with a lie of about 64 degrees and a steel-shaft length of about 37 inches.

What does OSS stand for in the Nike Slingshot OSS?

Nike marketed it as the oversize evolution of the Slingshot: the Slingback bridge moved farther from the face and the sole got wider, making it the most forgiving, super-game-improvement member of the family.

What clubs came in a stock Nike Slingshot OSS set?

The stock set was 4-iron through a 50-degree approach wedge. The 2-iron, 3-iron and 55-degree sand wedge were available as custom orders.

Finding a used set

The Nike Slingshot OSS ran from 2006 to 2007 and is no longer in production, so the used market is the only source. Search eBay, 2nd Swing, Golf Avenue and the PGA Tour Superstore trade-in listings, and check the loft and lie against the chart above before buying — sets this age have often been bent from standard.
