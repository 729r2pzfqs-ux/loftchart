About the Nike Vapor Fly Pro

The Vapor Fly Pro is the compact better-player model in Nike's final 2016 iron family, sold alongside the game-improvement Vapor Fly until Nike exited club manufacturing in August 2016. The taller, more compact head runs a hollow cavity in the 2- through 5-irons, an RZN-filled pocket in the 6- through 8-irons and a full RZN cavity in the short irons. Its loft ladder is the same as the standard Vapor Fly's — a 32-degree 7-iron and 44-degree pitching wedge, strong for a compact iron of the period — with the difference coming in the smaller head, thinner topline and less offset, plus a set that extends up to a 17-degree 2-iron. The set listed here is 2-iron through pitching wedge plus a 49-degree A-wedge. Offset, bounce and swing weight figures were published only by a single secondary source and are omitted rather than repeated unverified.

Frequently asked questions

What is the loft of a Nike Vapor Fly Pro 7-iron?

The Nike Vapor Fly Pro 7-iron has a loft of 32 degrees, with a 62.5-degree lie and a 37.75-inch steel-shaft length.

What is the difference between the Nike Vapor Fly and Vapor Fly Pro?

They share the same loft ladder, including a 32-degree 7-iron. The Vapor Fly Pro uses a more compact head with a thinner topline and less offset for better players, and its set extends up to a 17-degree 2-iron, while the standard Vapor Fly is the larger game-improvement model.

Were the Nike Vapor Fly Pro irons Nike's last players iron?

Effectively yes. The 2016 Vapor Fly family was Nike's final iron line; the company announced its exit from club manufacturing in August 2016.

Finding a used set

The Nike Vapor Fly Pro ran from 2015 to 2016 and is no longer in production, so the used market is the only source. Search eBay, 2nd Swing, Golf Avenue and the PGA Tour Superstore trade-in listings, and check the loft and lie against the chart above before buying — sets this age have often been bent from standard.
