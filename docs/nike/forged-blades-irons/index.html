About the Nike Forged Blades

The Forged Blades were Nike's first irons, launched in 2002 alongside the Forged Pro Combo as the company entered the club business. They are traditional muscle-back blades with no model name on the head - the back carries only the swoosh, which is why spec databases catalogue them as the Nike "Forged" or "(swoosh) blade" model. The lofts are old-school weak by modern standards, with a 37-degree 7-iron and a 49-degree pitching wedge, and the chart runs all the way to a 17-degree 1-iron. The standard set was 3-PW with the 1- and 2-irons available individually, in steel only. The published lie ladder has an odd step - 60 degrees at the 4-iron jumping to 62 at the 5- and 6-irons - which is transcribed here as printed in the source chart.

Frequently asked questions

What is the loft of a Nike Forged Blades 7-iron?

The Nike Forged Blades 7-iron has a loft of 37 degrees, with a 62.5-degree lie and a 36.75-inch steel-shaft length - a classic weak-lofted blade spec.

How do I identify Nike's original Forged Blades?

There is no model name on the club: the back of the muscle-back head carries only the Nike swoosh near the toe. Spec databases list them as the Nike 'Forged' or '(swoosh) blade' model, sold from 2002 in steel only.

What clubs came in a stock Nike Forged Blades set?

The standard set was 3-iron through pitching wedge with True Temper Dynamic Gold steel shafts. A 17-degree 1-iron and 20-degree 2-iron were on the spec chart as individual clubs.

Finding a used set

The Nike Forged Blades ran from 2002 to 2004 and is no longer in production, so the used market is the only source. Search eBay, 2nd Swing, Golf Avenue and the PGA Tour Superstore trade-in listings, and check the loft and lie against the chart above before buying — sets this age have often been bent from standard.
