About the Nike VR Pro Blade

The VR Pro Blade is the third blade set Nike ever made, announced in October 2010 with retail from 26 November 2010, and the first with the X3X groove pattern — deeper grooves set closer together to conform to the 2010 USGA and R&A groove rules. It keeps the same grind and profile as the VR Forged TW Blade it replaced, with a precision-forging process that delivers the head near-finished. Despite the name it is progressive: the 2- through 4-irons carry a pocket cavity, the 5- through 7-irons a split cavity, and only the 8-iron through pitching wedge are full blades. Lofts are fully traditional, with a 35-degree 7-iron and a 47-degree pitching wedge, and bounce runs progressively from 2 to 9 degrees. The stock set is 3-PW with Dynamic Gold shafts, the 2-iron sold separately. Nike announced no further blade after it; the model line ended when the Vapor iron family (whose most compact model, the Vapor Pro, is a modified muscle-back rather than a true blade) was announced in 2014.

Frequently asked questions

What is the loft of a Nike VR Pro Blade 7-iron?

The Nike VR Pro Blade 7-iron has a loft of 35 degrees, with a 62.5-degree lie and a 37-inch steel-shaft length.

Are the Nike VR Pro Blades true blades throughout the set?

No. The 2- through 4-irons use a pocket cavity for forgiveness, the 5- through 7-irons a split cavity, and only the 8-iron through pitching wedge are full blade designs.

When were the Nike VR Pro Blades released?

Nike announced them in October 2010 and they reached retail on 26 November 2010, priced at $1,079.99 for 3-PW with Dynamic Gold steel shafts. The 2-iron was sold separately.

How do the Nike VR Pro Blade lofts compare to the VR Pro Combo?

They are identical from the 3-iron (21 degrees) through the pitching wedge (47 degrees), and both sets carry the same progressive 2-to-9-degree bounce. The Blade additionally offered an 18-degree 2-iron and publishes offset figures.

Finding a used set

The Nike VR Pro Blade ran from 2010 to 2014 and is no longer in production, so the used market is the only source. Search eBay, 2nd Swing, Golf Avenue and the PGA Tour Superstore trade-in listings, and check the loft and lie against the chart above before buying — sets this age have often been bent from standard.
