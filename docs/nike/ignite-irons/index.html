About the Nike Ignite

The Ignite was Nike's value-tier game-improvement iron, sold alongside and below the more heavily marketed Slingshot line: a cast cavity-back head with a deep undercut, wide sole and pronounced offset aimed at beginners and high-handicap players rather than the Slingshot's broader mid-market pitch. The stock set replaced the traditional 3-iron with a 3-hybrid (labelled 3H) ahead of 4-iron through pitching wedge, with a sand wedge and, on some listings, an approach wedge sold as add-ons. Nike ran the name as a multi-year budget franchise — Ignite, then Ignite 2 and Ignite 3 — each generation getting little independent marketing, which is why retailer and forum documentation is thin compared with Nike's flagship iron lines of the same years.

Frequently asked questions

What is the loft of a Nike Ignite 7-iron?

The one spec table located for this model lists the Ignite 7-iron at 34 degrees with a 63-degree lie. That figure is not independently corroborated and matches the Nike Slingshot's 7-iron exactly, so it should be treated as indicative rather than certified.

What clubs came in a stock Nike Ignite set?

Retailer listings describe a 4-iron through pitching wedge set with a 3-hybrid (3H) in place of a traditional 3-iron, with a sand wedge and sometimes an approach wedge available as add-ons.

When did the Nike Ignite irons come out, and how do they relate to Ignite 2 and Ignite 3?

The exact year is poorly documented. Secondary sources point to 2006, the same year Nike refreshed its Slingshot OSS and CCi Forged irons. Ignite continued as a running budget franchise, with Ignite 2 and later Ignite 3 following as successive generations, each with little dedicated marketing.

Where did the Nike Ignite sit in Nike's iron lineup?

Below the Slingshot, which got a dedicated marketing mini-site and Golf Digest Hot List recognition. The Ignite was Nike's lower-price, more basic game-improvement offering aimed at beginners and high-handicap players.

Buying a set

Nike has since introduced the Nike Ignite 2 above it in the range, so the Ignite is at the end of its retail life rather than fully retired — closeout sets still turn up at retailers while stock lasts, and it is well represented used on eBay, 2nd Swing and Golf Avenue. Check the loft and lie against the chart above before buying a used set: irons are routinely bent from standard during a fitting.
