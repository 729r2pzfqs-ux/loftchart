About the Nike Vapor Fly

The Vapor Fly was the game-improvement iron in Nike's final iron family, announced in December 2015 and sold as the 2016 line, shortly before Nike exited club manufacturing altogether. The 4- through 7-irons use a hollow cavity to raise launch, while the 8-iron, 9-iron and pitching wedge switch to an RZN-filled pocket for stability and feel. It has a thick topline, a wide sole and more offset than the Vapor Fly Pro sold alongside it. Lofts are strong for the period, with a 32-degree 7-iron and a 44-degree pitching wedge. The set listed here is 4-iron through pitching wedge plus a 54-degree sand wedge; note the 10-degree gap between PW and SW, which is how the set was sold rather than an error in the table. Offset, bounce and swing weight were not published in the source and are omitted rather than inferred.

Frequently asked questions

What is the loft of a Nike Vapor Fly 7-iron?

The Nike Vapor Fly 7-iron has a loft of 32 degrees, with a 62.5-degree lie and a 37.75-inch steel-shaft length.

What is the difference between the Nike Vapor Fly and Vapor Fly Pro?

The Vapor Fly is the game-improvement model with a thicker topline, wider sole and more offset. The Vapor Fly Pro is the compact version aimed at better players; it shares the same loft ladder but has less offset and extends up to a 17-degree 2-iron.

Were the Nike Vapor Fly irons the last Nike irons?

Effectively yes. Nike announced its exit from golf club and ball manufacturing in August 2016, and the 2016 Vapor family was the final iron line it brought to market.

Finding a used set

The Nike Vapor Fly ran from 2015 to 2016 and is no longer in production, so the used market is the only source. Search eBay, 2nd Swing, Golf Avenue and the PGA Tour Superstore trade-in listings, and check the loft and lie against the chart above before buying — sets this age have often been bent from standard.
