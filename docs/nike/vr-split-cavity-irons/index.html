About the Nike VR Split Cavity

The Victory Red Forged Split Cavity - marketed and reviewed almost universally as the "VR Split Cavity" - was announced on 18 November 2008 as the middle tier of Nike's three-way Victory Red iron launch, sitting between the muscleback Victory Red Forged TW Blade and the game-improvement Victory Red Full Cavity. It is forged from 1025 carbon steel with a CNC-milled face and a split-back cavity that moves the centre of gravity behind the sweet spot, giving it a wider sole, thicker topline and more offset than the TW Blade while sharing an identical, fully traditional loft and lie ladder - a 35-degree 7-iron and a 47-degree pitching wedge. The stock set is 3-iron through pitching wedge in True Temper Dynamic Gold steel only (no graphite offered), with the 2-iron sold separately. Nike did not announce a named successor when the VR Pro generation replaced the Victory Red line in 2011, but the split-cavity/combo concept continued most directly in the players-category VR Pro Combo.

Frequently asked questions

What is the loft of a Nike VR Split Cavity 7-iron?

The Nike VR Split Cavity 7-iron has a loft of 35 degrees, with a 62.5-degree lie and a 37-inch steel-shaft length - identical to the Victory Red Forged TW Blade of the same generation.

Are the Nike VR Split Cavity irons the same as the Victory Red blades?

They share an identical loft and lie ladder, but the Split Cavity has a wider sole, thicker topline and more offset than the muscleback Victory Red Forged TW Blade, trading a little workability for added forgiveness from a split-back cavity.

When were the Nike VR Split Cavity irons released?

Nike announced the Victory Red iron family, including the Forged Split Cavity, on 18 November 2008, with a $999 MSRP (about $899 street) for the 3-iron through pitching wedge in True Temper Dynamic Gold steel; the 2-iron was sold separately.

Finding a used set

The Nike VR Split Cavity ran from 2008 to 2010 and is no longer in production, so the used market is the only source. Search eBay, 2nd Swing, Golf Avenue and the PGA Tour Superstore trade-in listings, and check the loft and lie against the chart above before buying — sets this age have often been bent from standard.
