About the Nike Slingshot Tour

The Slingshot Tour was the better-player member of Nike's Slingshot family, co-developed with input from Nike Golf's PGA Tour staff for golfers who wanted a classic profile with the Slingshot's game-improvement technology underneath. Compared with the original Slingshot and the wider, more forgiving Slingshot OSS, the Tour's Slingback bridge sits lower profile and less visible at address, the head is more compact with a thinner top-line and minimal offset, and the body is milled from 431 stainless steel (25% softer than the 17-4 stainless used in most cast irons) paired with the same Custom 455 stainless face as its siblings. Lofts carry over from the rest of the family, with an 18-degree 2-iron and a 34-degree 7-iron running to a 46-degree pitching wedge. The stock set was 3-iron through pitching wedge in steel only, with the 2-iron available as an individual order.

Frequently asked questions

What is the loft of a Nike Slingshot Tour 7-iron?

The Nike Slingshot Tour 7-iron has a loft of 34 degrees, with a lie of about 62 degrees and a 37-inch steel-shaft length.

What is the difference between the Nike Slingshot Tour and Slingshot OSS irons?

The Tour has a more compact head, a thinner top-line, minimal offset and a Slingback bridge that sits lower and is less visible at address, aimed at better players. The Slingshot OSS has a wider sole and more visible Slingback for maximum forgiveness. Both share the same loft progression and Custom 455 stainless face.

What clubs came in a stock Nike Slingshot Tour set?

The stock set was 3-iron through pitching wedge, in steel shafts only (True Temper Dynamic Gold R300, S300 or X100). The 2-iron was available as an individual order.

When did the Nike Slingshot Tour irons come out?

Archived retailer and specification records date the Slingshot Tour to 2006, the same year as the Slingshot OSS; the earliest known review of it is archived from November 2006.

Production status

Introduced in 2006, the Nike Slingshot Tour has no confirmed end date on file. LoftChart records a discontinuation year only where a source dates it, and none has been found for this set, so it may or may not still be in the Nike catalogue — check with Nike or a retailer before counting on a new set. Used sets appear on eBay, 2nd Swing and Golf Avenue, and should be measured against the chart above before buying, since irons are routinely bent from standard.
