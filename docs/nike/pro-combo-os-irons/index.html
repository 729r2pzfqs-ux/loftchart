About the Nike Pro Combo OS

The Pro Combo OS is the oversized, forgiving half of the 2005 split of Nike's original Forged Pro Combo line, sold alongside the better-player Pro Combo Tour. Like the original it is a progressive set forged from soft 1030 carbon steel: the long irons carry a deep undercut cavity with a low centre of gravity, the mid irons a full cavity back, and the short irons a muscle-cavity with a higher centre of gravity for control. The heads are larger than the Tour version with a wider sole and progressive offset. Stock shafts were Nike's Speed Step steel with Tour Velvet-style grips. The per-club figures below are the traditional Nike loft/lie/length ladder shared across the Pro Combo family; period sources confirm individual points on that ladder for the OS (43-degree 9-iron, 35-degree 7-iron) but no complete manufacturer chart for the OS specifically has survived, so treat the numbers as the family-standard specification rather than an OS-specific sheet.

Frequently asked questions

What is the loft of a Nike Pro Combo OS 7-iron?

The Nike Pro Combo OS 7-iron is listed at 35 degrees on the Pro Combo family spec ladder, with a 62.5-degree lie and 37-inch steel length.

What is the difference between the Nike Pro Combo OS and Pro Combo Tour?

Both are 2005 successors to the original Forged Pro Combo. The Tour kept compact better-player heads, while the OS used larger heads with a wider sole, more offset and a deeper undercut cavity in the long irons for extra forgiveness.

What construction does the Nike Pro Combo OS use?

It is forged from 1030 carbon steel as a progressive set: deep undercut-cavity long irons, full cavity-back mid irons and muscle-cavity short irons, so forgiveness is concentrated where it is needed most.

Finding a used set

The Nike Pro Combo OS ran from 2005 to 2006 and is no longer in production, so the used market is the only source. Search eBay, 2nd Swing, Golf Avenue and the PGA Tour Superstore trade-in listings, and check the loft and lie against the chart above before buying — sets this age have often been bent from standard.
