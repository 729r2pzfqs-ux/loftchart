About the Nike NDS

NDS stands for Nike Distance Series, an affordable game-improvement iron aimed at beginners and mid-to-high-handicap golfers rather than better players. The cast 431 stainless steel body is notably softer than typical 17-4 cast stainless for extra feel, and a notch cut into the back cavity concentrates weight toward the heel and toe; the notch is wider in the long irons for maximum forgiveness and narrows through the short irons to raise the center of gravity for more workability. A low, deep CG and progressive offset (heaviest in the long irons) are built in to help get the ball airborne and reduce fat shots. Lofts are moderately strong for a game-improvement set of its era, with a 35-degree 7-iron; the standard men's set runs 3-iron through pitching wedge with a matching sand wedge available.

Frequently asked questions

What is the loft of a Nike NDS 7-iron?

The Nike NDS 7-iron has a loft of 35 degrees, a 63-degree lie angle, and a 37-inch steel-shaft length.

What does NDS stand for on Nike irons?

NDS stands for Nike Distance Series, an affordable game-improvement iron line Nike introduced in 2007 aimed at beginners and mid-to-high-handicap golfers.

What are Nike NDS irons made of?

The heads are cast from 431 stainless steel, which Nike marketed as roughly 25 percent softer than the 17-4 stainless steel used in most cast iron heads, intended to give a softer feel while keeping manufacturing costs down.

What clubs came in a stock Nike NDS iron set?

The standard men's set ran 3-iron through pitching wedge, with a matching 55-degree sand wedge available, in either stock True Temper NDS steel (Uniflex) or Fujikura NDS graphite shafts.

Production status

Introduced in 2007, the Nike NDS has no confirmed end date on file. LoftChart records a discontinuation year only where a source dates it, and none has been found for this set, so it may or may not still be in the Nike catalogue — check with Nike or a retailer before counting on a new set. Used sets appear on eBay, 2nd Swing and Golf Avenue, and should be measured against the chart above before buying, since irons are routinely bent from standard.
