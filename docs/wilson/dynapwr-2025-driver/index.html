Hosel adjustment chart

Six-way adjustable hosel · loft −1° to +2° · 6 settings.

None — Wilson's hosel changes loft and face angle only, with no lie adjustment at any position. Face angle shifts with loft, up to about 2°: adding loft closes the face, so a 9° head set to 11° sits visibly closed at address.

The six positions span −1° to +2° in half-degree steps. Wilson does not publish the full position-by-position table online, so the exact six labels are not reproduced here — the confirmed anchors are the range, the half-degree increments, zero lie change and the face-angle shift.

About the Wilson Dynapwr

Wilson's 2025 Dynapwr generation reached retail on 12 February 2025 as a three-head family — Carbon, LS and Max — and grew to four on 29 January 2026 when the Dynapwr Max+ arrived as Wilson's first driver past 10,000 g·cm² of combined MOI. All use the PKR-360 variable-thickness face and Wilson's Open Hosel Construction, which removes material behind the six-way adjustable hosel so the face can flex more freely. The Carbon is the tour-inspired head Wilson says fits the most golfers, the compact 445 cc LS carries the heaviest movable weights for the biggest CG shift, the all-titanium Max is the big-footprint forgiveness head, and the Max+ sits above it with a carbon crown and the 10K MOI figure. Lite builds at 44.75 inches and D0 cover slower swing speeds.

Dynapwr LS8°, 9°, 10.5°
The most compact, fade-biased low-spin head, carrying the heaviest movable weights in the family (6 g and 12 g front-back) for the biggest CG shift. 8° is right-hand only.
Dynapwr Max9°, 10.5°, 12°
The largest-footprint, highest-MOI head of the 2025 trio — all-titanium, with a 19 g flippable asymmetric rear weight set neutral or heel-side for draw.

Added January 2026: Wilson's first 10K-MOI driver, with a carbon crown, a re-engineered lighter PKR-360 face and an adjustable rear weight. A 12° Lite build (44.75 inches, D0, UST Helium NCT 4) serves slower swings.

Frequently asked questions

What lofts does the Wilson Dynapwr come in?

The 2025-26 generation: Dynapwr Carbon and LS in 8, 9 and 10.5 degrees (8s right-hand only), and the Dynapwr Max and Max+ in 9, 10.5 and 12.

How adjustable is the Wilson Dynapwr hosel?

Six positions spanning minus 1 to plus 2 degrees of loft in half-degree steps. Unusually, the lie never changes — only loft and, with it, face angle, which closes up to about 2 degrees as loft is added.

What is the difference between the Dynapwr Max and Max+?

The 2025 Max is all-titanium with a 19 gram flippable rear weight; the 2026 Max+ adds a carbon crown and a lighter PKR-360 face to push combined MOI past 10,000 g·cm² — Wilson's first 10K driver — while keeping the same loft menu.

Which Dynapwr suits a slower swing speed?

The 12 degree Max or Max+ in the Lite build — 44.75 inches, D0 swing weight and the lightweight UST Helium NCT 4 shaft — is Wilson's package for swings well under 90 mph.

Where it sits in the lineup

Introduced in 2025, the Wilson Dynapwr is the current Wilson driver line as of October 2026, with no replacement announced. It replaced the Wilson Dynapwr (2023). New heads are sold in the stock lofts charted above; the hosel settings extend each head beyond its stamped loft, so a loft between two stock options is usually reachable from either. Before buying used, check the stamped loft against the chart above and ask which hosel setting it is in — an adjustable driver may be set a degree or more away from the number on the sole, and sleeve adapters are brand- and generation-specific.
