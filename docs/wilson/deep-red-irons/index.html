About the Wilson Deep Red

The Deep Red irons carried Wilson's early-2000s Deep Red brand into the iron category from February 2002, pairing oversized stainless cavity-back heads with the company's signature Fat Shaft — a nearly half-inch shaft tip running straight into the head to stiffen the joint and resist twisting. A urethane-filled "fluid feel" insert in the cavity damps the sting of mishits. The published range ran from a 1-iron to a lob wedge; stock sets were sold 3-PW or 4-SW. The Deep Red II family replaced it in 2003.

Frequently asked questions

What is the loft of a Wilson Deep Red 7-iron?

The Wilson Deep Red 7-iron has a loft of 32 degrees, a lie of 62 degrees and a length of 37.5 inches.

What is the Fat Shaft on Wilson Deep Red irons?

It is Wilson's oversized shaft tip — nearly half an inch in diameter where it meets the head — designed to stiffen the hosel joint and reduce head twisting on off-centre strikes.

When were Wilson Deep Red irons made?

The original Deep Red irons launched in February 2002 and were replaced by the Deep Red II family in 2003.

Finding a used set

The Wilson Deep Red ran from 2002 to 2003 and is no longer in production, so the used market is the only source. Search eBay, 2nd Swing, Golf Avenue and the PGA Tour Superstore trade-in listings, and check the loft and lie against the chart above before buying — sets this age have often been bent from standard.
