About the Wilson Fat Shaft

The Fat Shaft irons introduced Wilson's best-known late-90s technology: a shaft with a hugely oversized tip — .500 inch against the conventional .370 — running straight into the head to stiffen the hosel zone and cut head twist on mishits. The heads themselves are oversized stainless cavity backs with upright lies by the standards of the day. Wilson kept the recipe through several catalog years (FS Fat Shaft, Fat Shaft, and Fat Shaft RM/Tour variants) before folding the technology into the Deep Red line from 2002.

Frequently asked questions

What is the loft of a Wilson Fat Shaft 7-iron?

The Wilson Fat Shaft 7-iron has a loft of 33 degrees, a lie of 63 degrees and a length of 37.5 inches.

What made the Wilson Fat Shaft irons different?

The shaft tip measured .500 inch — far larger than the conventional .370 — which stiffened the hosel area and reduced head twisting on off-centre hits. Replacement shafts must be Fat Shaft-specific because of that tip diameter.

Are Fat Shaft lies upright?

Yes. At 63 degrees on the 7-iron and 65 on the wedges, the Fat Shaft ladder runs one to two degrees more upright than the typical late-90s spec.

Finding a used set

The Wilson Fat Shaft ran from 1998 to 2002 and is no longer in production, so the used market is the only source. Search eBay, 2nd Swing, Golf Avenue and the PGA Tour Superstore trade-in listings, and check the loft and lie against the chart above before buying — sets this age have often been bent from standard.
