About the Wilson Dynapwr (2023)

The 2023 Dynapwr revived Wilson's Dynapower name roughly seventy years after the original irons, as a two-head driver family. The Dynapwr Titanium is the forgiveness model — a 16 g rear weight pulls the centre of gravity low and deep for a higher, neutral-to-draw flight, with lofts up to 13 degrees — while the Dynapwr Carbon trades the crown and sole for carbon panels and moves mass forward for lower spin. Both use a machine-learning-optimised variable-thickness face and Wilson's six-way adjustable hosel. The three-head 2025 Dynapwr generation replaced the pair.

Frequently asked questions

What lofts does the 2023 Wilson Dynapwr come in?

The Dynapwr Titanium comes in 9, 10.5 and 13 degrees; the Dynapwr Carbon in 9, 10.5 and 12. The six-way hosel extends each a further minus 1 to plus 2 degrees.

What is the difference between the Dynapwr Titanium and Carbon?

The Titanium is the forgiveness model — a 16 gram rear weight for a low, deep CG and higher flight, up to a 13 degree loft. The Carbon swaps crown and sole for carbon panels and moves mass forward for a lower-spinning, lower flight.

Is the 2023 Wilson Dynapwr adjustable?

Yes — the same six-way hosel as the 2025 generation: minus 1 to plus 2 degrees of loft in half-degree steps, with no lie change at any position.

Is the 2023 Dynapwr still in production?

No — the 2025 Dynapwr generation (Carbon, LS and Max) replaced it. It is a used-market buy, usually at bargain prices for a machine-learning-faced driver.

Buying one

Wilson has since introduced the Wilson Dynapwr above it in the range, so the Dynapwr (2023) is at the end of its retail life rather than fully retired — closeout stock still turns up at retailers, and drivers one generation back are the sweet spot of the used market. Before buying used, check the stamped loft against the chart above and ask which hosel setting it is in — an adjustable driver may be set a degree or more away from the number on the sole, and sleeve adapters are brand- and generation-specific.
