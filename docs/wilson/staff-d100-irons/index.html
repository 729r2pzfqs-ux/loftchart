About the Wilson Staff D100

The D100 opened Wilson's modern D-series and its Super Light concept: reduced weight in the head, shaft and grip, paired with an 85-gram True Temper SL85 steel shaft, to help moderate-speed players swing faster. The head uses an undercut cavity with what Wilson called Exoskeleton perimeter weighting, moving around 35 percent more mass to the sole, heel and toe, with a face about seven percent thinner than its predecessor. Lofts are moderate for a distance iron, with a 31-degree 7-iron and a 43-degree pitching wedge. The set ran 4-iron through sand wedge; figures below are the standard men's steel specification.

Frequently asked questions

What is the loft of a Wilson Staff D100 7-iron?

The Wilson Staff D100 7-iron has a loft of 31 degrees, with a 62-degree lie and a 37.5-inch steel-shaft length.

What made the Wilson Staff D100 irons 'superlight'?

Wilson reduced weight across the whole club - head, shaft and grip - and fitted an 85-gram True Temper SL85 steel shaft, so the total club weight was well below a conventional game-improvement iron of the period.

What clubs came in a stock Wilson Staff D100 set?

The ladder ran 4-iron through sand wedge, with the gap wedge at 47 degrees and the sand wedge at 55 degrees; typical retail sets were 4-PW or 5-PW with the wedges as add-ons.

Finding a used set

The Wilson Staff D100 ran from 2013 to 2015 and is no longer in production, so the used market is the only source. Search eBay, 2nd Swing, Golf Avenue and the PGA Tour Superstore trade-in listings, and check the loft and lie against the chart above before buying — sets this age have often been bent from standard.
