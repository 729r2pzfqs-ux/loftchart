About the Wilson Staff Di11

The Di11 closed out Wilson Staff's Di distance line in 2010, built around what Wilson called the largest sweet spot of any iron it had made: weight stripped from the face perimeter and cavity leaves a large unsupported face, with mass moved low for launch and rearward for stability. Reviewers classed it at the most forgiving end of game improvement, and its spec sheet is unconventional — a 46-degree gap wedge sits only three degrees under the pitching wedge, leaving a nine-degree jump to the sand wedge.

Frequently asked questions

What is the loft of a Wilson Staff Di11 7-iron?

The Wilson Staff Di11 7-iron has a loft of 32 degrees, a lie of 62 degrees and a steel-shaft length of 37.5 inches.

Are the Wilson Di11 irons forgiving?

Yes — Wilson claimed the largest sweet spot of any iron it had produced, and reviewers placed the Di11 at the super-game-improvement end of the spectrum.

Why is the Di11 gap wedge only 46 degrees?

Wilson published a 43-degree pitching wedge and 46-degree gap wedge, an unusually tight 3-degree gap that leaves 9 degrees to the 55-degree sand wedge; players wanting even gapping often bent the GW weaker or added a wedge.

Finding a used set

The Wilson Staff Di11 ran from 2010 to 2013 and is no longer in production, so the used market is the only source. Search eBay, 2nd Swing, Golf Avenue and the PGA Tour Superstore trade-in listings, and check the loft and lie against the chart above before buying — sets this age have often been bent from standard.
