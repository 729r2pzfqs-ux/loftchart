About the Wilson Staff D7 Forged

The D7 Forged took the Power Hole concept from the cast D7 and moved it into a forged 8620 carbon steel head aimed at players who want distance in a more compact package. A urethane-filled Power Chamber sits behind the face and the Power Holes are limited to the sole, so the look at address is much cleaner than the standard D7. Lofts are strong for the players-distance class, with a 30.5-degree 7-iron and a 44-degree pitching wedge. The stock set is 4-iron through pitching wedge, with a 49-degree gap wedge available to match; the figures below are the standard men's steel specification.

Frequently asked questions

What is the loft of a Wilson Staff D7 Forged 7-iron?

The Wilson Staff D7 Forged 7-iron has a loft of 30.5 degrees, with a 62-degree lie and a 37.25-inch steel-shaft length.

What is the difference between the Wilson D7 and D7 Forged irons?

The standard D7 is a cast game-improvement iron with Power Holes around the sole and toe; the D7 Forged uses a forged 8620 carbon steel head with a urethane-filled Power Chamber and sole-only Power Holes, in a smaller players-distance shape with slightly stronger lofts club for club.

What clubs come in a stock Wilson Staff D7 Forged set?

The stock set is 4-iron through pitching wedge, with a 49-degree gap wedge available as a matching add-on.

Finding a used set

The Wilson Staff D7 Forged ran from 2020 to 2022 and is no longer in production, so the used market is the only source. Search eBay, 2nd Swing, Golf Avenue and the PGA Tour Superstore trade-in listings, and check the loft and lie against the chart above before buying — sets this age have often been bent from standard.
