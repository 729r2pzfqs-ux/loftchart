About the Wilson Staff Tour Blade

The late-80s Staff Tour Blade — the "Fluid Feel" model collectors know by the red plug in the heel of the sole and the tapered muscle bar — revived the drill-through hosel of Wilson's 1957-75 Staff Dynapower era. Catalog records show the model on sale from 1986 through 1988 (the W/S shield stamp was added in 1989), and Wilson had kept its blade specification essentially unchanged since the 1970s, so the numbers below follow the ladder Ralph Maltby recorded for the Wilson Staff blade in his 1982-published tables: a 38-degree 7-iron and 50-degree pitching wedge, one degree stronger than the 1974-published ladder. Wilson tweaked the specs again with the 1991 Tour Blade.

Frequently asked questions

What is the loft of a 1987 Wilson Staff Tour Blade 7-iron?

Approximately 38 degrees per the 1982-published Maltby tables — though a collector's reading of the 1988 Wilson catalog suggests 39 degrees, matching the older 1974 ladder. Either way it is roughly three clubs weaker than a modern distance iron.

What is the Fluid Feel on Wilson Staff Tour Blades?

Fluid Feel is Wilson's drill-through hosel: the shaft bore runs through to the sole (capped with a small plug), a construction revived on this model from the 1957-75 Staff Dynapower blades. It also makes these irons notoriously difficult to reshaft.

When was this Wilson Staff Tour Blade model sold?

Catalog records show it on sale from 1986 through 1988, with a W/S shield stamp added for 1989-90; it is popularly known as the '87-88 Fluid Feel'. Wilson revised the blade with the 1991 Tour Blade.

Finding a used set

The Wilson Staff Tour Blade ran from 1987 to 1991 and is no longer in production, so the used market is the only source. Search eBay, 2nd Swing, Golf Avenue and the PGA Tour Superstore trade-in listings, and check the loft and lie against the chart above before buying — sets this age have often been bent from standard.
