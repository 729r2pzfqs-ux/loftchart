About the Bridgestone J38 Dual Pocket Cavity

The J38 Dual Pocket Cavity — Bridgestone's own product page titles it the "J38 Dual Cavity Irons" — was the more forgiving of the two J38 forged irons, again aimed at "professionals to mid handicap amateurs." The midsize head is premium-forged from 1020 carbon steel with Bridgestone's dual pocket cavity removing discretionary weight to the perimeter for forgiveness while keeping more mass directly behind the hitting area for feel, plus a tapered sole and trailing/leading-edge relief. Lofts are one degree stronger than the J38 Cavity Back through most of the set (34-degree 7-iron, 46-degree pitching wedge) with correspondingly more offset, every club built to a D1 swing weight on a stock Project X flighted steel shaft with a Golf Pride Tour Velvet grip. It was sold right hand only as a 3-iron through pitching wedge. The J40 Dual Pocket Cavity succeeded it in the 2011 model generation.

Frequently asked questions

What is the loft of a Bridgestone J38 Dual Pocket Cavity 7-iron?

The Bridgestone J38 Dual Pocket Cavity 7-iron has a loft of 34 degrees, with a 63-degree lie, a 36.75-inch length and a D1 swing weight.

What is the difference between the Bridgestone J38 Dual Pocket Cavity and the J38 Cavity Back?

Both are forged from 1020 carbon steel, but the Dual Pocket Cavity uses two milled pockets to move weight to the perimeter for more forgiveness and lofts about a degree stronger (34-degree 7-iron versus 35), while the Cavity Back is the more compact, traditional-loft head with a magnesium-composite insert.

What replaced the Bridgestone J38 Dual Pocket Cavity?

The J40 Dual Pocket Cavity, part of Bridgestone's next forged iron generation.

What clubs came in a stock Bridgestone J38 Dual Pocket Cavity set?

It was sold right hand only as a 3-iron through pitching wedge set, on a stock Project X flighted steel shaft.

Finding a used set

The Bridgestone J38 Dual Pocket Cavity ran from 2010 to 2011 and is no longer in production, so the used market is the only source. Search eBay, 2nd Swing, Golf Avenue and the PGA Tour Superstore trade-in listings, and check the loft and lie against the chart above before buying — sets this age have often been bent from standard.
