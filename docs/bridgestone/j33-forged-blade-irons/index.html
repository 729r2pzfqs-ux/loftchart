About the Bridgestone J33 Forged Blade

The J33 Forged Blade was the muscle-back of Bridgestone's J33 forged line, aimed at "professionals and low handicap amateurs." The head is forged from 1020 mild carbon steel with a long hosel design that raises the center of gravity for extra shot-shaping ability, reduced offset, a thin top line, a small head and a narrow sole with a leading-edge grind and satin chrome finish. Lofts are traditional for the era (36-degree 7-iron, 48-degree pitching wedge, starting from a 2-iron), built to a D3 swing weight on a stock True Temper Dynamic Gold S300 shaft with a Golf Pride Tour Velvet grip. It was sold right hand only. The Condition of Competition rule made the J33 irons non-conforming for professional play from 1 January 2010, and the J36 generation succeeded it in Bridgestone's line-up in 2008.

Frequently asked questions

What is the loft of a Bridgestone J33 Forged Blade 7-iron?

The Bridgestone J33 Forged Blade 7-iron has a loft of 36 degrees, with a 63-degree lie, a 36.75-inch length and a D3 swing weight.

What makes the Bridgestone J33 Forged Blade a true muscle-back?

A long hosel that raises the center of gravity for shot-shaping, reduced offset, a thin top line and a narrow sole — all forged from 1020 mild carbon steel into a small, satin-chrome head.

What replaced the Bridgestone J33 Forged Blade?

The J36 Blade, part of Bridgestone's next forged iron generation, which arrived in Bridgestone's 2008 line-up.

What clubs came in a stock Bridgestone J33 Forged Blade set?

It was sold right hand only, spanning a 2-iron through pitching wedge, on a stock True Temper Dynamic Gold S300 shaft.

Finding a used set

The Bridgestone J33 Forged Blade ran from 2005 to 2008 and is no longer in production, so the used market is the only source. Search eBay, 2nd Swing, Golf Avenue and the PGA Tour Superstore trade-in listings, and check the loft and lie against the chart above before buying — sets this age have often been bent from standard.
