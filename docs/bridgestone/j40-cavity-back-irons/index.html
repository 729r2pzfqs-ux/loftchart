About the Bridgestone J40 Cavity Back

The J40 Cavity Back was the better-player iron in Bridgestone Golf's J40 club line, announced in September 2011 and at retail from October 2011, aimed at professionals through mid-handicap amateurs. Each head is premium-forged from 1020 mild carbon steel with a compact profile, a higher face progression and a V-cut cavity, and the sole carries relief at the leading edge, trailing edge, toe and heel for versatility. Lofts are traditional for the era, with a 35-degree 7-iron and a 47-degree pitching wedge, and every iron is built to a D1 swing weight with a Project X flighted steel shaft and Golf Pride Tour Velvet grip as stock. It was sold right hand only, as a 5-PW set (street price $699, MSRP $799) with the 3-iron and 4-iron available individually at $115 each; a J40 Dual Pocket Cavity model and J40 Combo sets covered players wanting more forgiveness. Offsets below are converted from Bridgestone's published millimetre figures (3.5 mm = 0.138 in). Bridgestone's J15 iron family, introduced in January 2015, succeeded the J40 generation.

Frequently asked questions

What is the loft of a Bridgestone J40 Cavity Back 7-iron?

The Bridgestone J40 Cavity Back 7-iron has a loft of 35 degrees, with a 63-degree lie, a 36.75-inch length and a D1 swing weight.

What clubs come in a stock Bridgestone J40 Cavity Back set?

The stock set was 5-iron through pitching wedge at a $699 street price, with the 3-iron and 4-iron sold individually at about $115 each. The model was offered in right hand only.

What is the difference between the Bridgestone J40 Cavity Back and the J40 Dual Pocket Cavity?

Both are forged J40-generation irons, but the Cavity Back is the compact better-player model with a V-cut cavity and traditional lofts, while the Dual Pocket Cavity uses two milled pockets of removed mass for more perimeter weighting and forgiveness. Bridgestone also sold J40 Combo sets mixing the two.

Are Bridgestone J40 Cavity Back irons forged?

Yes. Bridgestone forged the heads from 1020 mild carbon steel, one of the softer steels used in irons of that era.

Finding a used set

The Bridgestone J40 Cavity Back ran from 2011 to 2015 and is no longer in production, so the used market is the only source. Search eBay, 2nd Swing, Golf Avenue and the PGA Tour Superstore trade-in listings, and check the loft and lie against the chart above before buying — sets this age have often been bent from standard.
