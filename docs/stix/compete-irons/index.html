About the Stix Compete

The Compete marked a sharp turn for Stix Golf away from the beginner- and mid-handicapper-focused Play and Perform lines toward "better players who want more control and workability," per independent review coverage - the company's first players-oriented release. A two-piece hollow body pairs an ultra-thin, high-strength AM355P stainless steel face with a 17-4 stainless body for faster ball speed than the 431 stainless used in Stix's earlier irons, while keeping a tournament-grade, compact profile. MyGolfSpy's review called it a player's-distance iron that stood out for accuracy rather than raw distance. Sold 4-PW at $699 with a 120-gram steel performance shaft standard (graphite optional) and a soft rubber grip, in Reg, Stiff and Active flex options; a Jack Nicklaus-branded "Nicklaus Compete" version followed as Stix's first muscle-back iron collaboration.

Frequently asked questions

What is the loft of a Stix Compete 7-iron?

The Stix Compete 7-iron has a loft of 31 degrees, a lie of 62 degrees and a length of 37 inches.

How does the Stix Compete differ from the Stix Perform?

The Compete is Stix's first line built for better players rather than beginners or mid-handicappers - a two-piece hollow body with an ultra-thin AM355P stainless face for more ball speed, versus the Perform's cast 431 stainless deep cavity back.

What is the Nicklaus Compete Iron Set?

A Jack Nicklaus-branded version of the Compete platform, announced in April 2025 as Stix's first muscleback iron collaboration, built to the same tournament-grade positioning as the standard Compete.

Where it sits in the lineup

Introduced in 2024, the Stix Compete is a current players distance set in the Stix range, with no replacement announced. New sets are built to the standard lofts, lies and lengths in the chart above, though a fitting can move any of them — so confirm the numbers on the order sheet. Used sets appear on eBay, 2nd Swing and Golf Avenue, and should be measured against this chart before buying.
