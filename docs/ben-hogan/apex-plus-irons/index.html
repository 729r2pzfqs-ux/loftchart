About the Ben Hogan Apex Plus

The Apex Plus was the perimeter-weighted companion to the 1999 Ben Hogan Apex blade — a forged 1030 carbon steel head whose modified muscle-cavity kept a traditional profile only slightly larger than the blade while enlarging the sweet spot heel to toe. Hogan's launch material pitched it as the heir to the company's 1987 Edge, the first forged perimeter-weighted iron, and used progressive face thicknesses to move the centre of gravity lower in the long irons and higher in the short irons, with progressive offset for long-iron trajectory. Lofts are traditional (34-degree 7-iron, 46-degree Equalizer) with Hogan's characteristically flat lies (58 degrees at the 1-iron, 64 across the wedges), and the range ran from a 16-degree 1-iron to a 60-degree lob wedge. Hogan's letter wedges are recorded here as PW (Equalizer, 46°), GW (F wedge, 50°), SW (56°) and LW (60°). The Apex Edge announced in January 2002 replaced it as the forgiving forged Hogan.

Frequently asked questions

What is the loft of a Ben Hogan Apex Plus 7-iron?

The Ben Hogan Apex Plus 7-iron has a loft of 34 degrees, with a 62-degree lie, a 36.75-inch steel length and 0.132 inches of offset.

Are the Ben Hogan Apex Plus irons forged?

Yes — the heads are forged from 1030 carbon steel in Fort Worth, with perimeter weighting added to a modified muscle-cavity so the club kept forged feel while enlarging the sweet spot.

When did the Ben Hogan Apex Plus come out?

It launched in 1999 as the perimeter-weighted companion to that year's Apex blade, and was replaced when the Apex Edge was announced in January 2002.

How forgiving is the Ben Hogan Apex Plus compared to the Apex blade?

Hogan gave it a slightly larger profile than the Apex blade and perimeter weighting for a larger heel-to-toe sweet spot, but it kept the same traditional lofts and flat Hogan lies, so it remains a players iron rather than a game-improvement club.

Finding a used set

The Ben Hogan Apex Plus ran from 1999 to 2002 and is no longer in production, so the used market is the only source. Search eBay, 2nd Swing, Golf Avenue and the PGA Tour Superstore trade-in listings, and check the loft and lie against the chart above before buying — sets this age have often been bent from standard.
