About the Ben Hogan Apex FTX

The Apex F•T•X ("Forged Transition Set") is a precision forged iron whose head design changes gradually from club to club rather than splitting the set into a fixed long-iron and short-iron style. Hogan's own catalog copy describes it as "a precision designed, shot-making instrument" built for "the most demanding players and shot-makers": the 1-iron through 7-iron carry the perimeter weighting of a cavity-back blade with longer blade lengths for playability and a muscle pad inside the cavity for feel, and from the 8-iron through the wedges the cavity disappears entirely into a true muscle-back blade, with the muscle pad enlarging as the loft increases for scoring-club control. Lofts are traditional (34-degree 7-iron, 46-degree Equalizer) with Hogan's characteristically flat lies (59 degrees at the 1-iron, 64 across the wedges) and a range running from a 16-degree 1-iron to a 60-degree lob wedge. Hogan's letter wedges are recorded here as PW (Equalizer, 46°), GW (FW wedge, 50°), SW (56°) and LW (60°). It was sold right hand 1-E/F/S/L and left hand 2-E/S, at $1,000 (steel) or $1,080 (graphite).

Frequently asked questions

What is the loft of a Ben Hogan Apex FTX 7-iron?

The Ben Hogan Apex FTX 7-iron has a loft of 34 degrees, with a 62-degree lie, a 36.75-inch length and 0.127 inches of offset.

What does F•T•X stand for on the Ben Hogan Apex FTX?

Forged Transition Set. The clubhead shape changes gradually club to club: the long irons carry cavity-back perimeter weighting for playability, while from the 8-iron down the cavity disappears into a true muscle-back blade for scoring-club feel and control.

Are Ben Hogan Apex FTX irons forged?

Yes. Every head, from the cavity-back long irons to the muscle-back short irons, is precision forged, and Hogan marketed the set at 'the most demanding players and shot-makers.'

What clubs came in a stock Ben Hogan Apex FTX set?

It was offered right hand from 1-iron through Equalizer, FW wedge, SW and LW, and left hand from 2-iron through Equalizer and SW, at $1,000 in steel or $1,080 in graphite.

Where it sits in the lineup

Introduced in 2004, the Ben Hogan Apex FTX is a current players set in the Ben Hogan range, with no replacement announced. New sets are built to the standard lofts, lies and lengths in the chart above, though a fitting can move any of them — so confirm the numbers on the order sheet. Used sets appear on eBay, 2nd Swing and Golf Avenue, and should be measured against this chart before buying.
