About the Ben Hogan Apex Edge Pro

The Apex Edge Pro was the players model of the Spalding-era Apex Edge line — a compact forged cavity-back with minimal offset that became one of the best-loved late Fort Worth Hogan irons and stayed in tour bags for years after production ended. Its spec page was live on benhogan.com by April 2002, alongside the more offset game-improvement Apex Edge announced that January. Lofts are the traditional Hogan ladder — a 34-degree 7-iron and 46-degree Equalizer pitching wedge — with lies half a degree flatter per club than the Apex Edge, and the range ran from a 16-degree 1-iron to a 60-degree lob wedge with Hogan Apex steel shafts as stock. Hogan's letter wedges are recorded here as PW (Equalizer, 46°), GW (F wedge, 50°), SW (56°) and LW (60°). Production wound down after Callaway acquired the Hogan brand in 2003; the multi-material Apex FTX of 2004 took its slot in the Callaway-era line.

Frequently asked questions

What is the loft of a Ben Hogan Apex Edge Pro 7-iron?

The Ben Hogan Apex Edge Pro 7-iron has a loft of 34 degrees, with a 62-degree lie and a 36.75-inch steel length.

What is the difference between the Ben Hogan Apex Edge Pro and Apex Edge?

Both share the traditional 16-to-60-degree Hogan loft ladder, but the Edge Pro is the compact players model with minimal offset and lies half a degree flatter per club, while the Apex Edge is the larger game-improvement version with much more offset.

When did the Ben Hogan Apex Edge Pro come out?

Its specification page was live on benhogan.com by April 2002, and spec databases file it as a 2002 model; it reached wider distribution in 2003, the year Callaway acquired the Hogan brand.

Are the Ben Hogan Apex Edge Pro irons forged?

Yes — they are forged carbon-steel cavity-backs from the Fort Worth era, prized for their feel, and they remained in professionals' bags for years after production ended.

Finding a used set

The Ben Hogan Apex Edge Pro ran from 2002 to 2004 and is no longer in production, so the used market is the only source. Search eBay, 2nd Swing, Golf Avenue and the PGA Tour Superstore trade-in listings, and check the loft and lie against the chart above before buying — sets this age have often been bent from standard.
