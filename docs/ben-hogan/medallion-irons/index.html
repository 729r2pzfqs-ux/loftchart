About the Ben Hogan Medallion

The Medallion was Hogan's cast, game-improvement iron of the late 1970s and early 1980s, offered alongside the company's forged blades for players who wanted more forgiveness. This 1982 revision — identifiable by its "BH" sunburst medallion badge and offset hosel, as distinct from the original 1978-81 version — is nearly identical in profile to its predecessor apart from the badge; collectors report no other detectable differences between the two. A confirmed 2-iron of 19.5 degrees loft and 39.5-inch length matches the loft (though not the length) of Hogan's contemporaneous forged Radial almost exactly; the rest of the ladder below extrapolates from that single confirmed point using the same step pattern documented across Hogan's Radial and Apex-era tables of the period, since no complete Medallion-specific factory sheet was located. Confidence is marked low for that reason. Hogan's letter wedge is recorded here as PW.

Frequently asked questions

What is the loft of a Ben Hogan Medallion 7-iron?

No complete factory sheet for the Medallion was located; extrapolating from the confirmed 19.5-degree 2-iron using the same step pattern as Hogan's contemporaneous Radial gives a 37-degree 7-iron, 62-degree lie and 37-inch length.

What is the difference between the 1978 and 1982 Ben Hogan Medallion?

Collectors report the two are distinguished mainly by the 'BH' sunburst medallion badge used on the 1982 version — no other construction difference has been reliably documented between the badge years.

Is the Ben Hogan Medallion forged or cast?

Cast. It used investment-cast stainless steel, unlike most of Hogan's better-known forged blades, aimed at golfers who wanted more forgiveness than the company's players irons offered.

What replaced the Ben Hogan Medallion?

The cast Radial, which carried a similar forgiveness-first approach with a distinctive wide radial sole, per GolfWRX collector sourcing.

Finding a used set

The Ben Hogan Medallion ran from 1982 to 1983 and is no longer in production, so the used market is the only source. Search eBay, 2nd Swing, Golf Avenue and the PGA Tour Superstore trade-in listings, and check the loft and lie against the chart above before buying — sets this age have often been bent from standard.
