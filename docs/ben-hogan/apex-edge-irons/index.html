About the Ben Hogan Apex Edge

The Apex Edge was the game-improvement model of the Spalding-era Ben Hogan Apex Edge line, announced on 25 January 2002 alongside the more compact Apex Edge Pro. It is a forged cavity-back whose muscle-cavity design launched the ball higher than the Pro, with markedly more offset (0.242 inches at the 3-iron tapering to 0.107 at the Equalizer wedge) and lies half a degree more upright than the Pro through the set. Lofts are the traditional Hogan ladder — a 34-degree 7-iron and 46-degree Equalizer pitching wedge — and the full range ran from a 16-degree 1-iron to a 60-degree lob wedge, sold with Apex Edge steel or graphite shafts and a Ben Hogan Tour Velvet grip. Hogan's letter wedges are recorded here as PW (Equalizer, 46°), GW (F wedge, 50°), SW (56°) and LW (60°). The Apex Edge CFT, on Hogan's site by early 2003 as Callaway acquired the brand, succeeded it.

Frequently asked questions

What is the loft of a Ben Hogan Apex Edge 7-iron?

The Ben Hogan Apex Edge 7-iron has a loft of 34 degrees, with a 62.5-degree lie, a 36.75-inch steel length and 0.182 inches of offset.

What is the difference between the Ben Hogan Apex Edge and Apex Edge Pro?

They share the same traditional 16-to-60-degree loft ladder, but the Apex Edge is the game-improvement model with substantially more offset and lies half a degree more upright per club, while the Apex Edge Pro is the compact low-offset players version.

When did the Ben Hogan Apex Edge come out?

The Spalding-era Ben Hogan company announced it on 25 January 2002. The Apex Edge CFT succeeded it in 2003 as Callaway took over the brand.

What does the E stamped on the Ben Hogan Apex Edge wedge mean?

It stands for Equalizer, Hogan's traditional name for the pitching wedge — 46 degrees in this set, recorded here as the PW.

Finding a used set

The Ben Hogan Apex Edge ran from 2002 to 2003 and is no longer in production, so the used market is the only source. Search eBay, 2nd Swing, Golf Avenue and the PGA Tour Superstore trade-in listings, and check the loft and lie against the chart above before buying — sets this age have often been bent from standard.
