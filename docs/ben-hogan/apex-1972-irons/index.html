About the Ben Hogan Apex (1972)

The 1972 Apex was the first Hogan iron to carry the Apex name — a compact forged muscle-back blade for low-handicap golfers, built in Fort Worth on essentially the head shape of the 1970 Bounce Sole and paired with Hogan's lightweight Apex steel shaft, whose numbered stiffnesses (Apex 2 through 5) the model shared its name with. It began a blade dynasty that ran through the Apex II, PC, Redline and later generations. Specs are pure early-seventies Hogan: very flat lies (56.5 degrees at the 2-iron), a 36.5-degree 7-iron, and a 51.5-degree Equalizer pitching wedge — the strongest-numbered wedge of any Apex generation, later Hogans settling at 50 degrees or less. The stock set ran 2-iron through Equalizer (recorded here as PW), with the Sure-Out and other wedges sold separately. The factory chart misprints the 2-iron steel length as 37.25 inches (duplicating the 6-iron); 39.25 is recorded here, the only value consistent with the chart's own half-inch ladder and with every other Hogan chart of the era.

Frequently asked questions

What is the loft of a 1972 Ben Hogan Apex 7-iron?

The original 1972 Ben Hogan Apex 7-iron has a loft of 36.5 degrees, with a 61.5-degree lie and a 36.75-inch steel length — roughly three clubs weaker than a typical modern 7-iron.

What is the Equalizer on the 1972 Ben Hogan Apex?

Equalizer was Hogan's name for the pitching wedge. On the 1972 Apex it is 51.5 degrees — the highest-lofted Equalizer of any Apex generation; by the early 1980s Hogan had moved it to 50 degrees.

Why is the 1972 Ben Hogan Apex famous?

It was the first iron to carry the Apex name — taken from Hogan's own lightweight Apex steel shaft — and it began the forged blade dynasty that ran through the Apex II, Apex PC and Redline into the 1990s. The head shape itself carried over from the 1970 Bounce Sole.

What clubs came in a 1972 Ben Hogan Apex set?

The stock set ran from the 2-iron through the Equalizer pitching wedge; sand wedges such as the Sure-Out were sold separately.

Finding a used set

The Ben Hogan Apex (1972) ran from 1972 to 1979 and is no longer in production, so the used market is the only source. Search eBay, 2nd Swing, Golf Avenue and the PGA Tour Superstore trade-in listings, and check the loft and lie against the chart above before buying — sets this age have often been bent from standard.
