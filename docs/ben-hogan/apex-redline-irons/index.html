About the Ben Hogan Apex Redline

Collectors call this 1988-89 Apex generation the "Redline" for the red paint line Hogan ran under the signature on the back of the muscle-back — a cosmetic marker distinguishing it from the otherwise similar-looking 1984-87 Apex PC that preceded it. Equip2Golf's own chronological Apex history describes it as "a beautiful blade with larger sole flange and lower center of gravity," and it is widely regarded among Hogan collectors as one of the purest true blades the company ever forged — narrow topline and sole, minimal offset, a cascading-step muscle-back and a pinned, chrome-forged finish. Hogan's own 1988-89 factory spec sheet for the Redline could not be located; the loft, lie and length ladder below is instead the verified table for its direct successor, the 1990-92 Apex BH Grind — the next generation of the same small-blade lineage — and should be read as a close approximation rather than a Redline-specific factory figure. Confidence is marked low for that reason.

Frequently asked questions

Why is the 1988-89 Ben Hogan Apex called the 'Redline'?

Collectors named it for the thin red paint line Hogan ran under the Ben Hogan signature on the back of the muscle-back, which distinguishes it from the visually similar 1984-87 Apex PC that came before it.

What is the loft of a Ben Hogan Apex Redline 7-iron?

No factory spec sheet for the 1988-89 Redline itself has been located; the best documented approximation, from the 1990 factory table for its direct successor the Apex BH Grind, gives the 7-iron a 37-degree loft, 62-degree lie and 36.75-inch length.

What replaced the Ben Hogan Apex Redline?

The Apex BH Grind, a small-blade generation with a square toe that Hogan's own history places immediately after the Redline, current from 1990 to 1992.

Are Ben Hogan Apex Redline irons forged?

Yes. Collectors and vintage-club dealers consistently describe them as forged, chrome-finished muscle-backs with a pinned shaft — among the purest true blades Hogan ever made.

Finding a used set

The Ben Hogan Apex Redline ran from 1988 to 1990 and is no longer in production, so the used market is the only source. Search eBay, 2nd Swing, Golf Avenue and the PGA Tour Superstore trade-in listings, and check the loft and lie against the chart above before buying — sets this age have often been bent from standard.
