About the Srixon Z 545

The Z 545 is the cavity back of Srixon's 2014 Z line, forged from 1020 carbon steel with a SUP10 steel face for a higher COR and a double-laser-milled face for spin. Srixon publishes leading- and trailing-edge bounce separately for this set rather than a single figure, which is how the Tour V.T. Sole is specified: leading-edge bounce climbs from 13 to 21 degrees through the set while trailing-edge bounce stays negative. Lofts are moderately strong, 31 degrees at the 7-iron.

Frequently asked questions

What is the loft of a Srixon Z 545 7-iron?

The Srixon Z 545 7-iron has a loft of 31 degrees, a lie of 62.0 degrees and a steel-shaft length of 37.00 inches.

When were Srixon Z 545 irons made?

The Z 545 was introduced in 2014 and replaced by the Z 565 in 2016.

Why does Srixon publish two bounce numbers for the Z 545?

Because the Tour V.T. Sole is not a single plane. Srixon adds bounce at the leading edge — 13 degrees at the 3-iron rising to 21 at the pitching wedge — and removes it at the trailing edge, which is where the negative figures come from.

What is SUP10 steel?

A high-strength spring steel Srixon uses for the face insert, stiff enough to be made thin so the face rebounds more at impact while the 1020 carbon body keeps the feel soft.

Finding a used set

The Srixon Z 545 ran from 2014 to 2016 and is no longer in production, so the used market is the only source. Search eBay, 2nd Swing, Golf Avenue and the PGA Tour Superstore trade-in listings, and check the loft and lie against the chart above before buying — sets this age have often been bent from standard.
