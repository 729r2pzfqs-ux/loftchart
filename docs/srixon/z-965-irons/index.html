About the Srixon Z 965

The Z 965 is the compact muscle back blade at the top of Srixon's 2016 three-model Z Series iron line, above the Z 765 muscle cavity and Z 565 cavity back. Srixon describes it as ultra-soft 1020 carbon steel combined with a new special heat treatment and precision forged, with the revised Tour V.T. sole and grooves five percent larger than the previous Z 945 generation; the longer irons carry a tungsten toe weight. Lofts are traditional — a 33-degree 7-iron and 46-degree pitching wedge — and sit one to three degrees weaker than the Z 765 through the middle of the set. The stock range is 3-iron through pitching wedge, right hand only and steel only. Srixon published this model's offset figures directly in inches, so no unit conversion was applied. The Tour V.T. sole is listed with separate leading- and trailing-edge bounce figures rather than a single bounce number, so bounce is omitted here. The line was revealed in August 2016 and reached retail on 16 September 2016, so it is often catalogued as a 2017 model.

Frequently asked questions

What is the loft of a Srixon Z 965 7-iron?

The Srixon Z 965 7-iron has a loft of 33 degrees, with a 62-degree lie and a 37-inch steel-shaft length.

What clubs come in a stock Srixon Z 965 set?

The range is 3-iron through pitching wedge, sold as 3-PW, 4-PW or 5-PW sets or as individual irons, in right hand only with steel shafts only.

What is the difference between the Srixon Z 965 and Z 765?

The Z 965 is the compact muscle back blade with the least offset in the line (0.096 inches at the 7-iron) and slightly weaker lofts — its 7-iron is 33 degrees against the Z 765's 32. The Z 765 is the larger muscle-cavity players model.

What replaced the Srixon Z 965?

Srixon's Z-Forged blade, announced in January 2019, took over the muscleback slot after the Z 585 and Z 785 replaced the Z 565 and Z 765 in 2018.

Finding a used set

The Srixon Z 965 ran from 2016 to 2019 and is no longer in production, so the used market is the only source. Search eBay, 2nd Swing, Golf Avenue and the PGA Tour Superstore trade-in listings, and check the loft and lie against the chart above before buying — sets this age have often been bent from standard.
