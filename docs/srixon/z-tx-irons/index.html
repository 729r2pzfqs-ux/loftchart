About the Srixon Z-TX

The Z-TX replaced the I-701 series as Srixon's flagship forged players iron, announced in Japan in late 2008 and internationally in 2009, and commonly sold as a 2010 model in the US. The head is forged from 1025 carbon steel with a proprietary heat treatment, adds five grams of tungsten in the heel and toe of the sole for stability, and uses a stepped sole so the longer blade plays like a shorter one through the turf. A thinner face centre expanded the sweet spot by a claimed 20 percent. Lofts stay traditional at 34 degrees on the 7-iron and 46 on the pitching wedge; Srixon's Japanese-market Z-925 series succeeded it in 2012 before the international Z 545/745 relaunch of 2014.

Frequently asked questions

What is the loft of a Srixon Z-TX 7-iron?

The Srixon Z-TX 7-iron has a loft of 34 degrees, a lie of 62 degrees and a length of 36.75 inches.

When did the Srixon Z-TX irons come out?

The Z-TX generation was announced in Japan in late 2008 and internationally in 2009, and was sold in the US mainly as a 2010 model.

What replaced the Srixon I-701 Tour?

The Z-TX — it carried the flagship forged players role forward with the same 1025 forging tradition, adding tungsten sole weights and a stepped sole.

Finding a used set

The Srixon Z-TX ran from 2009 to 2012 and is no longer in production, so the used market is the only source. Search eBay, 2nd Swing, Golf Avenue and the PGA Tour Superstore trade-in listings, and check the loft and lie against the chart above before buying — sets this age have often been bent from standard.
