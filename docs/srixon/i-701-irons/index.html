About the Srixon I-701

The standard I-701 launched alongside the I-701 Tour on 8 August 2007 as the more forgiving half of Srixon's forged players pair. Where the Tour is a one-piece 1025 forging, the standard I-701 is a two-piece forged head that welds a softer 1020 carbon steel face to a 1025 body and hollows a pocket cavity behind the face, dropping the centre of gravity lower and deeper for easier launch. Srixon confirmed to owners that it shared the Tour's lofts and lies — a traditional 34-degree 7-iron and 46-degree pitching wedge — and it was sold 3-PW with True Temper Dynamic Gold shafts until the Z-TX generation arrived.

Frequently asked questions

What is the loft of a Srixon I-701 7-iron?

The Srixon I-701 7-iron has a loft of 34 degrees, with a 61.5-degree lie and a 36.75-inch length — the same specification as the I-701 Tour.

What is the difference between the Srixon I-701 and I-701 Tour?

The standard I-701 is a two-piece forging with a softer 1020 face and a pocket cavity for a lower, deeper centre of gravity and easier launch; the Tour is a one-piece 1025 forging with a slightly higher centre of gravity for a more penetrating flight. Srixon confirmed the two shared lofts and lies.

When was the Srixon I-701 sold?

From its announcement on 8 August 2007 until the Z-TX generation replaced the 701 series in 2009.

Finding a used set

The Srixon I-701 ran from 2007 to 2009 and is no longer in production, so the used market is the only source. Search eBay, 2nd Swing, Golf Avenue and the PGA Tour Superstore trade-in listings, and check the loft and lie against the chart above before buying — sets this age have often been bent from standard.
