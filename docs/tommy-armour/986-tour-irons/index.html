About the Tommy Armour 986 Tour

The Silver Scot 986 Tour is a traditional forged blade from Tommy Armour's PGA Golf era, sold alongside the brand's persimmon-headed 986 Tour woods — collector listings for complete 986 Tour bags routinely pair the persimmon driver and fairway woods with this iron head. Player forum consensus (a long-running GolfWRX thread on the model) dates production to 1986 through 1995, with "PGA" stamped on the sole of the earliest heads, and describes a straight-blade profile with no offset — some owners note it may even run slightly onset — putting it closer in feel to a true muscle-back than the cavity-backed 845s Silver Scot the same company sold at the same time. Forum players comparing notes on the model's lofts, which are strong for a 1980s blade, matched them up against the contemporary Wilson Staff FG-17 and FG-51 tour blades rather than any factory chart, since Tommy Armour's own spec sheet for the model has not surfaced online. Sold 3-iron through pitching wedge with True Temper Dynamic Gold shafts in the standard tour stiff/regular flexes of the day.

Frequently asked questions

What is the loft of a Tommy Armour 986 Tour 7-iron?

No factory chart for the 986 Tour has surfaced online. Based on forum cross-referencing against the contemporary Wilson Staff FG-17/FG-51 blades, the 7-iron loft is approximately 37 degrees — treat this as an informed estimate rather than a verified manufacturer figure.

When was the Tommy Armour 986 Tour made?

Player forum consensus dates production to 1986 through 1995, under Tommy Armour's PGA Golf ownership, with the earliest heads stamped 'PGA' on the sole.

Is the Tommy Armour 986 Tour a blade or a cavity back?

A true forged one-piece blade with no offset (some owners describe it as slightly onset) — a traditional players' blade, unlike the cavity-backed, cast 845s Silver Scot the company sold at the same time.

Finding a used set

The Tommy Armour 986 Tour ran from 1986 to 1995 and is no longer in production, so the used market is the only source. Search eBay, 2nd Swing, Golf Avenue and the PGA Tour Superstore trade-in listings, and check the loft and lie against the chart above before buying — sets this age have often been bent from standard.
