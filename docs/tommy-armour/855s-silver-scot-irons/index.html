About the Tommy Armour 855s Silver Scot

The 855s Silver Scot followed the enormously successful 845s as Tommy Armour's flagship cavity-back, announced in late 1994 and sold through the mid-to-late 1990s. It kept the 845s formula — investment-cast stainless steel, perimeter weighting and progressive offset behind a relatively traditional profile — while enlarging the head slightly and varying the cavity depth through the set to keep the centre of gravity consistent. Lofts are identical to the 845s catalog spec (36-degree 7-iron), but the set was built a quarter inch longer per club and the full range again ran from a 1-iron through the 48-degree pitching wedge, a 52-degree gap wedge and a 56-degree sand wedge.

Frequently asked questions

What is the loft of a Tommy Armour 855s Silver Scot 7-iron?

The Tommy Armour 855s Silver Scot 7-iron has a loft of 36 degrees, a lie of 61.5 degrees and a length of 37.25 inches — the same loft as the 845s catalog spec but built a quarter inch longer.

What is the difference between the Tommy Armour 845s and 855s?

The 855s is the 845s's successor, announced in late 1994. It kept the same investment-cast, cavity-balanced concept and identical lofts, but used a slightly larger head with varied cavity depths for consistent centre-of-gravity placement, and each club was built a quarter inch longer.

What wedges came with the Tommy Armour 855s?

The set ran through a 48-degree pitching wedge, a 52-degree gap wedge and a 56-degree sand wedge, all at a 63-degree lie.

Production status

Introduced in 1994, the Tommy Armour 855s Silver Scot has no confirmed end date on file. It replaced the Tommy Armour 845s Silver Scot. LoftChart records a discontinuation year only where a source dates it, and none has been found for this set, so it may or may not still be in the Tommy Armour catalogue — check with Tommy Armour or a retailer before counting on a new set. Used sets appear on eBay, 2nd Swing and Golf Avenue, and should be measured against the chart above before buying, since irons are routinely bent from standard.
