About the Mizuno MP-9

The MP-9 was one of the first irons in Mizuno's Mizuno Pro MP line for the North American market, a Grain Flow Forged blade that ran from 1988 to 1994 and helped establish the MP name among tour players - Sandy Lyle famously played Mizuno blades of this family when he won the 1988 Masters. Japanese catalogues list it as sharing its specification with the domestic MS-9. Lofts are classic, with a 35-degree 7-iron and 47-degree pitching wedge, and the lie angles are flat by modern standards. The 1- and 2-iron lofts are documented but their lie and length were not published in the surviving table, so those fields are omitted.

Frequently asked questions

What is the loft of a Mizuno MP-9 7-iron?

The Mizuno MP-9 7-iron has a loft of 35 degrees, with a 60-degree lie and a 36.5-inch steel-shaft length in the published Japanese-market specification.

When was the Mizuno MP-9 made?

It ran from 1988 to 1994, making it one of the longest-lived early MP models, and it shared its specification with the Japanese-market Mizuno Pro MS-9.

What is the PS club in the Mizuno MP-9 set?

PS is Mizuno's pitching-sand wedge, a 52-degree gap wedge that sat between the 47-degree pitching wedge and the 54-degree sand wedge.

Finding a used set

The Mizuno MP-9 ran from 1988 to 1994 and is no longer in production, so the used market is the only source. Search eBay, 2nd Swing, Golf Avenue and the PGA Tour Superstore trade-in listings, and check the loft and lie against the chart above before buying — sets this age have often been bent from standard.
