About the Mizuno MS-1

The MS-1 opened Mizuno's MS ("Mizuno Staff") line in 1980 and became its longest-running blade, staying in the range for over a decade through many stamping iterations. It is a scotch-blade design with a square toe, a reverse muscle, and essentially zero bounce and zero offset — as traditional as forged irons come, aimed squarely at purists. Lofts run 21 degrees at the 3-iron to a 48-degree pitching wedge, the same ladder Mizuno kept for the MS-11 at the decade's end. Factory lengths were never published in surviving sources, so they are omitted. This model is sometimes dated to the early 1980s generally; vintage-Mizuno references place the launch at 1980.

Frequently asked questions

What is the loft of a Mizuno MS-1 7-iron?

The Mizuno MS-1 7-iron has a loft of 35 degrees and a lie of 61 degrees.

When were Mizuno MS-1 irons made?

The MS-1 launched the Mizuno Staff line in 1980 and stayed in the range for over a decade, with many small stamping changes along the way.

Are Mizuno MS-1 irons forgiving?

No — the MS-1 is a square-toe scotch blade with a reverse muscle and virtually no bounce or offset, one of the most traditional and demanding designs Mizuno has made.

Finding a used set

The Mizuno MS-1 ran from 1980 to 1990 and is no longer in production, so the used market is the only source. Search eBay, 2nd Swing, Golf Avenue and the PGA Tour Superstore trade-in listings, and check the loft and lie against the chart above before buying — sets this age have often been bent from standard.
