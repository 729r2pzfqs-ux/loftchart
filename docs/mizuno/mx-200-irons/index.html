About the Mizuno MX-200

The MX-200 is the last forged game-improvement iron of Mizuno's MX era, launched in 2009 and effectively replaced when the JPX-800 family arrived a year later. It is Grain Flow Forged in one piece with a deep milled pocket cavity and a Y-shaped reinforcement pad — Mizuno's Y-Tune design — that pushes the sweet area toward the toe. Lofts are moderate for the category, 33 degrees at the 7-iron and 45 at the pitching wedge, with a full offset ladder from 0.211 inches at the 3-iron. The typical retail set was 4-iron through gap wedge, with the 3-iron and a 55-degree sand wedge catalogued. Figures are the men's standard steel build.

Frequently asked questions

What is the loft of a Mizuno MX-200 7-iron?

The Mizuno MX-200 7-iron has a loft of 33 degrees, with a 61.5-degree lie and a 36.75-inch steel-shaft length.

Are the Mizuno MX-200 irons forged?

Yes. They are Grain Flow Forged in one piece from 1025E Pure Select mild carbon steel, with a milled pocket cavity behind the face.

What replaced the Mizuno MX-200?

Mizuno retired the MX line after the MX-200 and MX-300; the cast JPX-800 and forged JPX-800 Pro, launched in 2010, took over their slots in the range.

Finding a used set

The Mizuno MX-200 ran from 2009 to 2010 and is no longer in production, so the used market is the only source. Search eBay, 2nd Swing, Golf Avenue and the PGA Tour Superstore trade-in listings, and check the loft and lie against the chart above before buying — sets this age have often been bent from standard.
