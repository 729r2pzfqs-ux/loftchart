About the Mizuno MP-55

The MP-55 is a Japan-market players cavity that Mizuno announced on 25 August 2015 alongside the MP-5 and released that September, sold in Japan and Asia through 2019 but never brought to the US or Europe. It pairs a sharp, straight-neck MP profile with a strong-lofted, easy-launching design: a new V-shaped T-SLOT filling most of the cavity keeps the impact area thick for a solid forged feel, while the deepest centre of gravity and lowest sweet-spot height (19.5 mm at the 5-iron) of the MP range at the time produce a high, strong flight. Versus the MP-54 the toe was cut down to shorten the centre-of-gravity distance and help the head square. Lofts run 32 degrees at the 7-iron to a 46-degree pitching wedge — Mizuno's strongest MP lofts to that point. Frequently asked questions

What is the loft of a Mizuno MP-55 7-iron?

The Mizuno MP-55 7-iron has a loft of 32 degrees, a lie of 61.5 degrees and a steel-shaft length of 36.75 inches — the strongest MP lofts of its era, matching Mizuno's JPX EZ Forged.

Was the Mizuno MP-55 sold outside Japan?

No. The MP-55 was a Japan/Asia-market model sold from September 2015 through 2019; it reached other markets only as a grey import, which is why it is little known in the US and Europe.

What is the T-SLOT cavity in the Mizuno MP-55?

A V-shaped slot machined into the cavity that leaves the metal directly behind the impact area thick. It lets Mizuno push weight low and deep for launch while preserving the solid Grain Flow Forged feel.

How does the MP-55 differ from the MP-54?

The MP-55 is two degrees stronger at the 7-iron (32 against 34), with a lower sweet spot (19.5 mm against 20.5 at the 5-iron), a deeper centre of gravity and a shorter centre-of-gravity distance from the trimmed toe.

Finding a used set

The Mizuno MP-55 ran from 2015 to 2019 and is no longer in production, so the used market is the only source. Search eBay, 2nd Swing, Golf Avenue and the PGA Tour Superstore trade-in listings, and check the loft and lie against the chart above before buying — sets this age have often been bent from standard.
