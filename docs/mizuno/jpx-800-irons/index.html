About the Mizuno JPX-800

The JPX-800 is the iron that established Mizuno's JPX line as a distance range. The 4 to 7 irons use the Max COR Hot Metal face over a pocket cavity with an external power bar; the 8-iron through pitching wedge move to a deep pocket cavity, and the gap and sand wedges to a solid power-bar design. Lofts are strong for the period — 32 degrees at the 7-iron and 44 at the pitching wedge — and offset is heavy, 0.209 inches at the 4-iron. The set starts at a 4-iron and runs to a 55-degree sand wedge.

Frequently asked questions

What is the loft of a Mizuno JPX-800 7-iron?

The Mizuno JPX-800 7-iron has a loft of 32 degrees, a lie of 61.5 degrees and a steel-shaft length of 36.75 inches.

When were Mizuno JPX-800 irons made?

The JPX-800 was introduced in 2011 and replaced by the JPX-825 in 2013.

Does the Mizuno JPX-800 come with a 3-iron?

No. The published set starts at a 22-degree 4-iron and runs down to a 55-degree sand wedge.

Is the JPX-800 forged?

No. The JPX-800 is cast, with a Hot Metal stainless face. Mizuno's forged JPX models start with the JPX-800 Pro sold alongside it.

Finding a used set

The Mizuno JPX-800 ran from 2011 to 2013 and is no longer in production, so the used market is the only source. Search eBay, 2nd Swing, Golf Avenue and the PGA Tour Superstore trade-in listings, and check the loft and lie against the chart above before buying — sets this age have often been bent from standard.
