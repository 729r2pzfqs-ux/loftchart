About the Mizuno MX-300

The MX-300 is the MX line at its most player-oriented: a Grain Flow Forged undercut cavity built without the offset and sole width the rest of the MX range carried, so it reads as an MP blade at address while keeping a forgiving cavity behind the face. The sole is dual-cambered to cut turf drag. Lofts are traditional — 33 degrees at the 7-iron, 45 at the pitching wedge — and the set extends to a 50-degree gap wedge. Figures are the men's standard steel build.

Frequently asked questions

What is the loft of a Mizuno MX-300 7-iron?

The Mizuno MX-300 7-iron has a loft of 33 degrees, a lie of 61.5 degrees and a steel-shaft length of 36.75 inches.

When were Mizuno MX-300 irons made?

The MX-300 was released in 2010, the last of Mizuno's MX line before the JPX name took over that part of the range.

Is the Mizuno MX-300 forged?

Yes — Grain Flow Forged from 1025E Pure Select mild carbon steel, with an undercut cavity behind the face.

How much offset does a Mizuno MX-300 have?

0.171 inches at the 3-iron down to 0.102 at the pitching wedge, noticeably less than the MX-17 and MX-23 that preceded it.

Finding a used set

The Mizuno MX-300 ran from 2010 to 2012 and is no longer in production, so the used market is the only source. Search eBay, 2nd Swing, Golf Avenue and the PGA Tour Superstore trade-in listings, and check the loft and lie against the chart above before buying — sets this age have often been bent from standard.
