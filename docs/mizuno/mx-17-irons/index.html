About the Mizuno MX-17

The MX-17 is the forgiving end of Mizuno's mid-2000s MX range: a cast pocket cavity with a wide sole and heavy offset — 0.232 inches at the 3-iron, more than double what the MP blades of the period carried. Bounce is unusually low through the long and mid irons, from 0 degrees at the 3-iron to 3.5 at the pitching wedge, with a 10-degree sand wedge completing the published chart. Lofts are conventional for the era, 34 degrees at the 7-iron.

Frequently asked questions

What is the loft of a Mizuno MX-17 7-iron?

The Mizuno MX-17 7-iron has a loft of 34 degrees, a lie of 61.5 degrees and a length of 36.75 inches.

When were Mizuno MX-17 irons made?

The MX-17 dates from 2006, in the middle of Mizuno's MX game-improvement run.

How much offset does a Mizuno MX-17 have?

A lot for a Mizuno — 0.232 inches at the 3-iron, tapering to 0.134 at the pitching wedge. The contemporary MP blades carried roughly half that.

Why is the bounce on a Mizuno MX-17 so low?

The published chart runs from 0 degrees at the 3-iron to 3.5 at the pitching wedge. The wide sole does the work of keeping the head from digging, so Mizuno kept the measured bounce angle small.

Finding a used set

The Mizuno MX-17 ran from 2006 to 2008 and is no longer in production, so the used market is the only source. Search eBay, 2nd Swing, Golf Avenue and the PGA Tour Superstore trade-in listings, and check the loft and lie against the chart above before buying — sets this age have often been bent from standard.
