About the Mizuno Pro TN-93

The Mizuno Pro TN-93 was the third and final Tommy Nakajima signature iron, a Japan-market Grain Flow Forged head with a shallow half-cavity that keeps a blade profile. Unusually for the series it uses half-degree lofts through most of the set, with a 35.5-degree 7-iron and 48.5-degree pitching wedge, and the catalogue range ran from a 17-degree driving 1-iron to two sand wedges: a conventional 53-degree S2 and the distinctive high-toe 59-degree SW. Lengths below are the Japanese-market steel spec with a 37.5-inch 5-iron; some export listings quote 37.75 inches, and the discrepancy is noted rather than resolved.

Frequently asked questions

What is the loft of a Mizuno Pro TN-93 7-iron?

The Mizuno Pro TN-93 7-iron has a loft of 35.5 degrees, with a 60.5-degree lie and a 36.5-inch Japanese-spec steel length.

Why does the TN-93 have two sand wedges?

The catalogue offered a conventional rounded 53-degree wedge labelled S2 and a distinctive 59-degree high-toe, high-offset SW; players picked one or carried both.

Is the TN-93 a blade or a cavityback?

It is a near-blade: a shallow half-cavity is milled into an otherwise muscleback profile, keeping the look and flight of a blade with slightly more forgiveness than the TN-87 and TN-91 before it.

Finding a used set

The Mizuno Pro TN-93 ran from 1994 to 1998 and is no longer in production, so the used market is the only source. Search eBay, 2nd Swing, Golf Avenue and the PGA Tour Superstore trade-in listings, and check the loft and lie against the chart above before buying — sets this age have often been bent from standard.
