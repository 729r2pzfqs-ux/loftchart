About the Mizuno MX-900

The MX-900 was Mizuno's 2006 forged game-improvement iron, unusual for the brand in using hollow "Hemi Cog" forgings in the 3- through 7-irons with progressive tungsten sole weighting to drive the centre of gravity low and deep. The 8-iron through pitching wedge are forged from a softer steel as conventional mid-cavity heads with minimal offset, and internal heel and toe weights steady the head on off-centre strikes. Lofts are traditional — a 34-degree 7-iron and 46-degree pitching wedge — over a 3-PW set. The MX-950 hybrid-iron set took over the concept in 2008.

Frequently asked questions

What is the loft of a Mizuno MX-900 7-iron?

The Mizuno MX-900 7-iron has a loft of 34 degrees, with a 61.5-degree lie and a 36.75-inch steel-shaft length.

Are the Mizuno MX-900 irons forged?

Yes — unusually for a game-improvement iron, the whole set is forged: the 3- through 7-irons as hollow bodies with tungsten sole weights, and the 8-PW from a softer steel as conventional mid-cavity heads.

What clubs came in the Mizuno MX-900 set?

The set ran 3-iron through pitching wedge, with a 21-degree 3-iron and a 46-degree pitching wedge; no gap or sand wedge was catalogued.

Finding a used set

The Mizuno MX-900 ran from 2006 to 2008 and is no longer in production, so the used market is the only source. Search eBay, 2nd Swing, Golf Avenue and the PGA Tour Superstore trade-in listings, and check the loft and lie against the chart above before buying — sets this age have often been bent from standard.
