About the Mizuno MP-H5

The MP-H5 refined Mizuno's hollow-body MP iron for the 2015 line, launched in autumn 2014 alongside the MP-15. The hollow construction runs through the whole 1-PW range with a progressively thinner profile toward the short irons, keeping the high-launch long irons while sharpening the look and turf feel of the scoring clubs. Lofts stay traditional, with a 34-degree 7-iron and 46-degree pitching wedge, and offset is modest throughout. No direct MP-H successor followed; the hollow role passed to the MP-18 family's MMC Fli-Hi in 2017. Specs below are the standard steel build.

Frequently asked questions

What is the loft of a Mizuno MP-H5 7-iron?

The Mizuno MP-H5 7-iron has a loft of 34 degrees, with a 61.5-degree lie and a 36.75-inch steel-shaft length.

What is the difference between the MP-H4 and MP-H5?

Both are hollow-bodied with Grain Flow Forged faces. The MP-H5 extends the range to a 16-degree 1-iron, thins the profile progressively toward the short irons for a cleaner look and better turf interaction, and trims offset slightly in the scoring clubs.

Did Mizuno make an MP-H6?

No. The MP-H5 was the last MP-H model; when the MP-18 family arrived in 2017, the hollow long-iron role passed to the MP-18 MMC Fli-Hi.

Finding a used set

The Mizuno MP-H5 ran from 2014 to 2017 and is no longer in production, so the used market is the only source. Search eBay, 2nd Swing, Golf Avenue and the PGA Tour Superstore trade-in listings, and check the loft and lie against the chart above before buying — sets this age have often been bent from standard.
