About the Mizuno JPX-919 Forged

The JPX-919 Forged replaced the JPX-900 Forged and was the first Mizuno iron Grain Flow Forged in one piece from Chromoly 4120, a harder alloy that allowed a thinner reverse-milled face than the 1025E carbon steel used in the MP line. It was sold as a 4-iron through gap wedge set with a compact players' cavity head and modest offset of 0.161 inches falling to 0.093 inches. Lofts are about a degree stronger than the JPX-900 Forged through the mid irons, giving a 32-degree 7-iron and a 46-degree pitching wedge, which is still notably weaker than the matching JPX-919 Hot Metal. Lofts, lies and lengths listed are the men's standard steel-shaft build.

Frequently asked questions

What is the loft of a Mizuno JPX-919 Forged 7-iron?

The Mizuno JPX-919 Forged 7-iron has a loft of 32 degrees, with a 61.5-degree lie and a 37-inch steel-shaft length.

How do JPX-919 Forged lofts compare with JPX-919 Hot Metal?

The Forged is about two degrees weaker through the set. Its 7-iron is 32 degrees against 30 degrees for the Hot Metal, and its pitching wedge is 46 degrees against 45 degrees.

What clubs came in the stock JPX-919 Forged set?

The stock range was 4-iron through gap wedge. The pitching wedge is 46 degrees and the gap wedge 51 degrees; sand and lob wedges were not part of the set.

Finding a used set

The Mizuno JPX-919 Forged ran from 2018 to 2020 and is no longer in production, so the used market is the only source. Search eBay, 2nd Swing, Golf Avenue and the PGA Tour Superstore trade-in listings, and check the loft and lie against the chart above before buying — sets this age have often been bent from standard.
