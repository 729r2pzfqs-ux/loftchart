About the Mizuno MX-1000

The MX-1000 was the hot-face, maximum-distance flagship of Mizuno's MX range, launched in 2009 alongside the compact MX-300. Its hollow heads plasma-weld a thin ES230 maraging steel face to a stainless body for high COR, with Mizuno's Y-Tune weighting spreading the sweet area toward the toe, a 23.5 mm wide sole and strong lofts — 32 degrees at the 7-iron and a 44-degree pitching wedge. The catalogued set runs 4-iron through sand wedge, and the whole MX range gave way to the JPX-800 family from 2011.

Frequently asked questions

What is the loft of a Mizuno MX-1000 7-iron?

The Mizuno MX-1000 7-iron has a loft of 32 degrees, with a 61.5-degree lie and a 36.75-inch steel-shaft length — strong lofts for 2009, two degrees ahead of the MX-19.

Are the Mizuno MX-1000 irons forged?

No. The MX-1000 is a hollow-body iron that plasma-welds a thin ES230 maraging steel face to a stainless steel body for a hot, high-COR face — unusual for Mizuno, which is best known for forgings.

What clubs came in the Mizuno MX-1000 set?

The catalogued set ran 4-iron through sand wedge, with a 44-degree pitching wedge, 49-degree gap wedge and 55-degree sand wedge.

Finding a used set

The Mizuno MX-1000 ran from 2009 to 2011 and is no longer in production, so the used market is the only source. Search eBay, 2nd Swing, Golf Avenue and the PGA Tour Superstore trade-in listings, and check the loft and lie against the chart above before buying — sets this age have often been bent from standard.
