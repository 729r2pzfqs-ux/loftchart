About the Mizuno T-Zoid Pro

The T-Zoid Pro launched in 1996 as the players iron of T-ZOID, Mizuno's first world-unified brand, and reached US retail for the 1998 season. The Grain Flow Forged head is a modified cavityback about ten percent larger than a standard Mizuno forging, with the T-Zoid Power Bar bracing the cavity directly behind the sweet spot to add mass and stability. Lofts are traditional, with a 35-degree 7-iron and 47-degree pitching wedge, across a 1-iron-to-lob-wedge range that includes a 51-degree gap wedge Mizuno labelled FW. Frequently asked questions

What is the loft of a Mizuno T-Zoid Pro 7-iron?

The Mizuno T-Zoid Pro 7-iron has a loft of 35 degrees, with a 61-degree lie and a 37-inch steel-shaft length.

What is the T-Zoid Power Bar?

A bracing bar that spans the cavity directly behind the sweet spot, adding mass in the centre of the head for a more stable, solid feel while the surrounding cavity keeps perimeter forgiveness.

When did the Mizuno T-Zoid Pro come out?

Mizuno launched the T-ZOID brand globally in 1996 with the T-Zoid Pro as its forged players iron; it reached the US line for the 1998 season and was replaced by the T-Zoid Pro II in 2000.

Finding a used set

The Mizuno T-Zoid Pro ran from 1996 to 2000 and is no longer in production, so the used market is the only source. Search eBay, 2nd Swing, Golf Avenue and the PGA Tour Superstore trade-in listings, and check the loft and lie against the chart above before buying — sets this age have often been bent from standard.
