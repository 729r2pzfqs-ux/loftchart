About the Mizuno JPX-900 Tour

The JPX-900 Tour was the first Tour model in Mizuno's JPX line, a compact Grain Flow Forged cavity back in 1025E Pure Select mild carbon steel with a PowerFrame chassis stiffening the perimeter. Lofts are traditional — 34 degrees at the 7-iron and 46 at the pitching wedge, against 31 and 45 degrees on the JPX-900 Forged — with modest offset from 0.130 inches at the 3-iron to 0.102 at the pitching wedge. An unsponsored Brooks Koepka used it to win the 2017 US Open and the 2018 US Open and PGA Championship. The set ran 3-iron through pitching wedge. Lofts, lies and lengths listed are the men's standard steel-shaft build.

Frequently asked questions

What is the loft of a Mizuno JPX-900 Tour 7-iron?

The Mizuno JPX-900 Tour 7-iron has a loft of 34 degrees, with a 61.5-degree lie and a 36.75-inch steel-shaft length.

What is the difference between the JPX-900 Tour and the JPX-900 Forged?

Both are Grain Flow Forged, but the Tour is a smaller head with traditional lofts — 34 degrees at the 7-iron against the Forged model's 31 — and less offset, 0.114 inches at the 7-iron against 0.132.

Did Brooks Koepka play the Mizuno JPX-900 Tour?

Yes. Koepka, unsponsored at the time, won the 2017 US Open and the 2018 US Open and PGA Championship with JPX-900 Tour irons before moving to the JPX-919 Tour.

Finding a used set

The Mizuno JPX-900 Tour ran from 2016 to 2018 and is no longer in production, so the used market is the only source. Search eBay, 2nd Swing, Golf Avenue and the PGA Tour Superstore trade-in listings, and check the loft and lie against the chart above before buying — sets this age have often been bent from standard.
