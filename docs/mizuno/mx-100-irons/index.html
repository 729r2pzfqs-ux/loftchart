About the Mizuno MX-100

The MX-100 was Mizuno's super-game-improvement hybrid set, launched in October 2008 with Y-Tune technology that stretches the sweet area toward the toe. The 4- and 5-irons are hybrid-style heads with a dropped-down crown, the 6- and 7-irons use an ultra-deep pocket cavity, and the 8-iron through gap wedge are deep undercut cavities, all on a super-wide sole. The stock set ran 4-iron through a 50-degree gap wedge with an optional 55-degree sand wedge. The MX-1000 took over the maximum-distance flagship slot in 2009, and the JPX-800 family replaced the MX range from 2011.

Frequently asked questions

What is the loft of a Mizuno MX-100 7-iron?

The Mizuno MX-100 7-iron has a loft of 33 degrees, with a 61.5-degree lie and a 36.75-inch steel-shaft length.

What clubs came in the Mizuno MX-100 set?

The stock set ran 4-iron through a 50-degree gap wedge, with a 55-degree sand wedge available; the 4- and 5-irons are hybrid-style heads with a dropped-down crown.

Are the Mizuno MX-100 irons forged?

No — the MX-100 is an investment-cast super-game-improvement set; Mizuno reserved forging for the MX-25/MX-200 side of the range in this era.

Finding a used set

The Mizuno MX-100 ran from 2008 to 2011 and is no longer in production, so the used market is the only source. Search eBay, 2nd Swing, Golf Avenue and the PGA Tour Superstore trade-in listings, and check the loft and lie against the chart above before buying — sets this age have often been bent from standard.
