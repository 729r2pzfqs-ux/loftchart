About the Mizuno JPX-850

The JPX-850 was Mizuno's cast game-improvement iron for 2014 to 2016, the generation between the JPX-825 and the first Hot Metal, sold alongside the boron-steel JPX-850 Forged. It uses a deep pocket cavity in the long and mid irons with a thin high-strength face, heavy offset — 0.230 inches at the 4-iron and 0.180 at the 7-iron — and negative measured bounce in the 4- to 6-irons. Lofts match the JPX-825 it replaced, with a 32-degree 7-iron and a 45-degree pitching wedge, and the stock set is 4-iron through gap wedge. Lofts, lies and lengths listed are the men's standard steel-shaft specification.

Frequently asked questions

What is the loft of a Mizuno JPX-850 7-iron?

The Mizuno JPX-850 7-iron has a loft of 32 degrees, with a 61.5-degree lie and a 36.75-inch steel-shaft length.

What is the difference between the JPX-850 and the JPX-850 Forged?

The JPX-850 is cast stainless steel with a deep pocket cavity and heavy offset — 0.180 inches at the 7-iron against 0.130 for the Forged. Both share a 32-degree 7-iron and a 45-degree pitching wedge.

What clubs come in a JPX-850 set?

The stock range is 4-iron through gap wedge, with the pitching wedge at 45 degrees and the gap wedge at 50 degrees.

Finding a used set

The Mizuno JPX-850 ran from 2014 to 2016 and is no longer in production, so the used market is the only source. Search eBay, 2nd Swing, Golf Avenue and the PGA Tour Superstore trade-in listings, and check the loft and lie against the chart above before buying — sets this age have often been bent from standard.
