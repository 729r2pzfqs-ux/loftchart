About the Mizuno JPX-923 Hot Metal Pro

The JPX-923 Hot Metal Pro is the compact model of the 2022 Hot Metal trio, cast from the reformulated Nickel Chromoly 4335 that let Mizuno thin the face 8 percent, and sold alongside the standard Hot Metal and the weaker-lofted Hot Metal HL. It shares the standard model's lofts, lies and lengths — a 28.5-degree 7-iron and 42.5-degree pitching wedge — in a smaller head with far less offset, 0.131 inches at the 7-iron against the standard model's 0.196. The US stock set was 4-iron through pitching wedge, with a 48-degree gap wedge available. Mizuno launched the JPX-923 line in September 2022 and retailers catalogue it as the 2023 range, so year_introduced records the announcement year. Lofts, lies and lengths listed are the men's standard steel-shaft build.

Frequently asked questions

What is the loft of a Mizuno JPX-923 Hot Metal Pro 7-iron?

The Mizuno JPX-923 Hot Metal Pro 7-iron has a loft of 28.5 degrees, with a 61.5-degree lie and a 37-inch steel-shaft length — the same as the standard JPX-923 Hot Metal.

What is the difference between the JPX-923 Hot Metal Pro and the standard Hot Metal?

Lofts, lies and lengths are identical. The Pro is a more compact head with much less offset — 0.131 inches at the 7-iron against 0.196 — and its US stock set stopped at the pitching wedge, where the standard model ran to a lob wedge.

What clubs came in a JPX-923 Hot Metal Pro set?

The US stock set was 4-iron through pitching wedge, seven clubs, with a 48-degree gap wedge available to extend the set.

Finding a used set

The Mizuno JPX-923 Hot Metal Pro ran from 2022 to 2024 and is no longer in production, so the used market is the only source. Search eBay, 2nd Swing, Golf Avenue and the PGA Tour Superstore trade-in listings, and check the loft and lie against the chart above before buying — sets this age have often been bent from standard.
