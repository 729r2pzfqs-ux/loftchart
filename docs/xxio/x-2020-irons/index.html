About the XXIO X 2020

The 2020 XXIO X is the first generation of the letter-X line, launched alongside the XXIO Eleven in early 2020 as the better-player's version of the brand's ultralight formula — not to be confused with the 2017-2019 "XXIO X" that was simply the tenth numbered generation. A milled Speed Groove channel runs around the perimeter of the back of the face plate to add flex at impact, and the Weight Plus butt-end counterweights carry over from the numbered sets, so graphite builds still read C9 to D0 on the swing weight scale. Lofts match the outgoing XXIO 10 with a 29-degree 7-iron, but the build is a quarter inch shorter through the set and, unlike the Eleven, a steel shaft was offered. Sold right-handed only, 6-PW, 5-PW or 5-PW plus SW, with a 4-iron and wedges as singles.

Frequently asked questions

What is the loft of a 2020 XXIO X 7-iron?

The 2020 XXIO X 7-iron has a loft of 29 degrees, a lie of 62.0 degrees and a length of 37.0 inches.

How does the 2020 XXIO X differ from the XXIO Eleven?

The X keeps the Eleven's ultralight, counterbalanced concept but aims it at players with more speed: lofts are a degree stronger at the 7-iron, every club is built half an inch to a quarter inch shorter, and a steel shaft option was offered where the Eleven was graphite only in the US.

Is the 2020 XXIO X the same as the XXIO 10?

No. The tenth numbered generation (2017-2019) was badged 'XXIO X' with the Roman numeral in the US, but the 2020 X is a separate players-distance line that continued with the second-generation X in 2022.

What shafts did the 2020 XXIO X irons come with?

The stock graphite shaft was the Miyazaki AX-1, and the steel option was the Nippon N.S. Pro 920GH D.S.T. made for XXIO, both in regular and stiff flexes.

Finding a used set

The XXIO X 2020 ran from 2020 to 2022 and is no longer in production, so the used market is the only source. Search eBay, 2nd Swing, Golf Avenue and the PGA Tour Superstore trade-in listings, and check the loft and lie against the chart above before buying — sets this age have often been bent from standard.
