Fairway Wood Loft Chart

What degree each fairway wood is, which long iron or hybrid it replaces, and the stock lofts every major manufacturer currently builds.

A standard 3-wood is 15°, a 5-wood 18°–19° and a 7-wood 21°–22°. The retail range runs from 13.5° low-spin 3-woods to 9-woods at 26° and beyond, and most current families add an adjustable hosel worth a degree or two in each direction. Unlike irons, fairway woods are usually bought one slot at a time — which makes the loft, not the number on the sole, the thing to gap by.

Loft decides the job, not the number

The number on a fairway wood's sole is a naming convention; the loft is what sets launch, carry and landing angle. A 15° 3-wood is a distance club that needs speed to fly from the turf. From about 18° upward the equation flips: the ball climbs easily, lands steeply and stops — which is why a 7-wood from light rough is one of the easiest long shots in golf, and why the highest-lofted woods have returned to tour bags.

When gapping, work downward from the driver in roughly 3°–4° steps of loft and make each slot either a fairway wood or a hybrid, not both. The same loft in a wood flies higher and further; in a hybrid it is shorter, more iron-like and easier to control.

Frequently asked questions

What loft is a 3-wood?

15° is the standard, with stock options running from about 13.5° on low-spin tour heads to 16.5° on high-launch builds. Most adjustable hosels move that by a degree or so in each direction.

Should I carry a 3-wood or a 5-wood?

Most amateurs get more out of a 5-wood. A 3-wood's 15° face needs clubhead speed to launch from the turf, so it often becomes a tee-only club; an 18°–19° 5-wood flies almost as far, launches far more easily and holds greens. Carry both only if the gap to the driver justifies it.

What fairway wood is easiest to hit?

The highest-lofted one that fills the gap. Loft adds launch and backspin, which keeps mis-hits airborne and reduces curve — a 7-wood or 9-wood is markedly easier from the fairway and rough than a 3-wood, which is why high-lofted woods are back in fashion.

Can a fairway wood replace a driver?

Off the tee, yes — a 13.5°–16.5° 3-wood costs distance against a driver but its shorter shaft and extra loft make it straighter. Players who fight the driver often tee off with a 3-wood and give up less than they think.
