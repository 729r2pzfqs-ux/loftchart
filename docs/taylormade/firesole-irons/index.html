About the TaylorMade Firesole

The Firesole expanded TaylorMade's multi-material experiments into a full iron set: titanium long-iron heads carry tungsten sole weights for launch and forgiveness, the mid irons are steel with tungsten heel and toe weighting to stretch the sweet spot, and the short irons are nickel for softer feel. Lofts are strong for the era, with a 33-degree 7-iron and 45-degree pitching wedge across a 3-SW range plus a right-hand only 50-degree attack wedge. Firesole Tour and Firesole Forged variants were sold alongside it. Lengths below are the Rifle steel build from TaylorMade's own table; the Bubble graphite option played 0.5 inches longer per club.

Frequently asked questions

What is the loft of a TaylorMade Firesole 7-iron?

The Firesole 7-iron has a loft of 33 degrees, with a 62.5-degree lie and a 36.5-inch Rifle steel length (37 inches with the Bubble graphite shaft).

What materials are TaylorMade Firesole irons made of?

The set mixes three head materials: titanium long irons with tungsten sole weights for high launch, steel mid irons with tungsten heel-toe weights for stability, and nickel short irons for soft feel.

Were there other Firesole iron models?

Yes. TaylorMade also sold the more compact Firesole Tour and a Firesole Forged model for better players; the specs here are the standard game-improvement Firesole.

Finding a used set

The TaylorMade Firesole ran from 1999 to 2001 and is no longer in production, so the used market is the only source. Search eBay, 2nd Swing, Golf Avenue and the PGA Tour Superstore trade-in listings, and check the loft and lie against the chart above before buying — sets this age have often been bent from standard.
