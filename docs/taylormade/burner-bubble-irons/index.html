About the TaylorMade Burner Bubble

Introduced in 1993 as the Burner Midsize, this foam-filled cast stainless cavityback became the Burner Bubble mid-size iron when TaylorMade paired it with the copper-coloured Bubble graphite shaft from 1995, the pairing most 90s golfers know it by. The foam filling let weight move to the perimeter while damping vibration, and the set ran from a 16-degree 1-iron through a 56-degree sand wedge with a 33-degree 7-iron and 47-degree pitching wedge. It was succeeded in 1996 by the larger Burner Bubble Oversize. Specs below are TaylorMade's published men's table; women's builds ran an inch shorter with no 1-iron.

Frequently asked questions

What is the loft of a TaylorMade Burner Bubble 7-iron?

The Burner Bubble mid-size 7-iron has a loft of 33 degrees, with a 62-degree lie and a 36.5-inch men's length.

Are the Burner Bubble and Burner Midsize the same iron?

Yes. The head launched in 1993 as the foam-filled Burner Midsize; from 1995 TaylorMade sold it with the copper Bubble graphite shaft and marketed the combination as the Burner Bubble iron, before the Oversize head replaced it in 1996.

What clubs came in the Burner Bubble set?

TaylorMade published specs from a 16-degree 1-iron through a 56-degree sand wedge, including a 52-degree attack wedge. Women's sets ran an inch shorter and started at the 2-iron.

Finding a used set

The TaylorMade Burner Bubble ran from 1993 to 1996 and is no longer in production, so the used market is the only source. Search eBay, 2nd Swing, Golf Avenue and the PGA Tour Superstore trade-in listings, and check the loft and lie against the chart above before buying — sets this age have often been bent from standard.
