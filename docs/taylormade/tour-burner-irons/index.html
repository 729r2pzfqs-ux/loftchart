About the TaylorMade Tour Burner

The Tour Burner iron paired the 2008 Burner family's distance technology with a cleaner, more compact look than the oversized Burner XD. A thin 2.2-millimetre face with Inverted Cone Technology drives ball speed, while the multi-functional sole combines moderate width and a bevelled trailing edge for a low CG without digging. Lofts are strong for the period, with a 33-degree 7-iron and 46-degree pitching wedge, and the full range runs from a 2-iron to a lob wedge. Specs below are the men's standard steel-shaft build.

Frequently asked questions

What is the loft of a TaylorMade Tour Burner 7-iron?

The TaylorMade Tour Burner 7-iron has a loft of 33 degrees, with a 63-degree lie and a 36.75-inch steel-shaft length.

What is the difference between the Tour Burner and the Burner XD?

The Tour Burner is the smaller, cleaner-looking of the pair, with a medium topline, moderate offset and an all-steel head. The Burner XD uses an extra-large head with a titanium face, much more offset and lofts around three degrees stronger, at 30 degrees in the 7-iron against 33 for the Tour Burner.

Are the Tour Burner lofts strong?

Yes for 2008: the 33-degree 7-iron and 46-degree pitching wedge sit about two degrees stronger than the traditional player's lofts of the era, which TaylorMade paired with the thin high-COR face for distance.

Finding a used set

The TaylorMade Tour Burner ran from 2008 to 2009 and is no longer in production, so the used market is the only source. Search eBay, 2nd Swing, Golf Avenue and the PGA Tour Superstore trade-in listings, and check the loft and lie against the chart above before buying — sets this age have often been bent from standard.
