About the TaylorMade r7 TP

The r7 TP brought TaylorMade's Inverted Cone face technology to a compact tour-shaped head, replacing the forged rac CB TP as the better-player model above the standard r7. A shallow cavity keeps the centre of gravity high, offset is minimal with a flatter lie than the mainstream r7, and a dampening web of soft aluminium sits in the cavity for feel. Lofts are traditional, with a 35-degree 7-iron and 47-degree pitching wedge across a 2-SW range. Right-handed sets shipped from September 2006 and left-handed from October; TaylorMade's Irons Legacy timeline files the model under 2007, its marketing model year. Frequently asked questions

What is the loft of a TaylorMade r7 TP 7-iron?

The r7 TP 7-iron has a loft of 35 degrees, with a 61.5-degree lie and a 36.75-inch steel-shaft length.

What is the difference between the r7 and r7 TP irons?

The r7 TP uses a smaller tour-shaped head with a shallow cavity, minimal offset, flatter lie angles and traditional lofts (35-degree 7-iron versus 32 on the r7), aimed at better players who want workability while keeping Inverted Cone face technology.

What shafts did the r7 TP come with?

The stock shaft was True Temper Dynamic Gold steel in R3, S3 and X1 flexes; TaylorMade's re*ax TP 115 graphite was available through custom order only.

Finding a used set

The TaylorMade r7 TP ran from 2006 to 2009 and is no longer in production, so the used market is the only source. Search eBay, 2nd Swing, Golf Avenue and the PGA Tour Superstore trade-in listings, and check the loft and lie against the chart above before buying — sets this age have often been bent from standard.
