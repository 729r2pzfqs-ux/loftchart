About the TaylorMade RocketBallz Max

The RocketBallz Max was the maximum-forgiveness companion to the 2012 RocketBallz, and TaylorMade billed it as the longest iron the company had made to that point. Every head in the set is hollow and built with the pull-face metalwood construction the standard RBZ reserved for its 3- to 5-irons, with tungsten sole weighting for a low, centred CG and a draw-biased head structure. The stock set runs 4-iron to lob wedge with mixed stock shafts — Matrix Program graphite in the long irons, KBS Satin steel in the mid and short irons — which is why the catalog long-iron lengths are the graphite build.

Frequently asked questions

What is the loft of a TaylorMade RocketBallz Max 7-iron?

The TaylorMade RocketBallz Max 7-iron has a loft of 30 degrees, with a 63-degree lie and a 37-inch length.

What is the difference between the RocketBallz and RocketBallz Max irons?

The Max is the more forgiving, distance-first model: every head is hollow with pull-face metalwood construction and a draw-biased structure, where the standard RocketBallz uses that construction only in its 3- to 5-irons. Lofts differ slightly too — the Max runs a 30-degree 7-iron and 44-degree pitching wedge against 30.5 and 45.

What clubs came in the stock RocketBallz Max set?

TaylorMade specified the set from the 4-iron through the lob wedge: 4 to 9, a 44-degree pitching wedge, a 49-degree approach wedge, a 54-degree sand wedge and a 59-degree lob wedge.

Finding a used set

The TaylorMade RocketBallz Max ran from 2012 to 2012 and is no longer in production, so the used market is the only source. Search eBay, 2nd Swing, Golf Avenue and the PGA Tour Superstore trade-in listings, and check the loft and lie against the chart above before buying — sets this age have often been bent from standard.
