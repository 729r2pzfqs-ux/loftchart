About the TaylorMade SuperSteel

The SuperSteel iron carried TaylorMade's late-1990s SuperSteel branding into a thin-faced stainless game-improvement set, sold alongside the multi-material Firesole as the value-oriented distance option. It keeps the Burner LCG's strong loft structure - a 33-degree 7-iron and 45-degree pitching wedge - across a 2-iron-to-lob-wedge range, with slightly more upright lies in the long irons. TaylorMade's Irons Legacy timeline files it under 1999; equip2golf lists it under 2000, its main retail year. Lengths below are the steel build from TaylorMade's own table; the stock Bubble graphite shaft played 0.5 inches longer per club.

Frequently asked questions

What is the loft of a TaylorMade SuperSteel 7-iron?

The SuperSteel 7-iron has a loft of 33 degrees, with a 62.5-degree lie and a 36.5-inch steel-shaft length (37 inches with the Bubble graphite shaft).

When did the TaylorMade SuperSteel irons come out?

TaylorMade's own timeline dates them to 1999, with 2000 as the main retail year. They sat below the multi-material Firesole in the range and were replaced by the 300-series irons in 2001.

What clubs came in the SuperSteel set?

The range ran from a 19-degree 2-iron through a 60-degree lob wedge, including a 50-degree attack wedge and 55-degree sand wedge. Women's sets used the L-60 Bubble shaft an inch shorter and skipped the 2-iron.

Finding a used set

The TaylorMade SuperSteel ran from 1999 to 2001 and is no longer in production, so the used market is the only source. Search eBay, 2nd Swing, Golf Avenue and the PGA Tour Superstore trade-in listings, and check the loft and lie against the chart above before buying — sets this age have often been bent from standard.
