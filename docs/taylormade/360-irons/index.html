About the TaylorMade 360

The 360 was the oversize game-improvement member of TaylorMade's 300 series, the largest of the three head sizes offered alongside the 300 Forged and 320. It carries the most offset in the range - 7.5 millimetres in the 2-iron - with lofts about a degree stronger than the 320 through the middle of the set, a 34-degree 7-iron and a 46-degree pitching wedge. The stock set ran 3-PW with a 2-iron and AW, SW and LW available. Lengths below are the published graphite-shaft build; steel shafts played 0.25 inches shorter except in the wedges, which were the same length in both.

Frequently asked questions

What is the loft of a TaylorMade 360 7-iron?

The TaylorMade 360 7-iron has a loft of 34 degrees, with a 62.5-degree lie and a 37-inch graphite-shaft length (36.75 inches in steel).

Is the TaylorMade 360 a forgiving iron?

Yes. It was the largest and most offset head in the 2001 300 series, aimed at golfers who wanted maximum forgiveness; the 320 offered a mid-size head and the 300 Forged a compact players head.

What wedges were available for the TaylorMade 360?

Beyond the 46-degree pitching wedge, TaylorMade offered a 51-degree attack wedge, a 55-degree sand wedge and a 60-degree lob wedge in the same head style.

Finding a used set

The TaylorMade 360 ran from 2001 to 2003 and is no longer in production, so the used market is the only source. Search eBay, 2nd Swing, Golf Avenue and the PGA Tour Superstore trade-in listings, and check the loft and lie against the chart above before buying — sets this age have often been bent from standard.
