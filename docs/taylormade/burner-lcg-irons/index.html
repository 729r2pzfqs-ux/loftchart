About the TaylorMade Burner LCG

The Burner LCG - Low Center of Gravity - followed the Burner Oversize as TaylorMade's Bubble-shaft game-improvement iron of the late 1990s, sold in the same copper-and-black cosmetic family. Sole weighting drops the centre of gravity for higher flight from the same strong lofts as the Oversize, with a 33-degree 7-iron and 45-degree pitching wedge across a 2-iron-to-lob-wedge range. TaylorMade's Irons Legacy timeline files it under 1997 as the Burner Bubble LCG; equip2golf lists it under 1998, its main retail year. Lengths below are the stock Bubble graphite build; the Royal Precision Rifle steel option played 0.5 inches shorter.

Frequently asked questions

What is the loft of a TaylorMade Burner LCG 7-iron?

The Burner LCG 7-iron has a loft of 33 degrees, with a 62.5-degree lie and a 37-inch length with the stock Bubble graphite shaft (36.5 inches with the Rifle steel shaft).

What does LCG stand for on TaylorMade Burner irons?

LCG stands for Low Center of Gravity: sole-concentrated weighting designed to launch the ball higher than the Burner Oversize it replaced, while keeping the same strong lofts.

What shafts did the Burner LCG come with?

The stock graphite shaft was TaylorMade's copper-coloured Burner Bubble, with the Royal Precision Rifle stepless steel shaft as the alternative, half an inch shorter per club.

Finding a used set

The TaylorMade Burner LCG ran from 1997 to 1999 and is no longer in production, so the used market is the only source. Search eBay, 2nd Swing, Golf Avenue and the PGA Tour Superstore trade-in listings, and check the loft and lie against the chart above before buying — sets this age have often been bent from standard.
