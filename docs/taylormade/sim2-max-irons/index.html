About the TaylorMade SIM2 Max

The SIM2 Max replaced the SIM Max in 2021 and swapped the Speed Bridge for the Cap Back design, a stiffening structure moved back and away from the topline so the face and the ECHO Damping System could be enlarged. The face is thinner than the SIM Max and uses progressive Inverted Cone Technology, with a Thru-Slot Speed Pocket in the sole of the longer irons. Lofts and lengths carry over unchanged from the SIM Max, with a 28.5-degree 7-iron, a 43.5-degree pitching wedge and TaylorMade's progressive length set measured on the ground plane. Frequently asked questions

What is the loft of a TaylorMade SIM2 Max 7-iron?

The TaylorMade SIM2 Max 7-iron has a loft of 28.5 degrees, with a 63-degree lie and a 37.25-inch steel-shaft length.

Did the lofts change between the SIM Max and SIM2 Max irons?

No. The two sets share the same loft, lie, length and offset through the whole set, from a 19-degree 4-iron to a 59-degree lob wedge. The changes are structural, chiefly the move from the Speed Bridge to the Cap Back design.

What is the Cap Back design on the SIM2 Max irons?

The Cap Back is a stiffening structure set back from the topline rather than bridging it, as the earlier Speed Bridge did. Moving it back allowed a larger, thinner face and a bigger ECHO Damping insert while keeping the sound and feel under control.

What is the pitching wedge loft on the SIM2 Max irons?

The SIM2 Max pitching wedge is 43.5 degrees, with a 49-degree approach wedge, a 54-degree sand wedge and a 59-degree lob wedge available in the set.

Finding a used set

The TaylorMade SIM2 Max ran from 2021 to 2022 and is no longer in production, so the used market is the only source. Search eBay, 2nd Swing, Golf Avenue and the PGA Tour Superstore trade-in listings, and check the loft and lie against the chart above before buying — sets this age have often been bent from standard.
