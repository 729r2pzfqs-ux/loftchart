About the TaylorMade SLDR

The SLDR iron was TaylorMade's attempt at a classic-looking head with the Speed Pocket behind it — a smaller, cleaner shape than the SpeedBlade of the previous year, with a ThruSlot cut in the sole. Lofts sit between the two camps: a 32-degree 7-iron, a degree and a half weaker than the SpeedBlade but stronger than a traditional players set, and the set runs to a 56-degree sand wedge.

Frequently asked questions

What is the loft of a TaylorMade SLDR 7-iron?

The TaylorMade SLDR 7-iron has a loft of 32 degrees, a lie of 62.5 degrees and a length of 36.75 inches.

When were TaylorMade SLDR irons made?

They were announced in 2014 and reached retail through TaylorMade Tuned Performance Centers on 6 June 2014.

How do SLDR iron lofts compare with the SpeedBlade?

The SLDR is weaker throughout — 32 degrees at the 7-iron against the SpeedBlade's 30.5, and 19 at the 3-iron against 17 — in a smaller head.

Where it sits in the lineup

Introduced in 2014, the TaylorMade SLDR is a current players distance set in the TaylorMade range, with no replacement announced. New sets are built to the standard lofts, lies and lengths in the chart above, though a fitting can move any of them — so confirm the numbers on the order sheet. Used sets appear on eBay, 2nd Swing and Golf Avenue, and should be measured against this chart before buying.
