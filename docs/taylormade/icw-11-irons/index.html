About the TaylorMade ICW 11

The ICW 11 - Inner Cavity Weighting - was one of a pair of early-90s TaylorMade irons, launched alongside the better-player ICW 5 with the ICW 11 aimed at the everyday player. A hidden internal cavity concentrates mass behind the strike zone while the compact head keeps a thin topline, and offset progresses through the set from long irons to wedges. Lofts are classic for the era, with a 34.5-degree 7-iron and 48-degree pitching wedge across a 1-iron-to-sand-wedge range. TaylorMade's own timeline files the model under 1991, though used-market listings often date the ICW family a few years earlier. Specs below are the men's build; women's lengths ran an inch shorter with no 1-iron.

Frequently asked questions

What is the loft of a TaylorMade ICW 11 7-iron?

The ICW 11 7-iron has a loft of 34.5 degrees, with a 62-degree lie and a 36.5-inch men's length.

What does ICW stand for on TaylorMade irons?

Inner Cavity Weighting: a hollow internal cavity that moves mass to the perimeter and behind the strike zone while the back of the head stays clean, an early alternative to the visible cavityback.

What is the difference between the ICW 5 and ICW 11?

TaylorMade developed them as a pair: the ICW 5 was the compact model for better players, while the ICW 11 carried more progressive offset and forgiveness for the everyday player. Both share the 1-iron-to-sand-wedge range.

Finding a used set

The TaylorMade ICW 11 ran from 1991 to 1993 and is no longer in production, so the used market is the only source. Search eBay, 2nd Swing, Golf Avenue and the PGA Tour Superstore trade-in listings, and check the loft and lie against the chart above before buying — sets this age have often been bent from standard.
