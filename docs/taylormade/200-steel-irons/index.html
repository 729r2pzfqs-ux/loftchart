About the TaylorMade 200 Steel

The 200 Steel (officially the 200 Series irons) was TaylorMade's mainstream game-improvement iron announced in late 2001 and sold as the 2002 line, applying the research behind the premium 300 Series to a longer-bladed, perimeter-weighted head. Cast from 431 stainless steel, it uses a low, deep centre of gravity for high launch, an Impact Pad in the cavity to preserve ball speed and soften feel, moderate offset, U grooves and radiused soles. Lofts are moderate for the era, with a 33-degree 7-iron and 45-degree pitching wedge over a 2-iron to sand wedge range. Specs below are the men's standard steel-shaft build; a lightweight TaylorMade Lite shaft option was also offered, in S, R and M flexes.

Frequently asked questions

What is the loft of a TaylorMade 200 Steel 7-iron?

The TaylorMade 200 Steel 7-iron has a loft of 33 degrees, with a 62.5-degree lie and a 36.75-inch steel-shaft length.

When did the TaylorMade 200 Steel irons come out?

TaylorMade announced the 200 Series in late 2001 and sold it as its 2002 game-improvement line, despite the model often being misdated earlier. It was replaced by the rac OS when the rac family launched for 2003.

What is the Impact Pad in the 200 Steel irons?

It is an insert placed in the cavity back that TaylorMade said maintains ball velocity on off-centre hits while producing a more solid sound and feel from the cast 431 stainless head.

Finding a used set

The TaylorMade 200 Steel ran from 2001 to 2003 and is no longer in production, so the used market is the only source. Search eBay, 2nd Swing, Golf Avenue and the PGA Tour Superstore trade-in listings, and check the loft and lie against the chart above before buying — sets this age have often been bent from standard.
