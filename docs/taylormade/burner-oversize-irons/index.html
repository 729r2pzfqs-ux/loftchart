About the TaylorMade Burner Oversize

The Burner Oversize (also sold as the Burner Bubble Oversize) was TaylorMade's hugely popular mid-1990s game-improvement iron, built around the copper-coloured Bubble graphite shaft that concentrates weight below the grip for faster swings. The oversized cast cavityback head carries progressive offset, a high moment of inertia and a low centre of gravity, and the set runs 2-iron through lob wedge with a 33-degree 7-iron and 45-degree pitching wedge. Specs below are the men's standard build with the stock Bubble graphite shaft, per TaylorMade's own vintage archive; the Rifle steel option played 0.5 inches shorter per club.

Stock shafts

Frequently asked questions

What is the loft of a TaylorMade Burner Oversize 7-iron?

The TaylorMade Burner Oversize 7-iron has a loft of 33 degrees, with a 62.5-degree lie and a 37-inch length with the stock Bubble graphite shaft (36.5 inches in steel).

What is the Bubble shaft on the Burner Oversize irons?

The Bubble is TaylorMade's signature 1990s graphite shaft, with a widened, hollow section just below the grip that moves weight up the shaft to reduce deflection at impact and make the club easier to swing fast. Flexes ran from L-60 for slower swings to TS-100 tour stiff, with a Rifle stepless steel shaft as the alternative.

What clubs were available in the Burner Oversize set?

The range ran from a 19-degree 2-iron through a 60-degree lob wedge, including a 50-degree attack wedge and 55-degree sand wedge, so a full 12 clubs were offered.

Finding a used set

The TaylorMade Burner Oversize ran from 1996 to 1998 and is no longer in production, so the used market is the only source. Search eBay, 2nd Swing, Golf Avenue and the PGA Tour Superstore trade-in listings, and check the loft and lie against the chart above before buying — sets this age have often been bent from standard.
