About the TaylorMade PSi

The PSi was TaylorMade's better-player iron of the M1 era, announced alongside that driver in September 2015. It runs a progressive build: tungsten weighting and a Speed Pocket in the long irons, forged heads from the 8-iron down, and face slots cut vertically into the toe and heel to keep ball speed up on mishits. Lofts are a degree and a half stronger than a traditional players set, with a 31.5-degree 7-iron, and the set runs all the way to a 55-degree sand wedge.

Frequently asked questions

What is the loft of a TaylorMade PSi 7-iron?

The TaylorMade PSi 7-iron has a loft of 31.5 degrees, a lie of 63.0 degrees and a length of 36.75 inches.

When were TaylorMade PSi irons made?

TaylorMade announced them on 9 September 2015 alongside the M1 driver and they reached retail on 6 November 2015.

Are TaylorMade PSi irons forged?

Only in part. The 8-iron through sand wedge are forged; the long irons are cast with tungsten weighting and a Speed Pocket in the sole.

Where it sits in the lineup

Introduced in 2015, the TaylorMade PSi is a current players distance set in the TaylorMade range, with no replacement announced. New sets are built to the standard lofts, lies and lengths in the chart above, though a fitting can move any of them — so confirm the numbers on the order sheet. Used sets appear on eBay, 2nd Swing and Golf Avenue, and should be measured against this chart before buying.
