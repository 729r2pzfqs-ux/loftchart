About the TaylorMade AeroBurner

The AeroBurner revived the Burner name for TaylorMade's 2015 distance iron, taking over the slot the SpeedBlade had occupied since late 2013. The head is large and keeps a near-constant size through the set rather than shrinking toward the short irons, with a low centre of gravity, a Speed Pocket in the 4 to 7 irons and strong lofts — a 19-degree 4-iron and a 29.5-degree 7-iron. A weaker-lofted AeroBurner HL companion model was sold alongside it. The M2 took over as TaylorMade's distance iron in 2016. Specs below are the men's standard steel-shaft build.

Frequently asked questions

What is the loft of a TaylorMade AeroBurner 7-iron?

The TaylorMade AeroBurner 7-iron has a loft of 29.5 degrees, with a 63-degree lie and a 37-inch steel-shaft length.

What is the difference between the AeroBurner and AeroBurner HL irons?

The HL is the higher-launching version with weaker lofts — a 20-degree 4-iron and 43.5-degree pitching wedge against 19 and 43 degrees on the standard model — while sharing the same large head style and offsets.

Do the AeroBurner irons have a Speed Pocket?

Yes, the 4- through 7-irons carry TaylorMade's Speed Pocket slot in the sole to help ball speed on shots struck low on the face. The short irons and wedges use a conventional sole.

What replaced the AeroBurner irons?

The TaylorMade M2, announced in 2016, took over as the company's mainstream distance iron.

Finding a used set

The TaylorMade AeroBurner ran from 2015 to 2016 and is no longer in production, so the used market is the only source. Search eBay, 2nd Swing, Golf Avenue and the PGA Tour Superstore trade-in listings, and check the loft and lie against the chart above before buying — sets this age have often been bent from standard.
