About the TaylorMade Qi10

The Qi10 Rescue family launched on 9 January 2024 alongside the Qi10 woods. The core Qi10 Rescue is a bonded five-loft line from 19° to 33° built to flight like an iron replacement; the Qi10 Max Rescue is the bonded high-MOI head, a degree weaker at each club number and out to a 35° 7-Rescue; and the compact Qi10 Tour Rescue is the player's head with the family's only adjustable loft sleeve, sold in 17°, 19.5° and 22°. The Qi35 Rescues replaced the family in January 2025.

Head variants

The heads this family is sold in, with each one’s stock loft options.

Qi10 Rescue19°, 22°, 25°, 29°, 33°
The core bonded Rescue in five lofts, with a D3 swing weight and lengths from 40.75 to 38.75 inches. Mid flight with a neutral-to-slight draw bias.
Qi10 Max Rescue20°, 23°, 27°, 31°, 35°
The bonded forgiveness head with the highest MOI in the family, lofted one degree weaker than the core Rescue at each club number and sold out to a 35° 7-Rescue. Lies run 59-61°, lengths 40.75-38.75 inches.
Qi10 Tour Rescue17°, 19.5°, 22°
The compact tour head with the family's only 3° loft sleeve, in 17°, 19.5° and 22° at a D2 swing weight — built to be flighted down and shaped like a long iron.

Frequently asked questions

What lofts do the TaylorMade Qi10 Rescues come in?

The core Qi10 Rescue comes in 19, 22, 25, 29 and 33 degrees; the Qi10 Max Rescue in 20, 23, 27, 31 and 35; and the Qi10 Tour Rescue in 17, 19.5 and 22.

Is the TaylorMade Qi10 Rescue adjustable?

Only the Qi10 Tour Rescue, which has a 3 degree loft sleeve (1.5 degrees each way). The core Qi10 Rescue and the Qi10 Max Rescue are bonded and play at their stamped loft.

What is the difference between the Qi10 Rescue and Qi10 Max Rescue?

The Max is the forgiveness model: higher MOI, a touch more draw bias, lofts one degree weaker at each club number, and an extra loft at the weak end — a 35 degree 7-Rescue the core line does not offer.

Buying one

TaylorMade has since introduced the TaylorMade Qi35 above it in the range, so the Qi10 is at the end of its retail life rather than fully retired — closeout stock still turns up at retailers, and hybrids one generation back are the sweet spot of the used market. Before buying used, check the stamped loft against the chart above and ask which hosel setting it is in — an adjustable hybrid may be set a degree or more away from the number on the sole, and sleeve adapters are brand- and generation-specific.
