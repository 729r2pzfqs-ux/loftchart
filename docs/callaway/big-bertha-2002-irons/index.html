About the Callaway Big Bertha (2002)

The 2002 Big Bertha irons revived the Big Bertha nameplate as Callaway's forgiving mainstream set alongside the Steelhead X-16, eight years after the original 1994 Big Bertha irons. The ladder is traditional — a 34 degree 7-iron and 46 degree pitching wedge — on the upright Callaway lie plane of the era topping out at 65 degrees, and the pitching wedge runs a quarter inch longer than the X-16's at 35.75 inches. The catalogued range ran 1-iron to lob wedge including a 50 degree W wedge, recorded here as AW.

Frequently asked questions

What is the loft of a 2002 Callaway Big Bertha 7-iron?

The 2002 Callaway Big Bertha 7-iron is 34 degrees, with a 62.5 degree lie and a 37 inch standard length.

How do the 2002 Big Bertha lofts compare with the 1994 original?

The 2002 set is a degree stronger through the middle — a 34 degree 7-iron against 33 — and its 42 degree 9-iron matches the original, with both topping out at upright 65 degree wedge lies.

What wedges came with the 2002 Callaway Big Bertha irons?

A 46 degree pitching wedge, a 50 degree W wedge (an approach wedge), a 54 degree sand wedge and a 58 degree lob wedge.

Finding a used set

The Callaway Big Bertha (2002) ran from 2002 to 2004 and is no longer in production, so the used market is the only source. Search eBay, 2nd Swing, Golf Avenue and the PGA Tour Superstore trade-in listings, and check the loft and lie against the chart above before buying — sets this age have often been bent from standard.
