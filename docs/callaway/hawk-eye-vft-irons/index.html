About the Callaway Hawk Eye VFT

The Hawk Eye VFT irons were the last of Callaway's all-titanium iron ranges, adding the Variable Face Thickness face of the VFT metalwoods to the tungsten-weighted titanium Hawk Eye head. The loft ladder is identical to the original Hawk Eye — 33 degree 7-iron, 46.5 degree pitching wedge — but the lies return to Callaway's classic 59.2/59.9/60.6/61.3 ladder in the long irons. The multi-material premium slot passed to the Big Bertha Fusion irons in 2004.

Frequently asked questions

What is the loft of a Callaway Hawk Eye VFT 7-iron?

The Hawk Eye VFT 7-iron has a loft of 33 degrees, with a 62.5 degree lie and a 37 inch standard length.

What does VFT mean on the Callaway Hawk Eye VFT irons?

Variable Face Thickness — the titanium face is thicker in the centre and thinner toward the edges, enlarging the effective hitting area, a technology carried over from Callaway's VFT metalwoods.

What is the difference between the Hawk Eye and Hawk Eye VFT irons?

Both are tungsten-weighted titanium heads with the same loft ladder; the VFT adds the variable-thickness face and moves the long-iron lies back to Callaway's classic ladder (59.9 degrees at the 3-iron versus 60 on the original Hawk Eye).

Finding a used set

The Callaway Hawk Eye VFT ran from 2002 to 2004 and is no longer in production, so the used market is the only source. Search eBay, 2nd Swing, Golf Avenue and the PGA Tour Superstore trade-in listings, and check the loft and lie against the chart above before buying — sets this age have often been bent from standard.
