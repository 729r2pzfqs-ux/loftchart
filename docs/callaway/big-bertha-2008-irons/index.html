About the Callaway Big Bertha (2008)

The 2008 Big Bertha irons were the last iron run under the plain Big Bertha name before Callaway rested it until 2014. It is a wide-sole, heavily-offset cavity back, and the first Callaway set offered with i-brid long irons — hybrid-bodied replacements for the 3-, 4- and 5-irons that could be mixed into the set at fitting. Lofts are moderate for the period with a 33-degree 7-iron and 45-degree pitching wedge, and the lie ladder runs notably upright, reaching 65 degrees in the wedges. Frequently asked questions

What is the loft of a 2008 Callaway Big Bertha 7-iron?

The 2008 Big Bertha 7-iron has a loft of 33 degrees, with a 62.5-degree lie and a 37-inch steel-shaft length.

What are Callaway i-brid clubs?

The 2008 Big Bertha set was the first Callaway iron line offered with i-brids — hybrid-bodied clubs matching the 3-, 4- and 5-iron lofts (20, 23 and 26 degrees) that could replace the standard long irons in the set.

When were the 2008 Big Bertha irons made?

They launched in 2008 and were the last classic Big Bertha iron run; Callaway's Diablo Edge replaced them in 2010, and the Big Bertha iron name returned in 2014.

Finding a used set

The Callaway Big Bertha (2008) ran from 2008 to 2010 and is no longer in production, so the used market is the only source. Search eBay, 2nd Swing, Golf Avenue and the PGA Tour Superstore trade-in listings, and check the loft and lie against the chart above before buying — sets this age have often been bent from standard.
