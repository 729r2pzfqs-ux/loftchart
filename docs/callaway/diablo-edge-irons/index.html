About the Callaway Diablo Edge

The Diablo Edge was Callaway's maximum-forgiveness iron of 2010, announced on 11 January 2010 and at retail from 15 January, positioned below the X-Series and sold alongside the compact Diablo Forged. It combines VFT face technology with a 360-degree undercut channel and a low, deep centre of gravity that puts the sweet spot in line with where most amateurs actually strike the face, over a wide Solid Impact sole. Lofts are a degree stronger than the X-24 Hot through the middle of the set — a 32 degree 7-iron and a 44 degree pitching wedge — in a deep 3-LW chart. Specs below are the standard men's steel-shaft numbers; the graphite build is swingweighted lighter at D0 to D3.

Frequently asked questions

What is the loft of a Callaway Diablo Edge 7-iron?

The Callaway Diablo Edge 7-iron has a loft of 32 degrees, with a 62.5 degree lie and a 37 inch standard steel length.

What is the loft of a Callaway Diablo Edge pitching wedge?

The Diablo Edge pitching wedge is 44 degrees, with a 49 degree A-wedge, a 54 degree sand wedge and a 59 degree lob wedge.

When were Callaway Diablo Edge irons released?

Callaway announced them on 11 January 2010 and they reached retail on 15 January 2010, at $599 for a steel-shafted set.

Are the Diablo Edge irons forged?

No. The Diablo Edge is a cast stainless steel game-improvement iron; the forged model sold alongside it was the separate Diablo Forged.

Finding a used set

The Callaway Diablo Edge ran from 2010 to 2011 and is no longer in production, so the used market is the only source. Search eBay, 2nd Swing, Golf Avenue and the PGA Tour Superstore trade-in listings, and check the loft and lie against the chart above before buying — sets this age have often been bent from standard.
