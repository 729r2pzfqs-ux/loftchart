About the Callaway XR Pro

The XR Pro is the compact, better-player version of the 2015 XR, and the first time Callaway put its cup-face technology into a players iron — a forged 360 Face Cup on the same two-piece, dual-heat-treated body as the standard XR, with an Internal Standing Wave positioning mass low and central. The head is smaller than the standard XR, with a thinner top line and a narrower sole, and lofts run around a degree weaker, with a 31 degree 7-iron and a 45 degree pitching wedge against the XR's 30 and 44. Heads ran from a 3-iron to a 50 degree A-wedge, and the XR Pro reached retail in the spring of 2015, a little after the standard model. Specs below are the standard men's steel-shaft numbers.

Frequently asked questions

What is the loft of a Callaway XR Pro 7-iron?

The Callaway XR Pro 7-iron has a loft of 31 degrees, a lie of 62.0 degrees and a length of 37 inches.

What is the difference between the Callaway XR and the XR Pro?

The XR Pro is a smaller head with a thinner top line, a narrower sole and much less offset — 0.165 inches at the 3-iron against the XR's 0.270 — and its lofts are around a degree weaker, with a 31 degree 7-iron against 30. It was also the first Callaway players iron with a forged cup face.

What shaft did the Callaway XR Pro come with?

The stock steel shaft was the KBS Tour-V 90, a lighter tour-profile shaft, in regular and stiff flexes.

Finding a used set

The Callaway XR Pro ran from 2015 to 2017 and is no longer in production, so the used market is the only source. Search eBay, 2nd Swing, Golf Avenue and the PGA Tour Superstore trade-in listings, and check the loft and lie against the chart above before buying — sets this age have often been bent from standard.
