About the Callaway S2H2

The S2H2 irons were Callaway's first modern iron line and the debut of the Short, Straight, Hollow Hosel that defined the company's designs for a decade: the shaft runs through a hollowed bore to the sole, and the weight saved from the neck is moved into the head. Introduced in 1988 and sold into the early 1990s, they carry a notably flat lie ladder for a Callaway (61 degrees at the 7-iron) and traditional lofts with a 34 degree 7-iron and 48 degree pitching wedge. The Big Bertha irons replaced them in 1994.

Frequently asked questions

What is the loft of a Callaway S2H2 7-iron?

The Callaway S2H2 7-iron has a loft of 34 degrees, with a 61 degree lie and a 37 inch standard length.

What does S2H2 stand for on Callaway irons?

Short, Straight, Hollow Hosel — the shortened hollow hosel lets the shaft run to the sole and moves the weight saved from the neck into the clubhead. The design debuted on these irons in 1988 and was carried into Callaway's woods and the Big Bertha era.

When were the Callaway S2H2 irons made?

Callaway introduced the S2H2 irons in 1988, its first iron line under the Callaway Golf name, and sold them until the original Big Bertha irons arrived in 1994.

Finding a used set

The Callaway S2H2 ran from 1988 to 1994 and is no longer in production, so the used market is the only source. Search eBay, 2nd Swing, Golf Avenue and the PGA Tour Superstore trade-in listings, and check the loft and lie against the chart above before buying — sets this age have often been bent from standard.
