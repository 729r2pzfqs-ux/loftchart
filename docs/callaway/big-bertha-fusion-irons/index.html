About the Callaway Big Bertha Fusion

The Big Bertha Fusion irons of 2004 brought the multi-material Fusion Technology of Callaway's FT drivers into an iron, bonding a thin titanium face cup to a body with tungsten weighting and a polymer core to push weight to the extreme perimeter. They were marketed as some of the most forgiving irons Callaway had built and sat above the steel Big Bertha line in the range. Lofts are traditional with a 33 degree 7-iron and a 46 degree pitching wedge; the sources publish a single standard length ladder without labelling the shaft material.

Frequently asked questions

What is the loft of a Callaway Big Bertha Fusion 7-iron?

The Big Bertha Fusion 7-iron has a loft of 33 degrees, with a 62.5 degree lie and a 37 inch standard length.

What made the Big Bertha Fusion irons different from other Callaway irons?

They used Fusion multi-material construction — a titanium face cup bonded to a body with tungsten weighting and a polymer core — instead of a one-piece cast stainless head, pushing far more weight to the perimeter.

What is the pitching wedge loft on the Big Bertha Fusion irons?

The Fusion pitching wedge is 46 degrees, followed by a 51 degree approach wedge, 56 degree sand wedge and 60 degree lob wedge.

Finding a used set

The Callaway Big Bertha Fusion ran from 2004 to 2006 and is no longer in production, so the used market is the only source. Search eBay, 2nd Swing, Golf Avenue and the PGA Tour Superstore trade-in listings, and check the loft and lie against the chart above before buying — sets this age have often been bent from standard.
