About the Callaway X-24 Hot

The X-24 Hot followed the X-22 as the game-improvement iron in Callaway's long-running X-Series, announced on 4 May 2010 and at retail from 28 May 2010. The face uses Callaway's VFT technology and thins progressively from sole to topline to lower the centre of gravity, over a 360-degree undercut channel, and the head carries a dark Stealth PVD finish. Callaway pitched it as the longest X-Series iron to date, with a 31 degree 7-iron and a 45 degree pitching wedge in a 4-SW set. Specs below are the standard men's steel-shaft numbers; the graphite build is a quarter inch longer at every club and swingweighted lighter.

Frequently asked questions

What is the loft of a Callaway X-24 Hot 7-iron?

The Callaway X-24 Hot 7-iron has a loft of 31 degrees, with a 62 degree lie and a 37 inch standard steel length.

What is the loft of a Callaway X-24 Hot pitching wedge?

The X-24 Hot pitching wedge is 45 degrees, with a 50 degree A-wedge and a 55 degree sand wedge.

Does the Callaway X-24 Hot come in a 3-iron?

No. Callaway's published chart runs from the 22 degree 4-iron to the 55 degree sand wedge, and the set was sold 4-SW.

How do X-24 Hot lofts compare with the X-22?

The X-24 Hot is two degrees stronger at the 7-iron — 31 degrees against the X-22's 33 — while both sets share a 45 degree pitching wedge, so the strengthening is concentrated in the middle irons.

Finding a used set

The Callaway X-24 Hot ran from 2010 to 2011 and is no longer in production, so the used market is the only source. Search eBay, 2nd Swing, Golf Avenue and the PGA Tour Superstore trade-in listings, and check the loft and lie against the chart above before buying — sets this age have often been bent from standard.
