About the Callaway X Hot Pro

The X Hot Pro is the compact, better-player version of the X Hot, sold alongside the standard model from early 2013. Instead of the standard head's deep undercut cavity it uses what Callaway called a J frame — a single piece of steel with the undercut only in the sole, stiffening the top of the face while letting the bottom flex — plus a face with 21 closely spaced grooves for more spin from the rough. Lofts are around one degree weaker than the standard X Hot through the scoring clubs, with a 31 degree 7-iron and a 45 degree pitching wedge, and heads ran from a 3-iron to a 50 degree A-wedge. Callaway announced the X Hot line on 17 December 2012 with retail from 25 January 2013, so the model is commonly known as the 2013 X Hot Pro even though the year below is the announcement year per this database's convention. Frequently asked questions

What is the loft of a Callaway X Hot Pro 7-iron?

The Callaway X Hot Pro 7-iron has a loft of 31 degrees, a lie of 62.0 degrees and a length of 37 inches.

What is the difference between the Callaway X Hot and the X Hot Pro?

The X Hot Pro is a smaller head with lofts around one degree weaker — a 31 degree 7-iron against 30, and a 45 degree pitching wedge against 44. It also swaps the deep undercut cavity for a stiffer J frame construction and comes stock with a heavier Project X 95 Flighted shaft.

What year are Callaway X Hot Pro irons?

Callaway announced the X Hot line on 17 December 2012 and it reached retail on 25 January 2013, which is why the X Hot Pro is usually called a 2013 model.

Finding a used set

The Callaway X Hot Pro ran from 2012 to 2013 and is no longer in production, so the used market is the only source. Search eBay, 2nd Swing, Golf Avenue and the PGA Tour Superstore trade-in listings, and check the loft and lie against the chart above before buying — sets this age have often been bent from standard.
