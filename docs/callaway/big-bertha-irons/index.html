About the Callaway Big Bertha

The 1994 Big Bertha irons are the set that carried Callaway's metalwood success into the iron market, built around the S2H2 short, straight, hollow hosel that moved weight out of the neck and into the head. Lofts are traditional for the era with a 33 degree 7-iron and a 46.5 degree pitching wedge, and the lie ladder is strikingly upright by modern standards, reaching 65 degrees through the wedges. Callaway published a single standard length per club rather than separate steel and graphite figures. The catalogued range ran from a 1-iron to a lob wedge, though 3-PW was the normal retail set.

Frequently asked questions

What is the loft of a Callaway Big Bertha 7-iron?

The 1994 Callaway Big Bertha 7-iron is 33 degrees, with a 62.5 degree lie and a 37 inch standard length.

What is the pitching wedge loft on the 1994 Big Bertha?

The Big Bertha pitching wedge is 46.5 degrees, followed by a 50.8 degree approach wedge, a 55 degree sand wedge and a 60 degree lob wedge.

Why are the Big Bertha lie angles so upright?

The 1994 Big Bertha runs 65 degrees at the wedges and 62.5 degrees at the 7-iron, several degrees more upright than a modern set. That was normal for Callaway irons of the period and is worth checking before buying a used set.

Finding a used set

The Callaway Big Bertha ran from 1994 to 1996 and is no longer in production, so the used market is the only source. Search eBay, 2nd Swing, Golf Avenue and the PGA Tour Superstore trade-in listings, and check the loft and lie against the chart above before buying — sets this age have often been bent from standard.
