About the Callaway Big Bertha (2004)

The 2004 Big Bertha irons replaced the 2002 set and softened the ladder by a degree at every club — a 33 degree 7-iron against 34 and a 45 degree pitching wedge against 46 — while keeping the upright 65 degree wedge lies and the 35.75 inch pitching wedge of the 2002 model. Every iron head has the same wide sole width to keep the centre of gravity low and deep across the set. The catalogued range ran 2-iron to lob wedge including a 50 degree W wedge, recorded here as AW, and the recorded swing weights are the steel build with graphite two to three points lighter.

Frequently asked questions

What is the loft of a 2004 Callaway Big Bertha 7-iron?

The 2004 Callaway Big Bertha 7-iron is 33 degrees, with a 62.5 degree lie and a 37 inch standard steel length.

What changed between the 2002 and 2004 Big Bertha irons?

The 2004 set is a degree weaker at every club — a 33 degree 7-iron against 34 and a 17 degree 2-iron against 18 — and Callaway published a bounce ladder for it, running from 0 degrees at the 2-iron to 12 at the sand wedge.

What wedges came with the 2004 Callaway Big Bertha irons?

A 45 degree pitching wedge, a 50 degree W wedge (an approach wedge), a 55 degree sand wedge and a 60 degree lob wedge.

Finding a used set

The Callaway Big Bertha (2004) ran from 2004 to 2006 and is no longer in production, so the used market is the only source. Search eBay, 2nd Swing, Golf Avenue and the PGA Tour Superstore trade-in listings, and check the loft and lie against the chart above before buying — sets this age have often been bent from standard.
