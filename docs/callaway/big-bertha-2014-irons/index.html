About the Callaway Big Bertha (2014)

The 2014 Big Bertha irons revived Callaway's most famous name for a distance-focused game-improvement set, announced in September 2014 and sold as the 2015 line above the X2 Hot. They were among the first mainstream hollow-bodied irons, pairing the 360 Face Cup — with the weld line moved behind the face so the whole face flexes — with an internal standing wave ridge that positions the centre of gravity for launch. Lofts are moderate by modern distance-iron standards, with a 30 degree 7-iron and a 44 degree pitching wedge, and Callaway published bounce for every club. The chart runs from the 4-iron to a 54 degree sand wedge. Specs below are the standard men's steel-shaft numbers.

Frequently asked questions

What is the loft of a 2014 Callaway Big Bertha 7-iron?

The 2014 Big Bertha 7-iron has a loft of 30 degrees, with a 62.5 degree lie and a 37.25 inch standard steel length.

What is the loft of the 2014 Big Bertha pitching wedge?

The pitching wedge is 44 degrees, followed by a 49 degree A-wedge and a 54 degree sand wedge.

Were the 2014 Big Bertha irons hollow?

Yes. They were among the first mainstream hollow-bodied game-improvement irons, using a 360 Face Cup with the weld line moved behind the face and an internal standing wave ridge to control the centre of gravity.

Are these the same as the 1994 Big Bertha irons?

No. The 2014 set revived the name two decades later with a completely different hollow, cup-faced construction. The 1994 original was a conventional cast cavity-back built around the S2H2 hosel.

Finding a used set

The Callaway Big Bertha (2014) ran from 2014 to 2016 and is no longer in production, so the used market is the only source. Search eBay, 2nd Swing, Golf Avenue and the PGA Tour Superstore trade-in listings, and check the loft and lie against the chart above before buying — sets this age have often been bent from standard.
