About the Callaway X-16 Pro Series

The Steelhead X-16 Pro Series is the better-player version of the 2002 X-16, with a more compact, blade-like head, a thinner topline and less offset for shot-shaping and a lower flight. Callaway kept the published ladder identical to the standard X-16 — the same 34 degree 7-iron, the same upright 65 degree wedge lies and the same lengths — so the differences are in the head, not the chart. The catalogued range ran 1-iron to lob wedge, with 3-PW the usual retail set on Constant Weight steel shafts.

Frequently asked questions

What is the loft of a Callaway X-16 Pro Series 7-iron?

The Callaway X-16 Pro Series 7-iron is 34 degrees, with a 62.5 degree lie and a 37 inch standard steel length — the same published numbers as the standard X-16.

What is the difference between the X-16 and the X-16 Pro Series?

The Pro Series has a more compact, blade-like head with a thinner topline and less offset for better players. The published loft, lie and length ladder is identical to the standard X-16.

Are Callaway X-16 Pro Series irons forgiving?

Less so than the standard X-16 — the smaller head trades forgiveness for workability and a lower flight, which is why Callaway aimed the Pro Series at low-to-mid handicaps.

Finding a used set

The Callaway X-16 Pro Series ran from 2002 to 2004 and is no longer in production, so the used market is the only source. Search eBay, 2nd Swing, Golf Avenue and the PGA Tour Superstore trade-in listings, and check the loft and lie against the chart above before buying — sets this age have often been bent from standard.
