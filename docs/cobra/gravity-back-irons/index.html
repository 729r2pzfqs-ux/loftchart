About the Cobra Gravity Back

The Gravity Back was Cobra's completely new iron for 1999, the first design of the Acushnet era to move on from the King Cobra oversize family. A distinctive Backweight bar behind the head positions the centre of gravity low and back in a lower-profile cast 17-4 stainless head, raising inertia and launch for golfers of all skill levels. The line ran from a 16-degree 1-iron to a 60-degree lob wedge, with the stock set sold 3-PW in steel or graphite, in right and left hand. Lofts are traditional — 33 degrees at the 7-iron and 46 at the pitching wedge — and every club is built to a D2 swing weight. Cobra published an offset column whose values run backwards relative to normal convention (negative in the long irons, increasing toward the wedges), so, as with the other Cobra records of this era, it is not reproduced here. It was followed by the CXI in 2000. Note that some listings date the Gravity Back 2000; Cobra's own 1999 page introduces it as new.

Frequently asked questions

What is the loft of a Cobra Gravity Back 7-iron?

The Cobra Gravity Back 7-iron has a loft of 33 degrees, a lie of 62 degrees and a length of 37.25 inches.

What year are the Cobra Gravity Back irons?

Cobra introduced the Gravity Back in 1999 — its own site presented the line as new that year — though some retailers list it as a 2000 model. The CXI replaced it in 2000.

What makes the Cobra Gravity Back irons unusual?

A Backweight bar behind the low-profile cast head shifts the centre of gravity low and rearward, which raises inertia and helps the ball launch higher — an early take on the extreme rear-weighting later common in game-improvement irons.

Finding a used set

The Cobra Gravity Back ran from 1999 to 2000 and is no longer in production, so the used market is the only source. Search eBay, 2nd Swing, Golf Avenue and the PGA Tour Superstore trade-in listings, and check the loft and lie against the chart above before buying — sets this age have often been bent from standard.
