About the Cobra FP

The King Cobra FP — Cobra's shorthand for forgiveness and playability — was the mid-handicap iron in Cobra's 2006 line, sitting between the maximum-forgiveness S9 and the Carbon CB players model. The 431 stainless cavity back carries a urethane sole insert for feel and Cobra's Sweet Zone face, and it earned a Golf Digest Hot List gold medal in 2007. Lofts are a degree or so weaker than the S9's, with a 33-degree 7-iron and 45-degree pitching wedge. Sets were sold in men's, women's and seniors' versions; the men's specs are below, identical in steel and graphite lengths. A revised "2008 FP" with slightly stronger short-iron lofts and much less offset replaced it for 2008.

Frequently asked questions

What is the loft of a Cobra FP 7-iron?

The 2006 King Cobra FP 7-iron has a loft of 33 degrees, a lie of 63 degrees and a length of 37 inches. The revised 2008 FP strengthened the 7-iron to 32.5 degrees.

What does FP stand for in the Cobra FP irons?

Cobra used FP to denote forgiveness and playability — the model sat between the maximum-forgiveness S9 and the Carbon CB players iron in the 2006 line.

What clubs were available in the Cobra FP line?

Heads ran from the 3-iron through a 60-degree lob wedge, including 50-degree gap and 55-degree sand wedges, in men's, women's and seniors' versions.

Finding a used set

The Cobra FP ran from 2006 to 2008 and is no longer in production, so the used market is the only source. Search eBay, 2nd Swing, Golf Avenue and the PGA Tour Superstore trade-in listings, and check the loft and lie against the chart above before buying — sets this age have often been bent from standard.
