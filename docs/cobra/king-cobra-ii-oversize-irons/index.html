About the Cobra King Cobra II Oversize

The King Cobra II is the 1996 follow-up to the King Cobra Oversize, the best-selling oversize iron of its era, and is usually catalogued as the King Cobra II Oversize to distinguish it from the 1997 King Cobra II Tour. Cobra announced it on 31 July 1996 and shipped the first fifteen thousand sets that September. Its Integrated Quad System combined four features — heel-side weighting on the hosel, a shaped "Hump" shaft with an oversize stabilising tip, a dual-purpose sole and an oversize cavity back — all aimed at putting more hits on the sweet spot. Heads are cast 17-4 stainless steel with a blast finish and black and gold accents. The stock set ran 1, 3-9, PW, GW and SW, with a 2-iron and lob wedge also catalogued; Cobra published a single length column alongside its Hump graphite, Hump steel and Apollo steel shaft options. Lofts are traditional, with a 32-degree 7-iron and a 45.5-degree pitching wedge.

Frequently asked questions

What is the loft of a King Cobra II 7-iron?

The King Cobra II Oversize 7-iron has a loft of 32 degrees, a lie of 62 degrees and a length of 37 inches.

What year did the King Cobra II irons come out?

Cobra announced the King Cobra II on 31 July 1996 and shipped the first sets to shops that September. The better-player King Cobra II Tour followed in January 1997.

What is the Integrated Quad System on King Cobra II irons?

It combines heel-side weighting, a shaped graphite shaft with an oversize stabilising tip, a dual-purpose sole and an oversize cavity back, all designed to help golfers strike the sweet spot more often.

Finding a used set

The Cobra King Cobra II Oversize ran from 1996 to 1999 and is no longer in production, so the used market is the only source. Search eBay, 2nd Swing, Golf Avenue and the PGA Tour Superstore trade-in listings, and check the loft and lie against the chart above before buying — sets this age have often been bent from standard.
