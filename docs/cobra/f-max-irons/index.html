About the Cobra F-Max

The F-Max was the first model in Cobra's F-Max family of lightweight super-game-improvement irons for golfers with moderate swing speeds, introduced in 2017. The cast cavity backs use a progressive construction — thinner 17-4 stainless steel in the 4 through 7 irons for speed, softer 431 stainless in the 8-iron through sand wedge for feel — under a nickel chrome plating, with weight positioned back and heelward for straighter, draw-biased flight. Lofts are weak by design at 31.5 degrees for the 7-iron and 45 degrees for the pitching wedge, offset tapers from 6.3 mm at the 4-iron to 0.8 mm at the sand wedge, and the steel build swings very light at C9 through the irons. The stock men's steel set was seven clubs, 5-PW plus gap wedge. The F-Max Superlite, announced on 12 September 2018, replaced it.

Frequently asked questions

What is the loft of a Cobra F-Max 7-iron?

The Cobra F-Max 7-iron has a loft of 31.5 degrees, a lie of 62.5 degrees and a steel-shaft length of 37.25 inches.

When were the original Cobra F-Max irons released?

In 2017. Cobra replaced them with the F-Max Superlite, announced on 12 September 2018, and the F-Max Airspeed followed in 2019.

Who were the Cobra F-Max irons designed for?

Golfers with moderate swing speeds. They combine weak lofts, generous offset, back-and-heel weighting for a draw and a very light build — around C9 swing weight with the stock 100-110 gram steel shaft.

How is the F-Max head construction progressive?

The 4 through 7 irons are cast from thinner, stronger 17-4 stainless steel for ball speed, while the 8-iron through sand wedge use softer 431 stainless steel for feel, all under a nickel chrome plating.

Finding a used set

The Cobra F-Max ran from 2017 to 2018 and is no longer in production, so the used market is the only source. Search eBay, 2nd Swing, Golf Avenue and the PGA Tour Superstore trade-in listings, and check the loft and lie against the chart above before buying — sets this age have often been bent from standard.
