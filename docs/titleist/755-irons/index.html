About the Titleist Forged 755

The Forged 755 was announced on 14 August 2006 as a new flagship iron in Titleist's 700 series, replacing the Forged 704.CB and slotting between the 735.CM combo set and the more forgiving 775.CB. The head is forged from soft 410 stainless steel in a multi-step patented process, with a red-and-silver 6061 T-6 forged aluminum insert in the cavity to damp vibration and a weight shelf that moves mass into the heel and toe. A dual-hosel design uses a shorter hosel in the 2-7 irons for higher launch and a longer hosel in the 8-PW for a flatter, controlled flight. Lofts are traditional — a 35-degree 7-iron and 47-degree pitching wedge — and the line gave way to the AP1 and AP2 announced in January 2008. Figures are the men's standard right-handed steel build.

Frequently asked questions

What is the loft of a Titleist Forged 755 7-iron?

The Forged 755 7-iron has a loft of 35 degrees, with a 63-degree lie and a 37-inch steel-shaft length.

Are the Titleist 755 irons forged?

Yes — from soft 410 stainless steel in a multi-step patented forging process, with a forged 6061 T-6 aluminum insert in the cavity acting as a vibration dampener.

What replaced the Titleist Forged 755?

Titleist's AP1 and AP2, announced in January 2008. The AP2 took over the 755's slot as the technology-infused forged players iron.

What shaft did the Titleist 755 come with?

The stock shaft was Titleist's Tri-Spec steel shaft in regular or stiff flex, a lightweight design that plays softer than a Dynamic Gold S300.

Finding a used set

The Titleist Forged 755 ran from 2006 to 2008 and is no longer in production, so the used market is the only source. Search eBay, 2nd Swing, Golf Avenue and the PGA Tour Superstore trade-in listings, and check the loft and lie against the chart above before buying — sets this age have often been bent from standard.
