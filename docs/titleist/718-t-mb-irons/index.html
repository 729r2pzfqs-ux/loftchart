About the Titleist 718 T-MB

The 718 T-MB carries the hollow-body concept of the 716 T-MB into Titleist's 718 line, keeping the muscleback-style profile over a thin, fast face while adding more high-density tungsten in the heel and toe for a lower centre of gravity and higher MOI. Unlike its predecessor, which was pitched mainly as a utility long iron, the 718 T-MB was offered worldwide as a full set from a 17-degree 2-iron through the 45-degree pitching wedge and a 50-degree wedge. Lofts, lies and lengths carry over unchanged from the 716 T-MB, with a 33-degree 7-iron sitting two degrees stronger than the 718 MB and CB. It left the line when the 2019 T-Series arrived, with the U500 utility iron taking over the hollow-body long-iron role. Figures are the men's standard right-handed steel build.

Frequently asked questions

What is the loft of a Titleist 718 T-MB 7-iron?

The 718 T-MB 7-iron has a loft of 33 degrees, a lie of 63 degrees and a steel-shaft length of 37 inches — two degrees stronger than the 718 MB and CB.

Was the 718 T-MB sold as a full set?

Yes. Unlike the 716 T-MB, which Titleist pitched mainly as a utility long iron, the 718 T-MB was offered worldwide from a 17-degree 2-iron through the 45-degree pitching wedge, plus a 50-degree W.

What changed from the 716 T-MB to the 718 T-MB?

The lofts, lies and lengths carried over unchanged; the 718 added more high-density tungsten in the heel and toe for a lower centre of gravity and higher MOI, and full sets became available in all markets.

What replaced the Titleist 718 T-MB?

The 2019 T-Series. Titleist's U500 utility iron took over the hollow-body long-iron role, with the T200 covering the players-distance set.

Finding a used set

The Titleist 718 T-MB ran from 2017 to 2019 and is no longer in production, so the used market is the only source. Search eBay, 2nd Swing, Golf Avenue and the PGA Tour Superstore trade-in listings, and check the loft and lie against the chart above before buying — sets this age have often been bent from standard.
