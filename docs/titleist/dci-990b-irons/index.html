About the Titleist DCI 990B

The DCI 990B is the better-player counterpart to the DCI 990, introduced in 2000 for low handicappers and professionals. It reads as a contemporary blade: a variable muscle-back cavity over a sole that is significantly narrower and flatter than the standard 990's, with more bounce and less offset. The centre of gravity moves progressively through the set — lower in the long irons for launch, higher in the short irons for a flatter, controlled flight. Lofts match the DCI 990 exactly, with a 36-degree 7-iron and a 49-degree pitching wedge over a 1-iron through pitching wedge range. Davis Love III and Dottie Pepper won with it soon after launch.

Frequently asked questions

What is the loft of a Titleist DCI 990B 7-iron?

The Titleist DCI 990B 7-iron has a loft of 36 degrees, with a 63-degree lie and a 36.75-inch standard steel length.

What is the difference between the DCI 990 and the DCI 990B?

Lofts, lies and lengths are identical. The 990B has a significantly narrower and flatter sole with more bounce, less offset — 0.092 against 0.103 inches at the 7-iron — and a more blade-like profile aimed at the low handicapper.

Are the Titleist DCI 990B irons blades?

Effectively yes for their era: Titleist described the 990B as a contemporary blade, using a shallow variable muscle-back cavity cast from 431 stainless steel rather than a full cavity back.

Finding a used set

The Titleist DCI 990B ran from 2000 to 2002 and is no longer in production, so the used market is the only source. Search eBay, 2nd Swing, Golf Avenue and the PGA Tour Superstore trade-in listings, and check the loft and lie against the chart above before buying — sets this age have often been bent from standard.
