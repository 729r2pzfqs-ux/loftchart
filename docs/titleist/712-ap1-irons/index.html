About the Titleist 712 AP1

The 712 AP1 is the second-generation model of Titleist's Advanced Performance game-improvement iron, announced on 13 September 2011 and at retail from 18 November 2011 as the 2012 line. It keeps the multi-material dual-cavity construction of the 710 AP1, with a redesigned stainless steel soleplate and an integrated tungsten nickel sole weight that Titleist credited with a 4 percent MOI increase in the long irons, plus a co-moulded elastomer and aluminium Tuned Feel insert. The short irons gained a progressively reduced blade length for a more traditional look. Lofts carried over from the 710 AP1 unchanged — a 33-degree 7-iron, 45-degree pitching wedge and a 50-degree wedge that Titleist badged W, recorded here as GW to match the neighbouring AP1 records. Unusually, Titleist published per-club bounce for this set, running from 1 degree at the 3-iron to 7 at the gap wedge. The 714 AP1 replaced it in 2013.

Frequently asked questions

What is the loft of a Titleist 712 AP1 7-iron?

The Titleist 712 AP1 7-iron has a loft of 33 degrees, a lie of 63 degrees and a standard steel length of 37 inches.

Did the 712 AP1 change lofts from the 710 AP1?

No. The ladder carried over unchanged — 20 degrees at the 3-iron, 33 at the 7-iron, 45 at the pitching wedge and a 50-degree gap wedge — with the changes coming in weighting, MOI and the reshaped short irons.

What shafts came stock on the Titleist 712 AP1?

True Temper Dynalite Gold XP in steel and a Tour AD by Graphite Design made exclusively for Titleist in graphite, with a Golf Pride-made Titleist Tour Velvet grip.

When were Titleist 712 AP1 irons made?

Titleist announced them on 13 September 2011 with retail availability from 18 November 2011, selling them as the 2012 line until the 714 AP1 arrived in 2013.

Finding a used set

The Titleist 712 AP1 ran from 2011 to 2013 and is no longer in production, so the used market is the only source. Search eBay, 2nd Swing, Golf Avenue and the PGA Tour Superstore trade-in listings, and check the loft and lie against the chart above before buying — sets this age have often been bent from standard.
