About the Titleist 716 T-MB

The 716 T-MB is the hollow-body model of Titleist's 716 line — a head with the blade length and profile of the 716 AP2 but a hollow structure behind a thin, high-strength face, with high-density tungsten packed into the heel and toe to drop the centre of gravity and raise MOI. Titleist marketed it primarily as a long-iron and hybrid replacement, though the spec chart runs a full 2-iron through pitching wedge and full sets were sold in some markets, notably Japan. Lofts are stronger than the 716 MB and CB — a 33-degree 7-iron and 45-degree pitching wedge against the musclebacks' 35 and 47 — with a 17-degree 2-iron at the top of the range that became a popular tour driving iron. The 716 line reached retail in the autumn of 2015 and was replaced by the 718 line in 2017, where the T-MB continued with more tungsten and worldwide full-set availability. Figures are the men's standard right-handed steel build.

Frequently asked questions

What is the loft of a Titleist 716 T-MB 7-iron?

The Titleist 716 T-MB 7-iron has a loft of 33 degrees, a lie of 63.0 degrees and a steel-shaft length of 37.00 inches — two degrees stronger than the 716 MB and CB.

What is the Titleist 716 T-MB?

A hollow-body iron in the 716 line that looks like a muscleback at address but hides a thin, fast face and high-density tungsten heel and toe weights, making it far more forgiving and longer than its size suggests. Titleist pitched it mainly as a long-iron and hybrid replacement.

Was the 716 T-MB sold as a full set?

The spec chart runs from a 17-degree 2-iron to a 45-degree pitching wedge, but Titleist marketed it primarily as individual long irons; full sets were available in some markets such as Japan. Its successor, the 718 T-MB, was offered as a full set worldwide.

When was the Titleist 716 T-MB made?

The 716 line reached retail in September 2015 and was replaced by the 718 line, including a new 718 T-MB, in 2017.

Finding a used set

The Titleist 716 T-MB ran from 2015 to 2017 and is no longer in production, so the used market is the only source. Search eBay, 2nd Swing, Golf Avenue and the PGA Tour Superstore trade-in listings, and check the loft and lie against the chart above before buying — sets this age have often been bent from standard.
