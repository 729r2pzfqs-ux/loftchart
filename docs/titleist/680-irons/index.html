About the Titleist Forged 680

The Forged 680 is the compact tour blade Titleist released in 2003 to run alongside the larger 690.MB, aimed at players wanting a smaller head for workability and trajectory control. It carries a slightly higher muscle position than the 690.MB for a slightly flatter ball flight, reduced offset, a classic profile with a thin topline and shallow heel, and a narrow sole with reduced bounce and moderate camber for players who pick the ball. Lofts are fully traditional - a 36 degree 7-iron and 48 degree pitching wedge - and the set ran from a 19 degree 2-iron through the pitching wedge, in right hand only. Long a favourite of tour players such as Adam Scott and Webb Simpson, it became a custom-order-only model by late 2005 and left the line as the 695 generation took over; Titleist reissued it as a limited-edition custom order in June 2025.

Frequently asked questions

What is the loft of a Titleist Forged 680 7-iron?

The Titleist Forged 680 7-iron is 36 degrees, with a 63 degree lie and a 37 inch standard steel length.

What is the difference between the Titleist Forged 680 and the 690.MB?

The 680 is more compact with reduced offset and a slightly higher muscle position, producing a flatter, more workable flight; the 690.MB is the slightly larger, more conventional muscleback of the same era. The 680 also runs one degree weaker through the mid irons, with a 36 degree 7-iron.

Which tour players used the Titleist 680 irons?

Adam Scott put them in play when they debuted around 2003 and used them for well over a decade, and Webb Simpson carried 680 MB mid and short irons long after the model was discontinued. Titleist reissued the 680 as a limited-edition custom order in June 2025.

Finding a used set

The Titleist Forged 680 ran from 2003 to 2006 and is no longer in production, so the used market is the only source. Search eBay, 2nd Swing, Golf Avenue and the PGA Tour Superstore trade-in listings, and check the loft and lie against the chart above before buying — sets this age have often been bent from standard.
