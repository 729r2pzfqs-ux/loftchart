About the Ping G700

The G700 was Ping's first hollow-body distance iron, built like a metalwood: a thin maraging C300 face plasma-welded to a hollow 17-4 stainless body with internal metal-injection-moulded weighting. It sits a full club stronger than the G400 of the same period, with a 29.5-degree 7-iron and a 44-degree pitching wedge, and is built long — the 4-iron measures 38.875 inches. Head weight is light enough that the stock steel build comes out at C9. Lies below are the black (standard) colour code.

Frequently asked questions

What is the loft of a Ping G700 7-iron?

The Ping G700 7-iron has a loft of 29.5 degrees, a standard (black dot) lie of 62.0 degrees and a steel-shaft length of 37.00 inches.

When were Ping G700 irons made?

The G700 was introduced in 2018 and was replaced by the G710 in 2020.

How strong are Ping G700 lofts?

Very strong for the period. The 7-iron is 29.5 degrees and the pitching wedge 44 degrees, about a club stronger than the G400 of the same era.

Why is the Ping G700 swing weight only C9?

The hollow head is light and the set is built long — 38.875 inches at the 4-iron — so the stock steel build measures C9 through the mid irons and rises to D1 in the wedges.

Finding a used set

The Ping G700 ran from 2018 to 2020 and is no longer in production, so the used market is the only source. Search eBay, 2nd Swing, Golf Avenue and the PGA Tour Superstore trade-in listings, and check the loft and lie against the chart above before buying — sets this age have often been bent from standard.
