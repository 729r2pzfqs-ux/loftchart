About the Ping Karsten

The 2014 Karsten revived the first name of Ping founder Karsten Solheim on a hybrid-iron combo set built for maximum height and distance. The heads have the longest heel-to-toe length Ping had produced to that point, with very wide soles, a deep centre of gravity and prominent offset, and the set replaces the long irons entirely: it starts at the 5-iron, with 19-, 22- and 25.5-degree Karsten hybrids covering the 3H-5H slots above it. Lofts are distance-spec for 2014 — a 30.5-degree 7-iron and 45-degree pitching wedge — and Ping published the lie angles unrounded, as listed below. Ping's colour-code (dot) system sets lie angle relative to standard; the lies below are the BLACK (standard) lies. The GMax replaced it in Ping's game-improvement slot in 2015.

Frequently asked questions

What is the loft of a Ping Karsten 7-iron?

The 2014 Ping Karsten 7-iron has a loft of 30.5 degrees, a standard (black dot) lie of 61.88 degrees and a steel-shaft length of 37 inches.

Why does the Ping Karsten set start at the 5-iron?

The Karsten was sold as a hybrid-iron combo. Ping replaced the long irons with matching Karsten hybrids — 3H, 4H and 5H at 19, 22 and 25.5 degrees — above the 5-iron through sand wedge irons.

Who is the Ping Karsten iron named after?

Karsten Solheim, the founder of Ping. The 2014 model revived his first name for a modern, wide-soled distance iron rather than reissuing any classic Karsten-era head.

When was the Ping Karsten iron made?

It was introduced in 2014 and remained in the line until the GMax replaced it in 2015, though sets stayed at retail into 2016.

Finding a used set

The Ping Karsten ran from 2014 to 2015 and is no longer in production, so the used market is the only source. Search eBay, 2nd Swing, Golf Avenue and the PGA Tour Superstore trade-in listings, and check the loft and lie against the chart above before buying — sets this age have often been bent from standard.
