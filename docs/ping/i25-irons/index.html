About the Ping i25

The i25 was Ping's better-player iron of 2014, announced on 2 January 2014 alongside the i25 driver, fairways and hybrids and the Karsten hybrid/iron set. It is cast from 17-4 stainless steel with a progressive set design: the long irons carry larger heads and wider soles than the i20 to raise launch and forgiveness, while the mid and short irons are more compact with thinner top rails, narrower soles and less offset for control into the green. The pitching wedge is a traditional-for-the-time 46 degrees, and Ping built matching UW, SW and LW heads so the set runs 3-iron through lob wedge. The stock shaft was Ping's own CFS steel. The i25 was replaced in 2015 when Ping dropped the numbering and launched the i iron (also catalogued as the iE1). Ping's colour-code (dot) system sets lie angle relative to standard; the lies below are the BLACK (standard) lies.

Frequently asked questions

What is the loft of a Ping i25 7-iron?

The Ping i25 7-iron has a loft of 33 degrees, a standard (black dot) lie of 62.25 degrees and a steel-shaft length of 36.75 inches.

When were Ping i25 irons released?

Ping announced the i25 line on 2 January 2014, with pre-orders opening the same day. The irons were replaced in 2015 by the Ping i (iE1).

What clubs were available in the Ping i25 set?

Heads ran from the 3-iron through the lob wedge — 3-9, a 46-degree pitching wedge, plus matching 50-degree UW, 54-degree SW and 58-degree LW.

Finding a used set

The Ping i25 ran from 2014 to 2015 and is no longer in production, so the used market is the only source. Search eBay, 2nd Swing, Golf Avenue and the PGA Tour Superstore trade-in listings, and check the loft and lie against the chart above before buying — sets this age have often been bent from standard.
