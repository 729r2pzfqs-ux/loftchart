About the Ping i

In 2015 Ping dropped the numbering from its players line and launched the iron simply called i, also catalogued as the iE1 — the name under which it was first spotted on tour at the Greenbrier Classic in July 2015. It was the first Ping iron cast from 431 stainless steel, chosen for its softer feel and high strength-to-weight ratio, with tungsten toe weighting for forgiveness in a compact players head. Lengths and lofts were pushed about a quarter inch longer and one degree stronger than the i25 in the scoring clubs — the 7-iron is 32 degrees at 37 inches — and the set runs 3-iron to pitching wedge plus a 50-degree UW, in right and left hand. One caution on the lies below: unlike Ping's charts for the i25 and i200, which are based on the black colour code, Ping's own specification chart for this model states the lie angles are 'based on Blue Color Code at standard length', and the figures are recorded here exactly as Ping published them, without conversion.

Frequently asked questions

What is the loft of a Ping i 7-iron?

The 2015 Ping i 7-iron has a loft of 32 degrees and a steel-shaft length of 37 inches. Ping's published lie is 62.63 degrees, which its chart bases on the blue colour code at standard length.

What is the Ping iE1?

iE1 is the catalogue designation for the 2015 Ping i iron — the model was spotted on tour under that name at the Greenbrier Classic in July 2015 before launching simply as the Ping i.

What is the difference between the Ping i25 and the Ping i irons?

The i switched from 17-4 to softer 431 stainless steel, added tungsten toe weighting, and runs about a quarter inch longer and one degree stronger through most of the set — the 7-iron went from 33 degrees at 36.75 inches to 32 degrees at 37 inches.

When were Ping i irons made?

The i was introduced in 2015 as the replacement for the i25 and was itself replaced by the i200 in 2017.

Finding a used set

The Ping i ran from 2015 to 2017 and is no longer in production, so the used market is the only source. Search eBay, 2nd Swing, Golf Avenue and the PGA Tour Superstore trade-in listings, and check the loft and lie against the chart above before buying — sets this age have often been bent from standard.
