About the Ping i15

Launched alongside the G15 in the autumn of 2009, the i15 is the tour-shaped half of that pair: a smaller cast head with a machined face, a thinner top line and a tungsten toe weight. Lofts step back from the i10 through the mid and short irons — 33 degrees at the 7-iron against 34, 41 at the 9-iron against 42 — while the lie, length and bounce chart carries over. The long irons run negative bounce, minus 2.7 degrees at the 3-iron. Lies below are the black (standard) colour code.

Frequently asked questions

What is the loft of a Ping i15 7-iron?

The Ping i15 7-iron has a loft of 33 degrees, a standard (black dot) lie of 62.25 degrees and a steel-shaft length of 36.75 inches.

When were Ping i15 irons made?

The i15 launched in 2009 alongside the G15 and was replaced by the i20 in 2012.

Why do Ping i15 long irons have negative bounce?

The 3-iron and 4-iron are published at minus 2.7 and minus 1.8 degrees of bounce, meaning the leading edge sits below the trailing edge at address. It is a tour-style sole intended to cut into the turf rather than skid off it.

What is the difference between the Ping i15 and the i10?

Lie, length and offset geometry carry over, but the i15 weakens the mid and short irons by a degree — 33 degrees at the 7-iron against the i10's 34 — and adds a machined face and a tungsten toe weight.

Finding a used set

The Ping i15 ran from 2009 to 2012 and is no longer in production, so the used market is the only source. Search eBay, 2nd Swing, Golf Avenue and the PGA Tour Superstore trade-in listings, and check the loft and lie against the chart above before buying — sets this age have often been bent from standard.
