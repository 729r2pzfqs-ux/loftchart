About the Ping i20

The i20 keeps the i15's loft, lie and length chart unchanged and reworks everything around it: a matte black finish, a slightly larger but thinner profile, and a sole that swaps the i15's negative long-iron bounce for a conventional progressive one running 4 degrees at the 3-iron to 14 at the sand wedge. Offset is cut throughout, from 0.26 to 0.24 inches at the 3-iron and 0.14 to 0.10 at the 7-iron. Lies below are the black (standard) code.

Frequently asked questions

What is the loft of a Ping i20 7-iron?

The Ping i20 7-iron has a loft of 33 degrees, a standard (black dot) lie of 62.25 degrees and a steel-shaft length of 36.75 inches.

When were Ping i20 irons made?

The i20 was the 2012 model in Ping's i-series, following the i15 and preceding the i25.

Are Ping i15 and i20 lofts the same?

Yes, club for club: 21, 24, 27, 30, 33, 37, 41 and 46 degrees from 3-iron to pitching wedge, at the same lies and lengths. The i20 carries less offset and a conventional positive-bounce sole where the i15 ran negative bounce in the long irons.

How much offset does a Ping i20 7-iron have?

0.10 inches, against 0.14 inches on the i15.

Finding a used set

The Ping i20 ran from 2012 to 2014 and is no longer in production, so the used market is the only source. Search eBay, 2nd Swing, Golf Avenue and the PGA Tour Superstore trade-in listings, and check the loft and lie against the chart above before buying — sets this age have often been bent from standard.
