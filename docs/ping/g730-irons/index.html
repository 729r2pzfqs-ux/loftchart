About the Ping G730

The G730 replaces the G710 but drops that model's hollow body for a cavity back: a 17-4 stainless steel head whose face is heat-treated so it can be made thinner and flex more, with Ping's PurFlex badge in the cavity. It is also the strongest-lofted iron Ping has made: 28 degrees at the 7-iron and 40 at the pitching wedge, four degrees stronger through the set than the G710. The set starts at a 5-iron rather than a 4-iron and is filled out with a 45-degree utility and 50- and 56-degree wedges, which is how Ping keeps the gapping usable underneath a 40-degree pitching wedge. Lies below are the black (standard) colour code.

Frequently asked questions

What is the loft of a Ping G730 7-iron?

The Ping G730 7-iron has a loft of 28 degrees, a standard (black dot) lie of 62.0 degrees and a steel-shaft length of 37.00 inches.

How much stronger is the G730 than the G710?

Four degrees through the set. The G730 7-iron is 28 degrees against 29.5 on the G710 at the same 37.00-inch length, and the pitching wedge is 40 degrees against 44.

Does the Ping G730 come with a 4-iron?

No. The set runs from a 5-iron at 21.5 degrees, then pitching wedge, utility wedge and 50- and 56-degree wedges. Ping expects the top of the bag to be covered by a hybrid or crossover.

What is the pitching wedge loft on the Ping G730?

40 degrees — the strongest pitching wedge Ping has catalogued — with a 45-degree utility wedge below it.

Finding a used set

The Ping G730 ran from 2024 to 2026 and is no longer in production, so the used market is the only source. Search eBay, 2nd Swing, Golf Avenue and the PGA Tour Superstore trade-in listings, and check the loft and lie against the chart above before buying — sets this age have often been bent from standard.
