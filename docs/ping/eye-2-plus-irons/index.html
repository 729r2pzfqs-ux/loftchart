About the Ping Eye 2 Plus

The Eye 2 Plus (stamped EYE2+) is the final major revision of the Eye 2, which Ping says underwent dozens of minor and a few major changes before the EYE2+ was introduced in April 1990 — the same month Ping's groove settlement with the USGA took effect. Lofts through the irons are about one degree stronger than the classic Eye 2 chart (35 degrees at the 7-iron against 36), and the wedges were re-spaced to a 48.5-degree PW, 53-degree SW and 58-degree LW in place of the old 50.5 / 57.5 / 61 set-up. Ping's colour-code (dot) system sets lie angle relative to standard, with BLACK the standard lie; the lies below are the black-dot lies, and Ping notes loft and lie are rounded to the nearest 0.5 degree. Ping's own chart adds that Eye 2 irons built after January 1998 were made to this same EYE2+ specification.

Frequently asked questions

What is the loft of a Ping Eye 2 Plus 7-iron?

The Ping Eye 2 Plus 7-iron has a loft of 35 degrees, a standard (black dot) lie of 62.5 degrees and a steel-shaft length of 36.75 inches — one degree stronger than the 36-degree 7-iron of the original Eye 2.

What is the difference between the Ping Eye 2 and the Eye 2 Plus?

The Eye 2 Plus, introduced in April 1990, strengthened the iron lofts by roughly one degree (7-iron from 36 to 35 degrees) and re-spaced the wedges to 48.5, 53 and 58 degrees from the Eye 2's 50.5, 57.5 and 61. Lie angles and lengths are unchanged. Ping also notes that Eye 2 irons built after January 1998 use the Eye 2 Plus specification.

When were Ping Eye 2 Plus irons made?

Ping introduced the EYE2+ in April 1990 as the final revision of the Eye 2 line. The Zing, announced in 1992, took over as Ping's mainstream game-improvement iron, though Eye 2-family irons remained orderable for years afterwards.

What shaft came in the Ping Eye 2 Plus?

The stock steel shaft was Ping's KT ('Karsten Tour'), introduced with the Eye 2+ and later reinforced with a heavier tip as the KT-M.

Finding a used set

The Ping Eye 2 Plus ran from 1990 to 1992 and is no longer in production, so the used market is the only source. Search eBay, 2nd Swing, Golf Avenue and the PGA Tour Superstore trade-in listings, and check the loft and lie against the chart above before buying — sets this age have often been bent from standard.
