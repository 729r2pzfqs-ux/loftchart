About the Ping Zing 2

The Zing 2 was a light refresh of the 1992 Zing, carrying identical lofts and lengths through the set. The published differences sit in the lie column and the wedges: the 5-iron lie is 62.0 degrees against 61.5, the 9-iron and pitching wedge lies are 64.5 and 65.0 where the Zing chart lists 65.0 and 64.5, and the Zing 2 chart carries a 57-degree LS wedge alongside a 61-degree lob wedge where the Zing lists only a 57-degree lob wedge. As with the Zing, Ping published no per-club offset or bounce figures. Ping's colour-code (dot) system sets lie angle relative to standard: gold 4 flat, brown 3 flat, orange 2 flat, red 1 flat, BLACK standard, blue 1 upright, green 2 upright, white 3 upright, silver 4 upright, maroon 5 upright. Frequently asked questions

What is the loft of a Ping Zing 2 7-iron?

The Ping Zing 2 7-iron has a loft of 34.5 degrees, a standard (black dot) lie of 63.5 degrees and a steel-shaft length of 36.75 inches.

When were Ping Zing 2 irons made?

The Zing 2 was introduced in 1994 and was replaced by the ISI in 1996.

What is the difference between the Ping Zing and Zing 2?

Lofts and lengths are identical through the set. The published charts differ in the lie column and the wedges: the 5-iron lie is 62.0 against 61.5 degrees, the 9-iron and pitching wedge lies are 64.5 and 65.0 against the Zing's 65.0 and 64.5, and the Zing 2 adds a 57-degree LS wedge alongside a 61-degree lob wedge where the Zing lists only a 57-degree lob wedge.

Finding a used set

The Ping Zing 2 ran from 1994 to 1996 and is no longer in production, so the used market is the only source. Search eBay, 2nd Swing, Golf Avenue and the PGA Tour Superstore trade-in listings, and check the loft and lie against the chart above before buying — sets this age have often been bent from standard.
