About the Ping i240

The i240 follows the i230 and carries its loft, lie, length and offset chart over unchanged, club for club: the 7-iron is still 33 degrees at 62.0 lie and 37 inches. What changes is around the numbers — a 3-iron on the published chart, AWT 3.0 as the stock steel shaft in place of AWT 2.0, and a softer bounce progression, with a degree less bounce in the 8 and 9-irons than the i230 carried. Ping's colour-code (dot) system sets lie angle relative to standard: gold 4 flat, brown 3 flat, orange 2 flat, red 1 flat, BLACK standard, blue 1 upright, green 2 upright, white 3 upright, silver 4 upright, maroon 5 upright. The lies below are the BLACK (standard) lies.

Frequently asked questions

What is the loft of a Ping i240 7-iron?

The Ping i240 7-iron has a loft of 33 degrees, a standard (black dot) lie of 62.0 degrees and a steel-shaft length of 37.0 inches — the same as the i230 before it.

When were Ping i240 irons released?

In 2025, following the i230. They were spotted in play on tour at the Memorial Tournament earlier that year.

What changed between the Ping i230 and i240?

Loft, lie and length are unchanged through the set. The i240 publishes a 3-iron, uses AWT 3.0 rather than AWT 2.0 as the stock steel shaft, and softens the bounce slightly in the 8 and 9-irons.

Where it sits in the lineup

Introduced in 2025, the Ping i240 is a current players set in the Ping range as of September 2026, with no replacement announced. It replaced the Ping i230. New sets are built to the standard lofts, lies and lengths in the chart above, though a fitting can move any of them — so confirm the numbers on the order sheet. Used sets appear on eBay, 2nd Swing and Golf Avenue, and should be measured against this chart before buying.
