About the Cleveland CG2

The CG2 joined the CG1 blade in Cleveland's line for 2005, using the same patented Carbon Metal Matrix — a carbon-infused steel Cleveland claimed was 10% less dense and 15% softer than traditional carbon steel — in a head for the low-to-mid handicapper who wants cavity-back forgiveness with blade-like workability. The 25-30 grams of discretionary weight freed by the CMM material drop the centre of gravity lower and deeper than most better-player irons of the period, easing launch in the long irons while keeping flight penetrating in the scoring clubs. The standard offering was 3-PW from a published 1-iron-through-SW range, with Dynamic Gold steel or Grafalloy Blue graphite stock. Lofts are a touch stronger than the CG1's — 34 degrees at the 7-iron and 46 at the pitching wedge.

Frequently asked questions

What is the loft of a Cleveland CG2 7-iron?

The Cleveland CG2 7-iron has a loft of 34 degrees, a lie of 62 degrees and a steel-shaft length of 36.75 inches.

Who are the Cleveland CG2 irons for?

Cleveland designed the CG2 for low-to-mid handicappers who want the forgiveness of a cavity back with the workability of a blade — one step easier to play than the CG1 muscle back.

What is CMM in the Cleveland CG2 irons?

Carbon Metal Matrix, Cleveland's patented carbon-infused steel: microscopic carbon spheres make it about 10% less dense and 15% softer than conventional carbon steel, freeing 25-30 grams of weight to lower and deepen the centre of gravity.

Finding a used set

The Cleveland CG2 ran from 2005 to 2007 and is no longer in production, so the used market is the only source. Search eBay, 2nd Swing, Golf Avenue and the PGA Tour Superstore trade-in listings, and check the loft and lie against the chart above before buying — sets this age have often been bent from standard.
