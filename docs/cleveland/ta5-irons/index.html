About the Cleveland TA5

The TA5 was the maximum-forgiveness model in Cleveland's Tour Action range, introduced in 1999 alongside the TA3 and TA4. Its low-profile head puts the centre of gravity low and deep for stability on off-centre hits and easy, high launch — especially in the long irons — while the patented Vibration Absorbing System (VAS) keeps impact feeling solid. Lofts are traditional (33.5-degree 7-iron, 45-degree pitching wedge) on a light D0 swing weight through the numbered irons. The published range ran 1-iron through sand wedge, with steel and graphite lengths listed separately; a GunMetal version and a Senior build followed in 2002, and the line was carried until the wide-soled TA6 arrived. Note that some lists date the TA5 to 1997; Cleveland's own December 1999 page introduces "the new TA5", matching the 1999 stock-shaft records.

Frequently asked questions

What is the loft of a Cleveland TA5 7-iron?

The Cleveland TA5 7-iron has a loft of 33.5 degrees, a lie of 61.5 degrees and a steel-shaft length of 36.75 inches.

What year are the Cleveland TA5 irons?

Cleveland introduced the TA5 in 1999 — its own site presented it as new that December — and kept it in the line into 2003, adding a GunMetal finish and a Senior build in 2002. Some lists date it 1997, which the period records do not support.

Who are the Cleveland TA5 irons for?

Cleveland aimed the TA5 at players seeking maximum forgiveness in a low-profile iron: the low, deep centre of gravity stabilises off-centre hits and makes shots easy to launch high and straight, especially with the long irons.

Finding a used set

The Cleveland TA5 ran from 1999 to 2004 and is no longer in production, so the used market is the only source. Search eBay, 2nd Swing, Golf Avenue and the PGA Tour Superstore trade-in listings, and check the loft and lie against the chart above before buying — sets this age have often been bent from standard.
