About the Cleveland VAS+

The VAS+ is the 1996 refinement of Cleveland's radical VAS 792 of 1993 — the inset-hosel, shovel-backed iron Corey Pavin used to win the 1995 U.S. Open at Shinnecock Hills. VAS stands for Vibration Absorbing System: a full cavity plate damps impact shock so completely that Cleveland could thin the face and push weight to the perimeter, while the hosel sits inset from the face — MIT testing under Dr. Rudrapatna Ramnath found the layout reduced torque transmitted to the shaft and squared the face more easily. Sets ran 1-iron (or 2-iron) through PW, DW, SW and LW with True Temper boron composite or steel shafts in Cleveland's Diamond (firm) and Square (medium) flex designations. Lofts are traditional with a 33.5-degree 7-iron. Note the task brief requested the 1993 VAS as a players iron: no per-club specification table for the original VAS 792 could be corroborated anywhere, so this record covers the fully documented VAS+, which period sources treat as a game-improvement design. The DW's specs were not published in the compiled tables and it is omitted below.

Frequently asked questions

What is the loft of a Cleveland VAS+ 7-iron?

The Cleveland VAS+ 7-iron has a loft of 33.5 degrees, a lie of 61.5 degrees and a length of 36.75 inches.

What is the difference between the Cleveland VAS 792 and VAS+?

The VAS 792 of 1993 was the original inset-hosel design — the set Corey Pavin used to win the 1995 U.S. Open — while the VAS+ of 1996 refined the same Vibration Absorbing System concept with a new assembly process and cleaner styling. Cleveland never published a per-club spec table for the 792 that survives today.

What does VAS mean on Cleveland irons?

Vibration Absorbing System — a full cavity damping plate that soaks up impact shock, which let Cleveland thin the clubface and move weight to the perimeter for forgiveness without harsh feel.

Finding a used set

The Cleveland VAS+ ran from 1996 to 1998 and is no longer in production, so the used market is the only source. Search eBay, 2nd Swing, Golf Avenue and the PGA Tour Superstore trade-in listings, and check the loft and lie against the chart above before buying — sets this age have often been bent from standard.
