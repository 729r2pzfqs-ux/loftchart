About the Cleveland CG1

The CG1 launched Cleveland's CG iron line in 2004 as a traditional muscle-back blade made from the company's patented Carbon Metal Matrix — a steel infused with microscopic carbon spheres that Cleveland claimed was 10% less dense and 15% softer than conventional carbon steel, at 77 on the Rockwell B scale. A slightly larger muscle moves the centre of gravity lower and deeper than a classic blade for higher flight in the long irons. The set ran 1-iron through pitching wedge in a mirror chrome finish, with True Temper Dynamic Gold taper-tip steel stock; a Black Pearl finish followed in 2006, and the softer-category CG2 joined it in 2005. Lofts are fully traditional: 35 degrees at the 7-iron, 47 at the pitching wedge. Note that this record files the CG1 as a blade; some lists group it broadly with players irons.

Frequently asked questions

What is the loft of a Cleveland CG1 7-iron?

The Cleveland CG1 7-iron has a loft of 35 degrees, a lie of 62 degrees and a length of 36.75 inches.

Are the Cleveland CG1 irons forged?

Cleveland never marketed the CG1 as a forging — the heads are made from its patented Carbon Metal Matrix (CMM), a carbon-infused steel the company claimed was softer-feeling than traditional forged carbon steel.

What is the difference between the Cleveland CG1 and CG2?

The CG1 is a 1-PW muscle-back blade with traditional lofts for the better player; the 2005 CG2 uses the same CMM material in a slightly stronger-lofted head with a lower, deeper centre of gravity, aimed at low-to-mid handicappers.

Finding a used set

The Cleveland CG1 ran from 2004 to 2007 and is no longer in production, so the used market is the only source. Search eBay, 2nd Swing, Golf Avenue and the PGA Tour Superstore trade-in listings, and check the loft and lie against the chart above before buying — sets this age have often been bent from standard.
