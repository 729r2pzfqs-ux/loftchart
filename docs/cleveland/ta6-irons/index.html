About the Cleveland TA6

The TA6 took over from the TA5 as the game-improvement model in Cleveland's Tour Action range for 2004. Material is pulled out of the cavity and moved into a wider sole and the extreme perimeter, dropping the centre of gravity lower and further from the face for quicker launch and a higher moment of inertia, with the patented Vibration Absorbing System damping impact. The long irons use a lower-profile head while the scoring clubs keep a deeper, more traditional face, and offset is generous throughout — 0.310 inch at the 1-iron down to zero at the sand wedge, published in the normal decreasing direction. Heads are cast 17-4 stainless in a satin finish on True Temper Action Lite steel or a lightweight graphite. Note that some lists date the TA6 to 2001; it is absent from Cleveland's own April 2003 iron line-up and appears in 2004, matching the compiled shaft records, and a W-Series women's version followed in 2005.

Frequently asked questions

What is the loft of a Cleveland TA6 7-iron?

The Cleveland TA6 7-iron has a loft of 33.5 degrees, a lie of 62.5 degrees and a steel-shaft length of 36.75 inches.

What year are the Cleveland TA6 irons?

The TA6 appears in Cleveland's line in 2004 — it is absent from the company's own April 2003 iron catalogue — and a W-Series women's version followed in 2005. Some retailer lists date it 2001, which the period records contradict.

How much offset do Cleveland TA6 irons have?

A lot by Cleveland standards: 0.310 inch in the 1- and 2-irons, tapering to 0.150 inch at the 7-iron and zero at the sand wedge, to help square the face at impact.

Finding a used set

The Cleveland TA6 ran from 2004 to 2006 and is no longer in production, so the used market is the only source. Search eBay, 2nd Swing, Golf Avenue and the PGA Tour Superstore trade-in listings, and check the loft and lie against the chart above before buying — sets this age have often been bent from standard.
