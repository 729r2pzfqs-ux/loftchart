Golf Club Loft Explained

What loft is, how it shapes trajectory and distance, and the real loft ranges for every club type — the beginner’s page the rest of this site builds on.

Loft is the backward tilt of the clubface, measured in degrees from vertical. It is the single spec that most decides what a club does: a 9° driver face sends the ball out low and fast; a 60° lob wedge sends the same swing almost straight up. Every other club in the bag sits on the ladder between them, spaced a few degrees apart so each one carries a different distance.

What loft actually does

At impact, loft splits the clubhead’s speed between forward ball speed and upward launch with backspin. Low loft keeps most of the energy going forward — fast, flat and long, but hard to get airborne without clubhead speed. High loft trades ball speed for height and spin — shorter, but easy to launch, quick to stop on a green and far more tolerant of an imperfect strike. That trade is the architecture of the whole bag: distance clubs at the low-loft end, scoring clubs at the high-loft end.

Loft also shrinks your misses. The more loft on the face, the more backspin dominates sidespin, so curved shots curve less — one reason beginners' slices are worst with the driver and vanish with the 9-iron, and why the standard advice for a new golfer is more driver loft, not less.

Why the same number means different lofts

Club numbers are labels, not measurements. No rule defines what a 7-iron is, and manufacturers have strengthened lofts steadily for forty years as low-CG designs made stronger lofts launchable — the full story is in the strong vs traditional lofts guide and the iron number equivalents chart.

The spread is real, not rounding. The Titleist 620 MB, a current blade, stamps “7” on a 35° head; the Yonex EZONE GT, a current super-game-improvement set, stamps the same number on a 25° head. Ten degrees — nearly three clubs’ worth of loft — under one label. Both are honest designs for different golfers; the label is just not the spec.

Using loft when you buy

Compare clubs by loft, not by number or by marketing category. When a new set claims an extra ten yards, check its loft chart first — most of the gain is usually a stronger loft, which you could also get by hitting one more club. When gapping a bag, write down the loft of every club from driver to lob wedge and look for even 3°–5° steps; holes and bunches in that ladder are where your distance gaps come from. Every model page on this site lists the full chart, and the compare pages put two sets side by side.

Frequently asked questions

What does loft mean on a golf club?

The angle between the clubface and the shaft axis — effectively how far the face tilts back from vertical. More loft sends the ball higher with more backspin and less distance; less loft sends it lower, further and with more roll.

How much does loft affect distance?

It is the main reason clubs go different distances. Within an iron set, each club is about 3 to 4 degrees apart, which works out to roughly 10 to 15 yards of carry per club for most golfers. Shaft length adds a little speed club to club, but loft does most of the work.

Is lower loft always longer?

Only when your swing is fast enough to launch it. A low-lofted club needs clubhead speed to get the ball up and carrying; without it, the ball flies low and dies. That is why beginners usually hit a 5-wood past a 3-wood and a hybrid past the long iron it replaced.

Why do lofts vary between brands?

No governing body sets lofts, so each maker chooses its own — and distance-oriented designs use stronger (lower) lofts because their low centre of gravity restores the launch. The same club number can differ by six degrees or more between two current sets, which is why this site charts the actual lofts per model.

What is dynamic loft?

The loft the face actually presents at impact, after shaft lean and shaft bend have added or removed a few degrees from the number stamped on the club. Good iron players deloft the face slightly; a scooping release adds loft. Launch monitors measure it directly.
