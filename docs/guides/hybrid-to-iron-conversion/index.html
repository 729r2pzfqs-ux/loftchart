Hybrid to Iron Conversion Chart

Which hybrid replaces which iron, why the answer depends on your iron set, and when the switch is worth making.

Match the hybrid's loft to the loft of the iron it replaces — not the number on the sole. Against a modern players set that means 19°–21° for the 3-iron, 22°–24° for the 4-iron and 25°–27° for the 5-iron. Against a strong-lofted distance set every slot shifts one club: its 4-iron is the old 3-iron. Expect the hybrid to carry about half a club further at the same loft.

Manufacturers publish the same mapping. Why the hybrid flies further, and what to do about it

A hybrid is built on a longer shaft than the matching iron — typically half an inch to an inch and a half — and its head puts the centre of gravity lower and further back. Both add ball speed and launch, which is the whole point when a long iron has stopped flying, but it means a same-loft swap adds carry at the top of the bag. Fitters handle it one of two ways: take the hybrid a degree or two weaker than the iron it replaces, or take the matching loft and accept that each club above the swap point moves up half a slot.

Either way, verify the result on the course or a launch monitor: the number that matters is the carry gap to the longest iron you kept, which should look like every other gap in the set — about 10–15 yards.

When to make the switch

The switch point is where an iron's loft can no longer buy you height. Mis-hit tolerance aside, an iron below about 25° needs real clubhead speed to fly its full carry and stop on a green; below 21° that is true even for strong players, which is why the 3-iron disappeared from most sets. Moderate swing speeds get more from hybrids from the 5-iron up; faster players often keep irons to the 4 and carry one 19°–21° hybrid. There is no penalty for mixing — most current sets are sold 5–PW precisely because buyers fill the top with hybrids.

Frequently asked questions

What hybrid replaces a 4-iron?

A 22 to 24 degree hybrid for a modern players set, where the 4-iron is about 23 degrees. If your set is a strong-lofted distance model, check its chart first: many current game improvement 4-irons are 19 to 21 degrees, which calls for a 3-hybrid instead.

What hybrid replaces a 5-iron?

25 to 27 degrees against a players set's 26 degree 5-iron; 22 to 24 degrees against a distance set's 22 degree 5-iron. Match the loft printed on your set's spec chart, not the number on the sole.

Do hybrids go further than the irons they replace?

Usually half a club to a full club further at the same loft. The hybrid's longer shaft adds clubhead speed and its deeper centre of gravity adds launch and ball speed. That is why fitters often put you in a hybrid one or two degrees weaker than the iron coming out.

When should I switch from irons to hybrids?

When a long iron stops flying high enough to be useful — for most amateurs that is any iron below about 25 degrees, and at moderate swing speeds the 5-iron too. A practical test: if your 4-iron and 5-iron carry within a few yards of each other, the longer one is not earning its slot and a hybrid will.

Can I carry both the iron and the matching hybrid?

Only if their lofts differ enough to create a real carry gap — three to four degrees, like an iron set's 23 degree 4-iron alongside a 19 to 20 degree hybrid. Two 23 degree clubs is one slot wasted, however different they look.
