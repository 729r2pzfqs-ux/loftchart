About the PXG 0311 T GEN4

The 0311 T is the tour head of PXG's GEN4 line, unveiled in February 2021 with pre-sales from 9 March and retail from 30 March at $349 per club — the most compact of the three GEN4 heads, with the least offset and the lowest bounce. GEN4 introduced XCOR, the polymer core that replaced the GEN3's DualCOR inside the hollow, five-times-forged 8620 carbon steel body, behind a 0.058-inch HT1770 maraging steel face, with a milled back surface and a Precision Weighting Technology plug that fitters could swap in 2-gram increments. Unlike the P and XP, whose GEN4 charts were strengthened by a degree, the T kept the traditional ladder unchanged from the T GEN3 — a 32-degree 7-iron and 46-degree pitching wedge — and passed it on unchanged to the T GEN5. PXG stamps the two scoring clubs W and G; they are listed here as PW and GW. Every set was built to order from PXG's shaft menu, so no stock shaft is recorded.

Frequently asked questions

What is the loft of a PXG 0311 T GEN4 7-iron?

The PXG 0311 T GEN4 7-iron has a loft of 32 degrees, a lie of 62.5 degrees and a length of 37.0 inches.

Did PXG 0311 T specs change from GEN3 to GEN4, or GEN4 to GEN5?

No. The 20-degree 3-iron through 51-degree gap wedge chart is identical across the T GEN3, GEN4 and GEN5; the GEN5 page simply dropped the 3-iron.

What is XCOR?

The polymer core PXG introduced with GEN4, replacing DualCOR. It fills the hollow forged head, supporting the 0.058-inch maraging steel face so it can flex for ball speed while damping sound and feel.

How does the PXG 0311 T GEN4 differ from the P GEN4?

The T is the compact tour head with weaker, traditional lofts — 32 degrees at the 7-iron against the P's 30 — about half the offset, and lower bounce throughout.

Finding a used set

The PXG 0311 T GEN4 ran from 2021 to 2022 and is no longer in production, so the used market is the only source. Search eBay, 2nd Swing, Golf Avenue and the PGA Tour Superstore trade-in listings, and check the loft and lie against the chart above before buying — sets this age have often been bent from standard.
