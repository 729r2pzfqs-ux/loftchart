About the PXG Stick'em

The Stick'em is PXG's fully forged wedge line, announced on 12 March 2026 and sold immediately through pxg.com and PXG stores. The triple-forged 8620 head carries high-toe weighting with a progressive centre of gravity, and wider, more closely spaced grooves — traditional on the 50 and 52 degree heads, full-face from 54 to 60. The matrix runs 10 loft/bounce combinations from 50 to 60 degrees in two grinds. It sits below PXG's milled Sugar Daddy III in price, and the two lines sell alongside each other. PXG builds every order to spec from its shaft catalogue rather than naming a single stock shaft.

Grind options

The sole grinds this model is sold in, with the lofts each one is available at.

S Grind50°, 52°, 54°, 56°, 58°, 60°
A slightly tapered sole with heel and toe relief at 10 degrees of bounce — versatility for square-face and open-face shots and creative short-game play from varied lies. Offered at every loft.
BP Grind54°, 56°, 58°, 60°
A wider, fuller sole delivering 13 degrees of effective bounce that glides through turf and sand — for steeper attack angles and softer conditions, offered from 54 to 60 degrees.

Frequently asked questions

What lofts does the PXG Stick'em come in?

The Stick'em is sold from 50 to 60 degrees in 2-degree steps — 10 loft, bounce and grind combinations.

What grinds does the PXG Stick'em offer?

Two: the S grind at 10 degrees of bounce at every loft, and the wider, higher-bounce BP grind at 13 degrees from 54 to 60 degrees.

Did the PXG Stick'em replace the Sugar Daddy III?

No — PXG still sells the milled Sugar Daddy III above it. The Stick'em is the cheaper, fully forged line that brings PXG wedges to a mainstream price.

Is the PXG Stick'em forged?

Yes — 3X (triple) forged from 8620 soft carbon steel, with high-toe weighting and a progressive centre of gravity across the lofts.

Where it sits in the lineup

Introduced in 2026, the PXG Stick'em is a current tour wedge in the PXG range as of October 2026, with no replacement announced. New wedges are built to the loft, bounce and grind options in the chart above; a fitting can bend loft and lie from standard, so confirm the numbers on the order sheet. Check the loft and bounce against the chart above before buying used — wedges are routinely bent from standard, and worn grooves cost spin long before a wedge looks worn.
