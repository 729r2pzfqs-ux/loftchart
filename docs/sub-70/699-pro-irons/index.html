About the Sub 70 699 Pro

The 699 Pro took the technology of Sub 70's original 699 — the precision-milled 1.7 mm 455 Carpenter steel face and TPE-injected hollow body — and refined it into a classic players shape: a thinner 7.0 mm topline, a compact 74 mm blade at the 6-iron, sharply reduced offset and a black Tour PVD finish that Sub 70's copy promised would age "in the dignified manner of a good cigar". Its product page was live by December 2019, and lofts are identical to the standard 699 — a 31-degree 7-iron and 45-degree pitching wedge across a 4-AW chart — so the two heads could be blended into combo sets. In MyGolfSpy's 2019 Most Wanted players-distance test the 699 family finished ahead of entries from Callaway, Ping and Titleist, helping establish the direct-to-consumer maker's reputation. The 699 Pro V2 replaced it in January 2023. Sets were built to order in Sycamore, Illinois from a no-upcharge shaft menu, so no stock shaft or swing weight is recorded.

Frequently asked questions

What is the loft of a Sub 70 699 Pro 7-iron?

The Sub 70 699 Pro 7-iron has a loft of 31 degrees, a lie of 62.5 degrees and a standard steel length of 37 inches — identical to the standard 699, which shares its whole loft chart.

Is the Sub 70 699 Pro forged?

No. Like the standard 699 it is a two-piece hollow-body iron with a precision-milled 1.7 mm 455 Carpenter steel face and a TPE resin-injected core; the Pro differs in shape, offset and its black Tour PVD finish.

How much offset does the Sub 70 699 Pro have?

3.5 mm (0.138 inches) at the 4-iron tapering to 0.75 mm at the AW — roughly 40 per cent less than the standard 699 at the long end, which is the main reason Sub 70 pitched it at lower handicaps.

When was the Sub 70 699 Pro replaced?

Sub 70 announced the 699 Pro V2 on 6 January 2023, with stronger lofts (30-degree 7-iron) and a new S450 steel face.

Finding a used set

The Sub 70 699 Pro ran from 2019 to 2023 and is no longer in production, so the used market is the only source. Search eBay, 2nd Swing, Golf Avenue and the PGA Tour Superstore trade-in listings, and check the loft and lie against the chart above before buying — sets this age have often been bent from standard.
