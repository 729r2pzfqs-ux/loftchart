About the Honma Beres 09

The ninth-generation Beres continues Honma's ultra-premium lightweight line with head specifications carried over unchanged from the Beres 08: a forged mild-steel 25C body with a cast AM355P face in the 5- through 9-irons and an ES235 face in the scoring clubs, at the same strong lofts (27-degree 7-iron) tuned to launch high from a deep, low centre of gravity. The changes are in the shafts and finish: new hand-rolled ARMRQ FX graphite in 2-Star to 5-Star grades (plus an even lighter FX LIGHT), still at very light C8-D0 swing weights, under two-layer nickel-chrome plating with gold detailing on the higher grades. Honma numbers the scoring clubs: the 40-degree 10 is the pitching wedge and the 45-degree 11 the gap wedge, ahead of 50-degree AW and 55-degree SW.

Frequently asked questions

What is the loft of a Honma Beres 09 7-iron?

The Honma Beres 09 7-iron has a loft of 27 degrees, a lie of 62 degrees and a length of 37.25 inches.

What changed between the Honma Beres 08 and Beres 09?

The head chart is identical — same lofts, lies, lengths and offsets. The 09 generation introduced the new ARMRQ FX shaft family (replacing the 08's ARMRQ MX) plus an extra-light FX LIGHT option, along with refreshed plating and finish grades.

Why do Honma Beres 09 irons cost so much?

Each set is hand-finished in Sakata, Japan, and the price scales with the star grade of the hand-rolled ARMRQ FX shaft and its finish — from the 2-Star entry build to the 5-Star flagship with gold-plated detailing; top grades run well into five figures per set.

Finding a used set

The Honma Beres 09 ran from 2023 to 2025 and is no longer in production, so the used market is the only source. Search eBay, 2nd Swing, Golf Avenue and the PGA Tour Superstore trade-in listings, and check the loft and lie against the chart above before buying — sets this age have often been bent from standard.
