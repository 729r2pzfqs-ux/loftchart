About the Honma TW-X

The T//World-X (TW-X) grew out of the TW-U utility iron that Justin Rose and other tour players carried as a driving iron: for June 2019 Honma extended the forged hollow-body, muscleback-look construction into a full set within the T//World 747 lineup. An S25C forged body carries a co-forged MS300 high-strength steel face plate and two internal steel weights in the heel and toe, and every head keeps a near-constant wide, cambered sole for a low, deep centre of gravity. Honma numbers the scoring clubs — the 44-degree 10 is the pitching wedge and the 49-degree 11 the gap wedge — and sold the set in 4-10 and 5-11 configurations at $175 per club in steel, with the long irons also available individually as utilities. Lengths below are the Vizard graphite build; Modus3 steel builds used the same lengths from the 4-iron down.

Frequently asked questions

What is the loft of a Honma TW-X 7-iron?

The Honma TW-X 7-iron has a loft of 31 degrees, a lie of 62.5 degrees and a length of 37 inches.

How is the Honma TW-X related to Justin Rose?

The set grew out of the TW-U utility iron Rose carried on the PGA Tour — he used one as his 2-iron when winning the 2019 Farmers Insurance Open. Honma extended that forged hollow-body construction into the full TW-X set launched in June 2019.

What set configurations did the Honma TW-X come in?

Two stock configurations: 4-iron through the 10 (pitching wedge), or 5-iron through the 11 (49-degree gap wedge). The 2, 3, 4 and 5 irons were also sold individually as utility irons.

Finding a used set

The Honma TW-X ran from 2019 to 2021 and is no longer in production, so the used market is the only source. Search eBay, 2nd Swing, Golf Avenue and the PGA Tour Superstore trade-in listings, and check the loft and lie against the chart above before buying — sets this age have often been bent from standard.
