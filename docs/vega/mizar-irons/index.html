About the Vega Mizar

The original Mizar headed Vega's Star Range and was the most powerful iron in the Japanese forging house's line - a one-piece forged S25C body carrying a constant-thickness 3.5 mm maraging face and a tungsten slug that moves progressively from heel (long irons) to toe (short irons) to lower the centre of gravity and help square the face. It was on sale by early 2018 and ran until the second-generation Mizar arrived in 2023; MyGolfSpy's launch coverage of the replacement put the original's run at six years, which dates the debut to 2017. Vega builds every set to order through fitters and publishes loft, lie, face progression and head weight but no standard lengths or offsets, so those keys are omitted. The stock set runs 4-iron to attack wedge with a 30-degree 7-iron and 42-degree pitching wedge.

Frequently asked questions

What is the loft of a Vega Mizar 7-iron?

The original Vega Mizar 7-iron has a loft of 30 degrees and a 61.5-degree lie.

Who makes Vega Mizar irons?

Vega grew out of the Kyoei forging house in Ichikawa, Japan, and its heads are forged in Japan. The brand has been owned by Professional Golf Europe (2011) and Elite Sports Brands (2024), with every set built to order.

What length is a standard Vega Mizar iron?

Vega does not publish standard lengths - every set is built to a fitting through its stockists - so no length column is listed here. Loft, lie, face progression and head weight are the manufacturer's published head specs.

Finding a used set

The Vega Mizar ran from 2017 to 2023 and is no longer in production, so the used market is the only source. Search eBay, 2nd Swing, Golf Avenue and the PGA Tour Superstore trade-in listings, and check the loft and lie against the chart above before buying — sets this age have often been bent from standard.
