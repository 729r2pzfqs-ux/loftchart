About the Wishon Sterling Single Length

The Sterling Irons were the design that carried single-length golf into the mainstream conversation alongside Bryson DeChambeau's rise: every club in the set is built to the same 8-iron-like length — 36.5 inches standard, with 36.75 or 37 as fitting options — on the same 63-degree lie and the same 274-gram head weight, so one swing repeats through the bag. Tom Wishon designed them with single-length advocate Jaacob Bowden and introduced them in early 2016, when the first run sold out immediately. Because short shafts cost long irons speed, the 4-iron through 7-iron use a high-COR construction — HS300 variable-thickness high-strength steel faces welded to 8620 carbon steel bodies — while the 8-iron through wedges are conventional one-piece 8620 castings; a 5-hybrid on the same length was offered in place of the 5-iron. Lofts run 19 to 60 degrees across a 4-iron-through-LW head range with wider gaps in the scoring clubs to preserve distance spacing. Sold through independent clubmakers, as all Wishon designs are, until the EQ1-NX — marketed as the Sterling's second generation — replaced it in 2021.

Frequently asked questions

What is the loft of a Sterling Single Length 7-iron?

The Sterling 7-iron has a loft of 31 degrees — but unlike a conventional set it is built to 36.5 inches on a 63-degree lie, the same length and lie as every other club in the set.

How long are Wishon Sterling single-length irons?

Every club is built to the same length — 36.5 inches standard, roughly an 8-iron's length, with 36.75 or 37 inches as fitting options — with identical 274-gram heads and 63-degree lies.

How do Sterling long irons keep their distance at 8-iron length?

The 4-iron through 7-iron use a high-COR construction: HS300 variable-thickness high-strength steel faces welded to 8620 carbon steel bodies, recovering the ball speed lost to the shorter shaft. The 8-iron through lob wedge are conventional one-piece castings.

Are Wishon Sterling irons still made?

No. They were discontinued when the Wishon EQ1-NX — marketed as the Sterling's second generation — arrived in 2021, though used sets remain common in the single-length community.

Finding a used set

The Wishon Sterling Single Length ran from 2016 to 2021 and is no longer in production, so the used market is the only source. Search eBay, 2nd Swing, Golf Avenue and the PGA Tour Superstore trade-in listings, and check the loft and lie against the chart above before buying — sets this age have often been bent from standard.
