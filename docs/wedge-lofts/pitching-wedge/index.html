What Degree Is a Pitching Wedge?

A pitching wedge is the strongest-lofted wedge in the bag and the club that finishes almost every iron set.

A pitching wedge is normally 43° to 48°. Across the 779 iron sets on file here that carry one, the most common PW loft is 46° and the published range runs 37°–51.5°. There is no industry standard, so the only figure that matters for your bag is the one on your own set’s chart.

How far does a pitching wedge go?

Carry distance, full swing, men’s amateur. These are ranges rather than a single number on purpose: swing speed and strike quality move wedge distance far more than a degree or two of loft does.

What a pitching wedge is used for

  • Full approach shots from roughly 100–140 yards for most amateurs.
  • Low, running chip shots from just off the green, where its strong loft keeps the ball down and lets it release.
  • Three-quarter and knock-down shots into wind, where a sand or lob wedge would balloon.

Why pitching wedge lofts keep getting stronger

The pitching wedge has lost roughly 4° of loft in a generation. Manufacturers strengthen iron lofts to advertise more distance, and because the wedge is the last club in the set it absorbs the whole shift. That is why a modern 7-iron can fly as far as a 1990s 5-iron, and why the club stamped “PW” in one bag is not the same club as the PW in another.

Two consequences follow. First, replacing an old set with a new one moves every yardage in the bag, so re-gapping is not optional. Second, a strong modern PW leaves a bigger hole underneath it — which is exactly why the gap wedge exists.

Frequently asked questions

What degree is a pitching wedge?

Most pitching wedges are 43° to 48°. Across the 779 sets on file at LoftChart the most common PW loft is 46°, and the full published range is 37 to 51.5 degrees.

How far does a pitching wedge go?

For an average male amateur, 95–125 yards of carry on a full swing. Slower swing speeds carry it 70–95 yards, and faster players 125–145 yards. Distance depends far more on swing speed and strike quality than on the loft stamped on the club.

Is a 46 degree wedge a pitching wedge?

Yes. 46° sits in the middle of the pitching wedge band and was the standard for most iron sets through the 2000s. Modern game-improvement and distance sets have moved to 43°-45°, while blades and players irons are still built at 46°-48°.

Why is my new pitching wedge stronger than my old one?

Because iron lofts have been strengthened steadily to advertise more distance, and the pitching wedge absorbs the whole shift as the last club in the set. Median PW loft across the sets on file has moved from 49° in 1980s sets to 44° in sets released since 2020.

Do I need a gap wedge if I have a pitching wedge?

If the difference between your pitching wedge and your sand wedge is more than about 6°, yes. A 44° PW and a 56° sand wedge leave a 12° hole, which is two clubs' worth of distance with nothing to cover it.
