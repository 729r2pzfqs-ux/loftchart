What Degree Is an Approach Wedge?

An approach wedge is the same club as a gap wedge — the name is simply what certain manufacturers stamp on the sole.

An approach wedge is normally 48° to 52°. Across the 244 iron sets on file here that carry one, the most common AW loft is 50° and the published range runs 42°–52°. There is no industry standard, so the only figure that matters for your bag is the one on your own set’s chart.

What an approach wedge is used for

  • Full approach shots from roughly 80–110 yards.
  • Pitch shots inside 60 yards where a sand wedge would come up short.
  • The bridge club between the pitching wedge and sand wedge in a modern set.

Approach wedge vs gap wedge

They are the same club. “Approach wedge” and “gap wedge” both describe the wedge that sits between the pitching wedge and the sand wedge, at 48°–52°. Only the stamping differs, and the stamping is a marketing decision — which is why the labels below all appear on sets in this archive at effectively the same loft.

Because the label carries no loft guarantee, never buy this club by its letter. A Callaway AW and a Ping UW from the same year can be 3° apart, and a set AW is built to match its own iron set rather than to any industry standard — see the gap wedge guide for the gapping maths.

Frequently asked questions

What degree is an approach wedge?

Most approach wedges are 48° to 52°. Across the 244 sets on file at LoftChart the most common AW loft is 50°, and the full published range is 42 to 52 degrees.

How far does an approach wedge go?

For an average male amateur, 80–110 yards of carry on a full swing. Slower swing speeds carry it 60–80 yards, and faster players 110–125 yards. Distance depends far more on swing speed and strike quality than on the loft stamped on the club.

What is the difference between an approach wedge and a gap wedge?

Nothing but the stamping. Both name the wedge between the pitching wedge and the sand wedge, at 48° to 52°. Callaway, TaylorMade and Srixon mark it AW; Cobra, Mizuno, PXG and Wilson mark it GW; Ping marks it UW.

What degree is an A wedge?

An A wedge, marked A or AW, is normally 48° to 52°. Set-matched approach wedges tend to sit at the strong end of that band because they are built to follow a strong modern pitching wedge.

How far should I hit my approach wedge?

About 80 to 110 yards for an average male amateur, roughly 10 to 15 yards shorter than a full pitching wedge. If the difference is much larger than that, the two clubs are gapped too far apart.
