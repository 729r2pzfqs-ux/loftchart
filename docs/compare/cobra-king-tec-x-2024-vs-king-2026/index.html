Lofts compared

What the differences mean

At the 7-iron, the KING TEC X (2024) is 27° and the KING (2026) is 27.5°, a 0.5° gap. Each degree of loft is worth roughly 3 to 4 yards of carry at the same swing speed, so the KING TEC X (2024) should carry about 2 yards further with that club, on a slightly lower, shallower flight than the KING (2026). Across the 8 clubs both sets share, the KING (2026) averages 0.4° weaker, with the largest gap at the 5-iron (21° vs 21.5°). The pitching wedges are 42° and 42.5°; the stronger one leaves a 8° jump to a 50° gap wedge, which matters when planning wedge gapping.

The sets are built differently: the KING TEC X (2024) runs 4-iron to GW (8 clubs), the KING (2026) 4-iron to SW (9 clubs).

The KING TEC X (2024) is a players distance iron and the KING (2026) a game improvement iron, so they sit one step apart on the forgiveness scale. The KING (2026) is the more forgiving design, with a wider sole, more offset and more perimeter weighting for roughly 10 to 25 handicaps. The KING TEC X (2024) trades some of that for a more compact head, thinner topline and more control over flight and shape, which suits roughly 5 to 15 handicaps who want extra distance from a compact head.

The KING TEC X (2024) is listed as hollow body with an internal tungsten weight and the KING (2026) as multi-piece distance iron with a 360 SPEEDSHELL face insert, a polymer-encased internal weight and a 3D-printed medallion. The KING TEC X (2024)'s hollow body lets the face flex more and moves mass low and outward, usually giving higher launch and more ball speed on off-centre strikes than the KING (2026), at some cost in feel. The KING TEC X (2024) adds tungsten weighting, which lowers the centre of gravity and raises stability on mis-hits, helping the long irons launch.

The KING (2026) came out 2 years after the KING TEC X (2024), but it is a different line rather than a direct replacement, so the two were designed for different golfers as well as different seasons.

Which should you choose?

The KING (2026) is the weaker-lofted set — up to 0.5° weaker than the KING TEC X (2024). That trades raw distance for a steeper landing angle and more predictable stopping power into greens.

Frequently asked questions

Is the Cobra KING TEC X (2024) stronger lofted than the KING (2026)?

Yes. The Cobra KING TEC X (2024) 7-iron is 27° against 27.5° for the KING (2026), 0.5° stronger, which is worth about 2 yards of extra carry with the same swing.

What is the loft difference between the Cobra KING TEC X (2024) and KING (2026)?

At the 7-iron the difference is 0.5° (27° vs 27.5°). The largest gap on a shared club is 0.5° at the 5-iron, and 6 of the 8 shared clubs differ.

Which is better for higher handicap golfers, the Cobra KING TEC X (2024) or the KING (2026)?

The KING (2026). As a game improvement iron it is the more forgiving design, suited to roughly 10 to 25 handicaps. The Cobra KING TEC X (2024) is a players distance iron, better suited to roughly 5 to 15 handicaps who want extra distance from a compact head.

Do the Cobra KING TEC X (2024) and KING (2026) have the same lie and length?

At the 7-iron, yes: both are 63.5° lie and 37.25 inches long.
