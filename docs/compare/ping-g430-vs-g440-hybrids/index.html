What the differences mean

Both families are sold in 1 head — Std for the G430, Std for the G440.

The G430 is sold from 17° to 34°, the G440 from 17° to 34°. Only the G430 has a stock 19° and 22° option. Only the G440 has a stock 20° and 23° option.

Adjustability is a wash: both use the same hosel system (Trajectory Tuning 2.0, ±1.5° of loft, 8 settings), so a given head reaches the same effective loft window in either generation.

The G440 arrived 2 years after the G430 as its replacement. Hybrid generations usually carry over the loft ladder and move the technology — face design, adjustability, head options — so the older family is the value buy on the used market while the newer one holds retail price.

Which should you choose?

The G440 is the newer generation, so it carries the current face technology and full retail availability; the G430 is the value buy used or on closeout. If the head and loft you want is only in one lineup, that decides it — otherwise the newer family is the default at equal prices, and the older one the smarter buy at a real discount.

Both families are charted in full on their own pages: Ping G430 hybrid specs and Ping G440 hybrid specs.

Frequently asked questions

What lofts do the Ping G430 and G440 hybrids come in?

The Ping G430 is sold in stock lofts from 17 to 34 degrees and the G440 from 17 to 34 degrees. They share 4 stock lofts.

Which has more head options, the Ping G430 or the G440?

Both families are sold in 1 head.

Are the Ping G430 and G440 hybrids adjustable?

Yes — the Ping G430 uses Trajectory Tuning 2.0, ±1.5° of loft, 8 settings, and the G440 uses Trajectory Tuning 2.0, ±1.5° of loft, 8 settings. Each model page carries the full setting-by-setting adjustment chart.
