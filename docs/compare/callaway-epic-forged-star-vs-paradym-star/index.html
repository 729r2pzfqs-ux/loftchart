Lofts compared

What the differences mean

At the 7-iron, the Epic Forged Star is 26° and the Paradym Star is 30°, a 4° gap. Each degree of loft is worth roughly 3 to 4 yards of carry at the same swing speed, so the Epic Forged Star should carry roughly 12 to 16 yards further with that club, on a slightly lower, shallower flight than the Paradym Star. Across the 8 clubs both sets share, the Paradym Star averages 4.5° weaker, with the largest gap at the 9-iron (33° vs 40°). The pitching wedges are 38° and 45°; the stronger one leaves a 12° jump to a 50° gap wedge, which matters when planning wedge gapping. Beyond loft, the Epic Forged Star 7-iron sits 1.5° flatter (60.5° vs 62°), which nudges start direction slightly right for a right-hander; the Epic Forged Star 7-iron is 0.38 inches longer (37.625" vs 37.25").

The sets are built differently: the Epic Forged Star runs 5-iron to SW (9 clubs), the Paradym Star 5-iron to SW (8 clubs).

Both are super game improvement irons, built for high handicaps, beginners and slower swing speeds. The most forgiving category — very wide soles, maximum perimeter weighting and the strongest lofts. Because they target the same golfer, the decision comes down to the specific loft, feel and shaping differences rather than who the sets are made for.

The Epic Forged Star is listed as forged hollow body with a cup face and the Paradym Star as hollow body with a forged 455 face cup. Both use hollow-body heads, so both trade a little feel for face flex and launch help. The Epic Forged Star adds tungsten weighting, which lowers the centre of gravity and raises stability on mis-hits, helping the long irons launch.

There are 4 years between these sets (2019 and 2023), more than one design generation. Over that time irons moved toward stronger lofts, thinner and faster faces, hollow and multi-material heads and wider use of tungsten, so the newer set is not simply a better version of the older one: it may launch and spin quite differently. Here the 7-iron loft moved from 26° to 30°.

Which should you choose?

The Paradym Star is the weaker-lofted set — up to 7° weaker than the Epic Forged Star. That trades raw distance for a steeper landing angle and more predictable stopping power into greens.

Frequently asked questions

Is the Callaway Epic Forged Star stronger lofted than the Paradym Star?

Yes. The Callaway Epic Forged Star 7-iron is 26° against 30° for the Paradym Star, 4° stronger, which is worth roughly 12 to 16 yards of extra carry with the same swing.

What is the loft difference between the Callaway Epic Forged Star and Paradym Star?

At the 7-iron the difference is 4° (26° vs 30°). The largest gap on a shared club is 7° at the 9-iron, and all 8 shared clubs differ.

Are the Callaway Epic Forged Star and Paradym Star aimed at the same golfer?

Yes. Both are super game improvement irons, built for high handicaps, beginners and slower swing speeds. Choose on feel, looks and gapping, ideally after hitting both.

Do the Callaway Epic Forged Star and Paradym Star have the same lie and length?

Not exactly. At the 7-iron the Callaway Epic Forged Star is 60.5° lie and 37.625 inches, the Paradym Star 62° and 37.25 inches. Both can be bent or built to your own fit.
