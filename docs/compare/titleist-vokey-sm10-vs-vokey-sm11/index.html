What the differences mean

Both models offer 27 loft/bounce/grind combinations, so neither wins on raw selection. Each is ground 6 ways — F, S, M, D, K, and T for the Vokey SM10, F, S, M, D, K, and T for the Vokey SM11.

The Vokey SM10 is sold from 46° to 62°, the Vokey SM11 from 44° to 60°. Only the Vokey SM10 comes in 62°. Only the Vokey SM11 comes in 44°.

The scoring lofts are where bounce and grind choice matters most: at 56° both offer 4 options; at 58° both offer 6 options; at 60° both offer 6 options. More options at a degree means more ways to match the sole to your turf and swing, which is what a wedge fitting actually selects.

The Vokey SM11 arrived 2 years after the Vokey SM10 as its replacement. Wedge generations usually carry over the core loft ladder and adjust the grind catalogue, groove spec and finishes, so the older model is the value buy while stock lasts — the trade-off is the newer grooves, which matter more the more you play.

Which should you choose?

The Vokey SM11 is the newer generation, so it carries the current groove spec and the fuller retail availability; the Vokey SM10 is the value buy while closeout and used stock lasts. The option chart above is the tiebreaker — if the exact loft, bounce and grind you play is only in one of them, that decides it.

Both option charts are reproduced in full on their own pages: Titleist Vokey SM10 specs and Titleist Vokey SM11 specs.
