Lofts compared

What the differences mean

At the 7-iron, the Pro 223 is 32° and the Pro 225 is 30°, a 2° gap. Each degree of loft is worth roughly 3 to 4 yards of carry at the same swing speed, so the Pro 225 should carry roughly 6 to 8 yards further with that club, on a slightly lower, shallower flight than the Pro 223. Across the 8 clubs both sets share, the Pro 225 averages 1.6° stronger, with the largest gap at the 7-iron (32° vs 30°). The pitching wedges are 46° and 44°; the stronger one leaves a 6° jump to a 50° gap wedge, which matters when planning wedge gapping.

The sets are built differently: the Pro 223 runs 4-iron to GW (8 clubs), the Pro 225 2-iron to GW (10 clubs).

The Pro 223 is a players iron and the Pro 225 a players distance iron, so they sit one step apart on the forgiveness scale. The Pro 225 is the more forgiving design, with a wider sole, more offset and more perimeter weighting for roughly 5 to 15 handicaps who want extra distance from a compact head. The Pro 223 trades some of that for a more compact head, thinner topline and more control over flight and shape, which suits roughly scratch to 10 handicaps.

The Pro 223 is listed as Grain Flow Forged HD, multi-material and the Pro 225 as Grain Flow Forged HD, hollow body multi-material. The Pro 225's hollow body lets the face flex more and moves mass low and outward, usually giving higher launch and more ball speed on off-centre strikes than the Pro 223, at some cost in feel.

Both sets were introduced in 2021, so this is a choice between two models from the same lineup rather than an upgrade: Mizuno designed them to sit side by side for different players.

Which should you choose?

The Pro 225 is the stronger-lofted set — up to 2° stronger than the Pro 223 on matching clubs. Expect longer carry per numbered iron but a flatter descent angle; gapping at the wedge end is where that shows up.

Frequently asked questions

Is the Mizuno Pro 223 stronger lofted than the Pro 225?

No. The Pro 225 is the stronger-lofted set: its 7-iron is 30° against 32° for the Mizuno Pro 223, 2° stronger and worth roughly 6 to 8 yards of extra carry.

What is the loft difference between the Mizuno Pro 223 and Pro 225?

At the 7-iron the difference is 2° (32° vs 30°). The largest gap on a shared club is 2° at the 7-iron, and all 8 shared clubs differ.

Which is better for higher handicap golfers, the Mizuno Pro 223 or the Pro 225?

The Pro 225. As a players distance iron it is the more forgiving design, suited to roughly 5 to 15 handicaps who want extra distance from a compact head. The Mizuno Pro 223 is a players iron, better suited to roughly scratch to 10 handicaps.

Do the Mizuno Pro 223 and Pro 225 have the same lie and length?

At the 7-iron, yes: both are 61.5° lie and 37 inches long.
