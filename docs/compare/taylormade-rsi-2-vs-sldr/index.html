Lofts compared

What the differences mean

At the 7-iron, the RSi 2 is 31° and the SLDR is 32°, a 1° gap. Each degree of loft is worth roughly 3 to 4 yards of carry at the same swing speed, so the RSi 2 should carry roughly 3 to 4 yards further with that club, on a slightly lower, shallower flight than the SLDR. Across the 10 clubs both sets share, the SLDR averages 0.7° weaker, with the largest gap at the 6-iron (27° vs 28°). The pitching wedges are 45° and 46°; the stronger one leaves a 5° jump to a 50° gap wedge, which matters when planning wedge gapping.

The sets are built differently: the RSi 2 runs 2-iron to SW (11 clubs), the SLDR 3-iron to SW (10 clubs).

Both are players distance irons, built for roughly 5 to 15 handicaps who want extra distance from a compact head. Forged or multi-material heads that keep a compact look while adding face technology and stronger lofts for distance. Because they target the same golfer, the decision comes down to the specific loft, feel and shaping differences rather than who the sets are made for.

The RSi 2 is listed as cast/forged hybrid and the SLDR as cast with a ThruSlot Speed Pocket. The RSi 2 adds tungsten weighting, which lowers the centre of gravity and raises stability on mis-hits, helping the long irons launch.

Both sets were introduced in 2014, so this is a choice between two models from the same lineup rather than an upgrade: TaylorMade designed them to sit side by side for different players.

Which should you choose?

The SLDR is the weaker-lofted set — up to 1° weaker than the RSi 2. That trades raw distance for a steeper landing angle and more predictable stopping power into greens.

Frequently asked questions

Is the TaylorMade RSi 2 stronger lofted than the SLDR?

Yes. The TaylorMade RSi 2 7-iron is 31° against 32° for the SLDR, 1° stronger, which is worth roughly 3 to 4 yards of extra carry with the same swing.

What is the loft difference between the TaylorMade RSi 2 and SLDR?

At the 7-iron the difference is 1° (31° vs 32°). The largest gap on a shared club is 1° at the 6-iron, and 7 of the 10 shared clubs differ.

Are the TaylorMade RSi 2 and SLDR aimed at the same golfer?

Yes. Both are players distance irons, built for roughly 5 to 15 handicaps who want extra distance from a compact head. Choose on feel, looks and gapping, ideally after hitting both.

Do the TaylorMade RSi 2 and SLDR have the same lie and length?

At the 7-iron, yes: both are 62.5° lie and 36.75 inches long.
