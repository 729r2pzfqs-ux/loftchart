What the differences mean

The ZX Mk II family is the broader line: 3 heads against 2 — ZX5 and ZX7 for the ZX, ZX5 Mk II, ZX5 LS Mk II, and ZX7 Mk II for the ZX Mk II.

The ZX is sold from 9.5° to 10.5°, the ZX Mk II from 8.5° to 10.5°. Only the ZX Mk II has a stock 8.5° option.

Adjustability is a wash: both use the same hosel system (Quick Tune System (QTS) sleeve, ±1° of loft, 12 settings), so a given head reaches the same effective loft window in either generation.

The ZX Mk II arrived 2 years after the ZX as its replacement. Driver generations usually carry over the loft ladder and move the technology — face design, adjustability, head options — so the older family is the value buy on the used market while the newer one holds retail price.

Which should you choose?

The ZX Mk II is the newer generation, so it carries the current face technology and full retail availability; the ZX is the value buy used or on closeout. If the head and loft you want is only in one lineup, that decides it — otherwise the newer family is the default at equal prices, and the older one the smarter buy at a real discount.

Frequently asked questions

What lofts do the Srixon ZX and ZX Mk II drivers come in?

The Srixon ZX is sold in stock lofts from 9.5 to 10.5 degrees and the ZX Mk II from 8.5 to 10.5 degrees. They share 2 stock lofts.

Which has more head options, the Srixon ZX or the ZX Mk II?

The ZX Mk II: 3 head variants against 2.

Are the Srixon ZX and ZX Mk II drivers adjustable?

Yes — the Srixon ZX uses Quick Tune System (QTS) sleeve, ±1° of loft, 12 settings, and the ZX Mk II uses Quick Tune System (QTS) sleeve, ±1° of loft, 12 settings. Each model page carries the full setting-by-setting adjustment chart.
