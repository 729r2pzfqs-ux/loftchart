What the differences mean

At the 7-iron, the KING TEC (2024) is 29.5° and the KING TEC X (2024) is 27°, a 2.5° gap. Each degree of loft is worth roughly 3 to 4 yards of carry at the same swing speed, so the KING TEC X (2024) should carry roughly 8 to 10 yards further with that club, on a slightly lower, shallower flight than the KING TEC (2024). Across the 8 clubs both sets share, the KING TEC X (2024) averages 2.1° stronger, with the largest gap at the 7-iron (29.5° vs 27°). The pitching wedges are 44° and 42°; the stronger one leaves a 8° jump to a 50° gap wedge, which matters when planning wedge gapping. Beyond loft, the KING TEC (2024) 7-iron sits 1° flatter (62.5° vs 63.5°), which nudges start direction slightly right for a right-hander.

The sets are built differently: the KING TEC (2024) runs 3-iron to GW (9 clubs), the KING TEC X (2024) 4-iron to GW (8 clubs).

Both are players distance irons, built for roughly 5 to 15 handicaps who want extra distance from a compact head. Forged or multi-material heads that keep a compact look while adding face technology and stronger lofts for distance. Because they target the same golfer, the decision comes down to the specific loft, feel and shaping differences rather than who the sets are made for.

The KING TEC (2024) is listed as forged body and face and the KING TEC X (2024) as hollow body with an internal tungsten weight. The KING TEC X (2024)'s hollow body lets the face flex more and moves mass low and outward, usually giving higher launch and more ball speed on off-centre strikes than the KING TEC (2024), at some cost in feel. The KING TEC X (2024) adds tungsten weighting, which lowers the centre of gravity and raises stability on mis-hits, helping the long irons launch.

Both sets were introduced in 2024, so this is a choice between two models from the same lineup rather than an upgrade: Cobra designed them to sit side by side for different players.

Which should you choose?

The KING TEC X (2024) is the stronger-lofted set — up to 2.5° stronger than the KING TEC (2024) on matching clubs. Expect longer carry per numbered iron but a flatter descent angle; gapping at the wedge end is where that shows up.

Frequently asked questions

Is the Cobra KING TEC (2024) stronger lofted than the KING TEC X (2024)?

No. The KING TEC X (2024) is the stronger-lofted set: its 7-iron is 27° against 29.5° for the Cobra KING TEC (2024), 2.5° stronger and worth roughly 8 to 10 yards of extra carry.

What is the loft difference between the Cobra KING TEC (2024) and KING TEC X (2024)?

At the 7-iron the difference is 2.5° (29.5° vs 27°). The largest gap on a shared club is 2.5° at the 7-iron, and all 8 shared clubs differ.

Are the Cobra KING TEC (2024) and KING TEC X (2024) aimed at the same golfer?

Yes. Both are players distance irons, built for roughly 5 to 15 handicaps who want extra distance from a compact head. Choose on feel, looks and gapping, ideally after hitting both.

Do the Cobra KING TEC (2024) and KING TEC X (2024) have the same lie and length?

Not exactly. At the 7-iron the Cobra KING TEC (2024) is 62.5° lie and 37.25 inches, the KING TEC X (2024) 63.5° and 37.25 inches. Both can be bent or built to your own fit.
