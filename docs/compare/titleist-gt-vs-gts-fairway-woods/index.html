What the differences mean

The GT family is the broader line: 3 heads against 2 — GT1, GT2, and GT3 for the GT, GTS2 and GTS3 for the GTS.

The GT is sold from 13.5° to 24°, the GTS from 13.5° to 21°. Only the GT has a stock 24° option.

Adjustability is a wash: both use the same hosel system (SureFit hosel, −0.75° to +1.5° of loft, 16 settings), so a given head reaches the same effective loft window in either generation.

The GTS arrived 2 years after the GT as its replacement. Fairway wood generations usually carry over the loft ladder and move the technology — face design, adjustability, head options — so the older family is the value buy on the used market while the newer one holds retail price.

Which should you choose?

The GTS is the newer generation, so it carries the current face technology and full retail availability; the GT is the value buy used or on closeout. If the head and loft you want is only in one lineup, that decides it — otherwise the newer family is the default at equal prices, and the older one the smarter buy at a real discount.

Both families are charted in full on their own pages: Titleist GT fairway wood specs and Titleist GTS fairway wood specs.

Frequently asked questions

What lofts do the Titleist GT and GTS fairway woods come in?

The Titleist GT is sold in stock lofts from 13.5 to 24 degrees and the GTS from 13.5 to 21 degrees. They share 5 stock lofts.

Which has more head options, the Titleist GT or the GTS?

The Titleist GT: 3 head variants against 2.

Are the Titleist GT and GTS fairway woods adjustable?

Yes — the Titleist GT uses SureFit hosel, −0.75° to +1.5° of loft, 16 settings, and the GTS uses SureFit hosel, −0.75° to +1.5° of loft, 16 settings. Each model page carries the full setting-by-setting adjustment chart.
