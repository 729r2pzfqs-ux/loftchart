What the differences mean

Both families are sold in 4 heads — TSR1, TSR2, TSR3, and TSR4 for the TSR, GT1, GT2, GT3, and GT4 for the GT.

Stock loft coverage is identical: both run 8° to 12° across the same 5 stock lofts.

Adjustability is a wash: both use the same hosel system (SureFit hosel, −0.75° to +1.5° of loft, 16 settings), so a given head reaches the same effective loft window in either generation.

The GT arrived 2 years after the TSR as its replacement. Driver generations usually carry over the loft ladder and move the technology — face design, adjustability, head options — so the older family is the value buy on the used market while the newer one holds retail price.

Which should you choose?

The GT is the newer generation, so it carries the current face technology and full retail availability; the TSR is the value buy used or on closeout. If the head and loft you want is only in one lineup, that decides it — otherwise the newer family is the default at equal prices, and the older one the smarter buy at a real discount.

Both families are charted in full on their own pages: Titleist TSR driver specs and Titleist GT driver specs.

Frequently asked questions

What lofts do the Titleist TSR and GT drivers come in?

The Titleist TSR is sold in stock lofts from 8 to 12 degrees and the GT from 8 to 12 degrees. They share 5 stock lofts.

Which has more head options, the Titleist TSR or the GT?

Both families are sold in 4 heads.

Are the Titleist TSR and GT drivers adjustable?

Yes — the Titleist TSR uses SureFit hosel, −0.75° to +1.5° of loft, 16 settings, and the GT uses SureFit hosel, −0.75° to +1.5° of loft, 16 settings. Each model page carries the full setting-by-setting adjustment chart.
