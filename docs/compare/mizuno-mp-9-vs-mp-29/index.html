Lofts compared

What the differences mean

At the 7-iron, the MP-9 is 35° and the MP-29 is 35°, identical at the club most golfers compare on, although other clubs in the sets differ. Across the 11 clubs both sets share, the differences roughly cancel out, with the largest gap at the 1-iron (17° vs 15°). The pitching wedges are 47° and 48°; the stronger one leaves a 3° jump to a 50° gap wedge, which matters when planning wedge gapping. Beyond loft, the MP-9 7-iron sits 0.5° flatter (60° vs 60.5°), which nudges start direction slightly right for a right-hander; the MP-29 7-iron is 0.25 inches longer (36.5" vs 36.75").

The sets are built differently: the MP-9 runs 1-iron to SW (12 clubs), the MP-29 1-iron to SW (11 clubs).

Both are blade irons, built for low single-digit handicaps who strike the ball consistently. Compact muscleback irons with minimal offset, thin toplines and the smallest sweet spot — built for shot-shaping over forgiveness. Because they target the same golfer, the decision comes down to the specific loft, feel and shaping differences rather than who the sets are made for.

Both heads are listed as forged. With the build method this similar, differences in feel come down to head size, sole width, topline and offset rather than how the heads are made.

There are 4 years between these sets (1988 and 1992), more than one design generation. Over that time irons moved toward stronger lofts, thinner and faster faces, hollow and multi-material heads and wider use of tungsten, so the newer set is not simply a better version of the older one: it may launch and spin quite differently.

Which should you choose?

Loft differences between the MP-9 and MP-29 run in both directions rather than shifting the whole set one way, so the choice comes down to shaping, feel and gapping at the ends of the set rather than outright distance.

Frequently asked questions

Is the Mizuno MP-9 stronger lofted than the MP-29?

Not at the 7-iron, where both are 35°. Other clubs in the sets differ, so compare the full table above.

What is the loft difference between the Mizuno MP-9 and MP-29?

At the 7-iron the difference is 0° (35° vs 35°). The largest gap on a shared club is 2° at the 1-iron, and 4 of the 11 shared clubs differ.

Are the Mizuno MP-9 and MP-29 aimed at the same golfer?

Yes. Both are blade irons, built for low single-digit handicaps who strike the ball consistently. Choose on feel, looks and gapping, ideally after hitting both.

Do the Mizuno MP-9 and MP-29 have the same lie and length?

Not exactly. At the 7-iron the Mizuno MP-9 is 60° lie and 36.5 inches, the MP-29 60.5° and 36.75 inches. Both can be bent or built to your own fit.
