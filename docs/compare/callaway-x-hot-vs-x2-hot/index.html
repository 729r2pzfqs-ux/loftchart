What the differences mean

At the 7-iron, the X Hot is 30° and the X2 Hot is 30°, identical at the club most golfers compare on, although other clubs in the sets differ. Across the 11 clubs both sets share, the differences roughly cancel out, with the largest gap at the 4-iron (20° vs 20.5°).

Both are game improvement irons, built for roughly 10 to 25 handicaps. Perimeter-weighted cavity backs with wider soles, more offset and stronger lofts, aimed at mid handicaps. Because they target the same golfer, the decision comes down to the specific loft, feel and shaping differences rather than who the sets are made for.

Both heads are listed as investment cast. With the build method this similar, differences in feel come down to head size, sole width, topline and offset rather than how the heads are made.

The X2 Hot arrived 1 year after the X Hot, a typical one-generation refresh. Changes over that span are usually incremental: refined face thickness, revised weighting, updated looks and small loft tweaks rather than a new concept.

Which should you choose?

The X2 Hot is the weaker-lofted set — up to 0.5° weaker than the X Hot. That trades raw distance for a steeper landing angle and more predictable stopping power into greens.

Frequently asked questions

Is the Callaway X Hot stronger lofted than the X2 Hot?

Not at the 7-iron, where both are 30°. Other clubs in the sets differ, so compare the full table above.

What is the loft difference between the Callaway X Hot and X2 Hot?

At the 7-iron the difference is 0° (30° vs 30°). The largest gap on a shared club is 0.5° at the 4-iron, and 1 of the 11 shared clubs differ.

Are the Callaway X Hot and X2 Hot aimed at the same golfer?

Yes. Both are game improvement irons, built for roughly 10 to 25 handicaps. Choose on feel, looks and gapping, ideally after hitting both.

Do the Callaway X Hot and X2 Hot have the same lie and length?

At the 7-iron, yes: both are 62.5° lie and 37 inches long.
