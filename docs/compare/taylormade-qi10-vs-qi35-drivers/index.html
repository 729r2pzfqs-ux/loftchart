What the differences mean

The Qi35 family is the broader line: 4 heads against 3 — Std, LS, and Max for the Qi10, Std, LS, Max, and Max Lite for the Qi35.

Stock loft coverage is identical: both run 8° to 12° across the same 4 stock lofts.

Adjustability is a wash: both use the same hosel system (4° Loft Sleeve, ±2° of loft, 12 settings), so a given head reaches the same effective loft window in either generation.

The Qi35 arrived 1 year after the Qi10 as its replacement. Driver generations usually carry over the loft ladder and move the technology — face design, adjustability, head options — so the older family is the value buy on the used market while the newer one holds retail price.

Which should you choose?

The Qi35 is the newer generation, so it carries the current face technology and full retail availability; the Qi10 is the value buy used or on closeout. If the head and loft you want is only in one lineup, that decides it — otherwise the newer family is the default at equal prices, and the older one the smarter buy at a real discount.

Frequently asked questions

What lofts do the TaylorMade Qi10 and Qi35 drivers come in?

The TaylorMade Qi10 is sold in stock lofts from 8 to 12 degrees and the Qi35 from 8 to 12 degrees. They share 4 stock lofts.

Which has more head options, the TaylorMade Qi10 or the Qi35?

The Qi35: 4 head variants against 3.

Are the TaylorMade Qi10 and Qi35 drivers adjustable?

Yes — the TaylorMade Qi10 uses 4° Loft Sleeve, ±2° of loft, 12 settings, and the Qi35 uses 4° Loft Sleeve, ±2° of loft, 12 settings. Each model page carries the full setting-by-setting adjustment chart.
