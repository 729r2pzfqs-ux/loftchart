About the New Level 623-MB

The 623-MB is the compact muscle-back at the top of New Level's original 623 family, aimed at elite amateurs and professionals rather than the wider-market 623-M. New Level teased the 623 line in late October 2021 with a January 2022 retail date — the same announcement that introduced the 623-CB — and pitched the MB as a true player's iron: a traditional muscle-back shape with a thin topline, narrow sole and minimal offset for precise turf interaction and full workability, built from forged 1025 carbon steel for a soft, forged feel. New Level's retail spec chart lists the 623-MB on the same loft, lie and bounce ladder as the 623-CB — a 34-degree 7-iron and 46-degree pitching wedge across the stock 4-PW run — so the MB and CB share one set of numbers and are differentiated by head shape rather than loft, while the 623-M runs one degree weaker throughout. Sold right- and left-handed, built to order from New Level's shaft menu, so no stock shaft is recorded. It left the range when the 702 series, including a direct 702-MB successor, arrived in 2025.

Frequently asked questions

What is the loft of a New Level 623-MB 7-iron?

The New Level 623-MB 7-iron has a loft of 34 degrees, a lie of 62 degrees and a length of 37 inches — the same numbers as the 623-CB.

How does the 623-MB differ from the 623-CB and 623-M?

The MB and CB share an identical loft, lie, offset and bounce ladder and are differentiated by head shape: the MB is a compact muscle-back for elite ball-strikers, the CB a cavity-back with a little more forgiveness. The 623-M sits one degree weaker throughout and is New Level's own description of a 'nearly a blade' muscle iron for a broader market.

When did the New Level 623-MB come out?

New Level announced the 623 family in late October 2021, with the 623-MB and 623-CB reaching retail together in January 2022. It was discontinued when the 702 series, including a 702-MB, arrived in 2025.

Finding a used set

The New Level 623-MB ran from 2021 to 2025 and is no longer in production, so the used market is the only source. Search eBay, 2nd Swing, Golf Avenue and the PGA Tour Superstore trade-in listings, and check the loft and lie against the chart above before buying — sets this age have often been bent from standard.
