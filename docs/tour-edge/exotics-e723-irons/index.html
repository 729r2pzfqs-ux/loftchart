About the Tour Edge Exotics E723

The Exotics E723 was the Extreme-spec game-improvement iron in Tour Edge's Exotics 723 Series, announced 7 February 2023 with pre-sale from 8 February and retail availability from 1 March 2023. It uses a one-piece high-strength steel body with a deep 360-degree undercut pocket injected with VIBRCOR TPU, a multi-material electroform cap incorporating carbon fiber, extreme toe weighting for added MOI, and a Diamond Face VFT face with 99 diamond-shaped variable-thickness zones. Its lofts are far stronger than the companion C723's — 27.5 degrees at the 7-iron and 42 at the pitching wedge, against the C723's 30 and 44 — and the line runs from a 19-degree 4-iron through 46-degree AW, 51-degree GW and 56-degree SW scoring clubs. Pricing was $99.99 per club in steel ($699.99 for a 7-piece set) and $114.99 in graphite ($799.99). It was replaced by the Exotics E725 when the 725 iron family was announced on 3 October 2024. Tour Edge does not publish offset, bounce or swing weight for this model, so those keys are omitted.

Frequently asked questions

What is the loft of a Tour Edge Exotics E723 7-iron?

The Exotics E723 7-iron has a loft of 27.5 degrees, with a 62.5-degree lie and a 37.25-inch standard steel length — 2.5 degrees stronger than the companion Exotics C723.

What is the difference between the Tour Edge Exotics E723 and C723?

The E723 is the larger Extreme-spec game-improvement model with a one-piece undercut body, carbon fiber cap and much stronger lofts (27.5-degree 7-iron). The C723 is the compact hollow-body players-distance model with more traditional lofts (30-degree 7-iron).

What clubs were available in the Tour Edge Exotics E723 irons?

The E723 ran from a 19-degree 4-iron through the 42-degree PW, plus 46-degree AW, 51-degree GW and 56-degree SW scoring clubs, in right- and left-handed options.

Finding a used set

The Tour Edge Exotics E723 ran from 2023 to 2024 and is no longer in production, so the used market is the only source. Search eBay, 2nd Swing, Golf Avenue and the PGA Tour Superstore trade-in listings, and check the loft and lie against the chart above before buying — sets this age have often been bent from standard.
